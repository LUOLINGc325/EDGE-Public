Sharp constellation size for stretched-exponential accuracy on the amplitude-constrained Gaussian channel

Abstract

For the scalar Gaussian channel with unit noise variance and input constraint |X|≤A|X|\le A, let Kϵ(A)K_\epsilon(A) be the smallest support size of an input whose mutual information falls within ϵ\epsilon of capacity. For every fixed 0<τ<2/30<\tau<2/3, we prove Kϵ(A)∼Aπlog(1/ϵ) K_\epsilon(A)\sim \frac{A}{\pi}\sqrt{\log(1/\epsilon)} in the joint limit A→∞A\to\infty, ϵ→0\epsilon\to0, log(1/ϵ)=O(Aτ)\log(1/\epsilon)=O(A^\tau). The minimum discrete input entropy, allowing countably many atoms, equals the logarithm of this expression up to o(1)o(1). For the exact optimizer this gives a support growth exponent of at least 4/34/3, the exponent predicted by a continuum calculation of Abbott and Machta. The proof quantitatively localizes the normalized optimal output near the two amplitude boundaries. A polynomial approximation of a Gaussian convolution on intervals of length O(m)O(\sqrt m) controls derivatives of order up to mm without a degree-dependent prefactor. This estimate permits a cutoff comparison on many adjacent windows, yielding a stretched-exponential localization bound for every exponent below 2/32/3.

Appendices A and B treat the wrapped Gaussian channel. They give a direct large-effective-noise proof and derive the all-noise comparison from established theorems of periodic quadrature and spherical energy.

Reading guide

For a Gaussian channel with a bounded input amplitude, a constellation is a finite set of input points together with their probabilities. A target gap from capacity sets how accurately this finite input must reproduce the optimal output law. The main theorem counts the fewest points needed when the amplitude grows and the target gap shrinks in the stated joint regime.

The proof separates the interior of the amplitude interval from the two edges. It first bounds the interior deviations of the optimal output density. It then uses those bounds to obtain a necessary number of points and constructs a positive constellation of the same leading size. The appendices describe the separate circle model and its relation to known periodic quadrature.

7. Relation to earlier bounds

Smith proved finite support for the exact optimizer. Dytso, Yagli, Poor and Shamai proved an O(A2)O(A^2) upper bound on its cardinality. Wang, Barletta and Dytso, Theorem 1, obtained a lower bound of order AlogAA\sqrt{\log A} by estimating the relative entropy of the uniform-input output with respect to the optimal output on the real line and then using a wrapped-channel comparison. Bounding the loss of the uniform input by O(A−1)O(A^{-1}) alone limits such a converse to logarithmic growth in the inverse accuracy. Theorem 3 keeps the boundary input, which provides the precision needed for the power-law lower bound (3).

Barletta and Dytso, Theorems 2–4 in their August 2026 author version, study the same Kϵ(A)K_\epsilon(A). Their polynomial-accuracy bounds establish the AlogAA\sqrt{\log A} order, and their upper bound gives O(A3/2)O(A^{3/2}) at ϵ=e−A\epsilon=e^{-A}. The upper and lower constants in their polynomial bounds differ, and their exponential lower bound is still on the AlogAA\sqrt{\log A} scale. Ma, Wu and Yang, Theorem 1 and Sections 3–4, establish the approximation order for the worst compactly supported Gaussian location mixture, using local moment matching and trigonometric moment matrices. These are the antecedents of the corresponding approximation ingredients above. Here the target is an entropy optimizer, and quantitative localization is what makes a nearly full uniform component available at much smaller loss. It also confines the general moment-matching approximation to a negligible boundary fraction, allowing the sharp constant from the Gaussian Fourier multiplier to survive in the real channel.

Abbott and Machta, equations (13)–(14), use a local-density ansatz in a continuum expansion to predict KK of order A4/3A^{4/3} for the exact optimizer. Corollary 2 rigorously reaches the predicted exponent from below without a local-spacing hypothesis.
