An Axiomatic Diversity Measure from Threshold Semidefinite Programs

Abstract

We construct a diversity functional on arbitrary symmetric nonnegative dissimilarity matrices whose zero entries represent genuine duplicates. It satisfies strict coordinatewise monotonicity, strict preference for replacing a duplicate by a new object, and continuity, without a triangle inequality or a Euclidean embedding assumption. The construction integrates a weighted semidefinite optimum over distance thresholds. Its central property is exact invariance under merging duplicate coordinates: aggregation preserves the moment matrix constraint and the objective. At small positive thresholds the optimum becomes the sum of distances between distinct objects, which gives strictness even when a coordinatewise increase splits duplicate classes. We prove explicit strictness margins, a Lipschitz estimate, scale covariance, and diameter bounds. The square root of the functional is homogeneous of degree one and globally Lipschitz. Evaluation reduces to at most (n2)\binom n2 semidefinite programs with an explicit rational interior ball. Consequently, rational inputs admit deterministic relative approximation in time polynomial in the input length and the logarithm of the inverse accuracy. This is an approximation result for an exact axiomatic functional; exact symbolic evaluation is not asserted.

1. Introduction and scope

Mironov and Prokhorenkova [MP25] formulated three requirements for diversity on dissimilarity matrices: strict increase when distances increase, strict preference for a new object over a duplicate, and continuity. Their question asks whether all three can coexist with feasible computation. We use their full input domain, including duplicated objects and dissimilarities that need not satisfy a triangle inequality.

Threshold integration already appears in [MP25], and semidefinite lifting and numerical optimization are classical [LS91, GW95, VB96]. The contribution here is a particular entrywise-nonnegative, pair-weighted semidefinite functional whose feasible points admit exact duplicate aggregation. Together with a positive-length interval on which the quotient threshold graph is complete, that identity delivers all three strict axioms and a quantitative deterministic evaluation guarantee on the full domain. The argument does not require solving a maximum clique problem.

Ban, Roh, Shun, and Lakshman [BRSL26] recently introduced Geometric Kernel Diversity for Euclidean-realizable inputs. Their abstract states exact polynomial-time algorithms in dimensions at most three and randomized relative approximation in arbitrary dimension. Our domain is the unrestricted dissimilarity domain, and our approximation guarantee is deterministic in the rational bit model. The methods differ: their construction uses geometric unions, whereas ours uses threshold semidefinite programs. We make no claim about practical speed relative to their geometric algorithms.

The exact functional and its numerical approximation must be distinguished. Every axiom proved here concerns the exact real-valued function. An approximate numerical output need not preserve comparisons separated by arbitrarily small margins. Section 3 gives margins with which comparisons can instead be certified at a chosen precision. Section 6 gives a rational input with an irrational exact value.

2. Domain and construction

We use the following precise forms of the three axioms.

  1. If A,B∈𝒟nA,B\in\mathcal D_n, A≥BA\geq B entrywise, and A≠BA\ne B, the diversity of AA is strictly larger.
  2. Fix n−1n-1 indexed objects. Appending an object distinct from all of them has strictly greater diversity than appending a duplicate of any one of them.
  3. The diversity is continuous on 𝒟n\mathcal D_n in the relative topology inherited from real matrices.

The second axiom permits the distances from the new object to be smaller than the corresponding distances from the duplicate it replaces. The remaining n−1n-1 objects may themselves contain duplicates.

7. Interpretation and limitations

The duplicate contraction lemma explains why this relaxation is suitable for the axioms. The condition Xii=xiX_{ii}=x_i, together with vanishing within-class off-diagonal entries, makes the diagonal identity survive aggregation. Positive semidefiniteness survives the same aggregation by congruence. Entrywise nonnegativity preserves coordinatewise comparisons, and the rank-one all-ones point fixes the optimum on the complete quotient graph. Integrating over thresholds preserves weak comparisons and assigns positive measure to an interval on which they are strict.

The guarantees concern a mathematical measure on exact dissimilarities. They neither identify a uniquely appropriate measure for a particular application nor establish that satisfying these three axioms predicts an application’s empirical notion of diversity. The score is invariant under duplicate multiplicities, so it intentionally measures the represented distinct objects rather than their sampling frequencies. It is not normalized to a fixed range across different numbers of distinct objects.

The evaluation guarantee is polynomial bit complexity for approximation. It does not give a low-degree implementation bound, establish performance on large datasets, or furnish a finite rational representation of every exact optimum. Proposition 6.2 makes the latter distinction explicit. The deterministic approximation theorem gives a positive answer to the computational approximation version of the axiomatic existence question on the full dissimilarity domain.

Appendix B. Exact rational separation

Let yy be a rational query point in the free coordinates of Lemma 5.2. If an allowed coordinate zijz_{ij} is negative, the inequality zij≥0z_{ij}\geq0 separates yy from the feasible body. Otherwise form the rational symmetric matrix M(y)M(y).

The following recursive elimination either establishes positive semidefiniteness or constructs a rational vector ww with w⊤M(y)w<0w^\top M(y)w < 0. A negative diagonal entry gives a coordinate-vector witness. If every diagonal entry is zero, a nonzero off-diagonal entry hijh_{ij} gives the witness ei−sign(hij)eje_i-\operatorname{sign}(h_{ij})e_j; an all-zero matrix is positive semidefinite. In the remaining case, choose a positive diagonal pivot and write the current matrix as H=(ar⊤rB),a>0. H=\begin{pmatrix}a&r^\top\\r&B\end{pmatrix},\qquad a>0. Apply the same procedure to the rational Schur complement B−rr⊤/aB-rr^\top/a. A negative witness uu for that complement lifts to (−r⊤u/au), \begin{pmatrix}-r^\top u/a\\u\end{pmatrix}, whose quadratic form under HH equals the negative quadratic form under the complement. If the complement is positive semidefinite, the Schur complement criterion shows that HH is positive semidefinite. Thus zero pivots are not mistaken for a positive-semidefiniteness certificate.

This computation can be performed with polynomial bit complexity. After clearing denominators, fraction-free elimination, or equivalent determinant-ratio formulas, expresses the needed Schur complement entries through minors of a polynomial-size integer matrix. Hadamard’s determinant bound makes their encoding lengths polynomial in the original query length. There are at most n+1n+1 pivots. Back substitution in the associated triangular factors constructs the lifted witness by rational operations with the same polynomial encoding bound. Integer-preserving Gaussian elimination is classical; see [B68].

For the resulting witness w=(w0,w1,…,wn)w=(w_0,w_1,\ldots,w_n), the inequality w02+∑i(2w0wi+wi2)xi+2∑{i,j}∈Ewiwjzij≥0 w_0^2+\sum_i(2w_0w_i+w_i^2)x_i +2\sum_{\{i,j\}\in E}w_iw_jz_{ij}\geq0 \tag{B.1} holds throughout the feasible body and is violated at yy. Its linear coefficient vector is nonzero, since the feasible body is nonempty and the constant term w02w_0^2 is nonnegative. Dividing by the infinity norm of this rational coefficient vector supplies a rational normalized separator. An exact feasible query is, in particular, within every prescribed positive weak-feasibility tolerance. Therefore this strong separation procedure implements the weak separation oracle used by [GLS88].
