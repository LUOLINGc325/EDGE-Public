One-bit guessing at growing moment orders

Optimal high-moment powers, a half-erasure solution, and finite-block phase transitions

Research manuscript, 26 November 2026. The paper gives converses for all one-bit helpers, an exact solution at half erasure, and finite-block comparisons of two specified helpers. Proof-assistant verification and external peer review have not been completed.

Abstract

A helper sees independent erasures of a uniform binary word and sends one bit before the word is guessed in posterior order. We connect arbitrary helper-dependent guess orders to small-set probabilities of two source copies with a common observation. Classical reverse hypercontractivity then yields a converse for every helper: if the erasure probability is ε\varepsilon, the normalized positive rank moment is bounded below, uniformly in block length nn and moment order ss, by a constant times εn+(s+1)−max{1,(1−ε)/ε}\varepsilon^n+(s+1)^{-\max\{1,(1-\varepsilon)/\varepsilon\}}. For 0<ε≤1/20\lt\varepsilon\le1/2, the optimized infinite-block cost has the exact high-moment power (1−ε)/ε(1-\varepsilon)/\varepsilon; observed majority attains this power, with its optimal logarithmic correction left open below half erasure. At ε=1/2\varepsilon=1/2, the common-observation copies can be independent, and the greedy first-observed-bit helper is globally optimal for every nondecreasing rank loss at every finite block length. Its rank has the law of the minimum of two independent uniform ranks. Away from this exact point, greedy and observed majority have distinct joint blocklength–moment behavior. We prove a uniform greedy cost of order εn+(s+1)−log2(1/ε)\varepsilon^n+(s+1)^{-\log_2(1/\varepsilon)}, exact logarithmic costs when logs/n→θ∈(0,log2)\log s/n\to\theta\in(0,\log2), and a majority-to-greedy ratio tending to 1+((1−ε)/ε)2e−c1+((1-\varepsilon)/\varepsilon)^2e^{-c} when s/2n−logn→cs/2^n-\log n\to c. We also determine the majority high-moment constant, the low-erasure greedy dyadic-periodic coefficient, and an exact finite-prefix counterexample. No global optimality of majority or exact optimized cost away from half erasure is asserted.

1.2 What is and is not new

Weinberger and Shayevitz [WS20] introduced the fixed-bit guessing-ratio problem, analyzed greedy posterior ordering over the binary erasure channel, and proved the mutual-best-response property of greedy and lexicographic decoding for ε≤1/2\varepsilon\le1/2. Their Conjecture 1 posits greedy optimality for every positive moment. Their binary-symmetric-channel majority analysis already contains a related Gaussian integral. Gaussian approximation, the method of types, endpoint Laplace asymptotics, and Mellin periodization are established methods, not inventions of this paper.

An earlier unpublished companion study [I1] addresses strict finite-prefix improvements in the high-erasure interval and a fixed-moment limit as ε↑1\varepsilon\uparrow1. It explicitly leaves high-moment uniformity open. We do not count its high-erasure counterexample or its all-helper converse as new results here. Our contributions include this joint dependence on nn and ss, a converse for every helper, the optimal low-erasure high-moment power, and the exact finite-block solution at half erasure. The high-erasure part of the universal-greedy-optimality classification in Corollary 9.6 remains an attributed consequence of [I1].

The positive-rate helper problem of Graczyk, Lapidoth, Merhav, and Pfister [GLMP22] optimizes an exponential-in-nn guesswork criterion with nRnR message bits. The fixed-one-bit normalization and growing moment order here are different scales. The unreliable-oracle model of Ardimanov, Shayevitz, and Tamo [AST20] applies noise after a function of the source has been computed; here erasure precedes the helper’s computation. Our theorems do not transfer their exact oracle optimum to this setting. The small-set questions of Ordentlich, Polyanskiy, and Shayevitz [OPS20] concern correlated binary strings. We use that model as an intermediate coupling, with its correlation parameter specified explicitly.

Mossel, O’Donnell, Regev, Steif, and Sudakov [MORSS06, Corollary 3.3 and Theorem 3.4] established the reverse-hypercontractive rectangle bound used here. Their Theorems 4.1–4.2 already combine reverse hypercontractivity and majority to determine a polynomial exponent for noninteractive correlation distillation, or equivalently high moments of a noise-smoothed Boolean function. We claim neither that inequality nor that method pattern. A BEC helper may depend on the full erasure pattern, and its guessing objective involves the two posterior rank orders rather than the high norm of a single BSC-smoothed Boolean function. Lemma 7.2 and Theorem 9.5 provide the bridge for this guessing problem. The correlated pair to which we apply the classical inequality is binary–binary with correlation 1−2ε1-2\varepsilon; it is not the original binary–ternary BEC input-output pair. The latter has zero-probability cells and a different reverse-hypercontractivity region, studied by Nair and Wang [NW17]. We do not infer equivalence of the guessing and correlation-distillation objectives merely from an algebraic match of exponents.

10. Verification, scope, and remaining questions

The paper’s proofs are analytic. The accompanying exact certificate verifies the finite-prefix witness by integer and rational arithmetic; it is not a proof of the general rate theorems. Numerical evaluations of the rate function and posterior sums provide independent checks of signs, constants, and the critical window. The source audit records the exact primary literature and the unpublished predecessor used for comparison. It does not certify global priority.

The following questions remain open in this work:

  • Away from half erasure, the optimal normalized cost over all binary helpers is not characterized in the interior exponential regime. Proposition 5.1 gives the saturation-region exponent and supercritical equivalence. For the sequential limit, Theorem E determines the low-erasure power, but leaves its logarithmic correction and leading constant open; it does not determine the high-erasure optimized power.
  • The finite-block majority-to-greedy ratio is not determined for every n,s,εn,s,\varepsilon.
  • At logsn/n→log2\log s_n/n\to\log2, Theorem C treats its stated last-rank window and does not cover every subexponential offset.
  • No claim is made that the 31-bit witness has minimum size.
  • The dyadic-periodic coefficient is derived only for ε<1/2\varepsilon\lt 1/2. The uniform greedy bounds apply to all 0<ε<10\lt \varepsilon\lt 1.
  • The constants in the two-sided greedy bounds and the asymptotic expansions may depend on a fixed ε\varepsilon. Uniformity of those expansions as erasure tends to 0, 1/21/2, or 1 is not claimed. The numerical constant 1/(2e+1)1/(2e+1) in Theorem E is universal.
  • There is no proof-assistant verification, external peer review, or claim of worldwide novelty certification.

The exact half-erasure solution concerns every nondecreasing loss; the low-erasure optimality result concerns a power exponent after taking the block limit first. These conclusions do not replace the distinct finite-block strategy comparisons.
