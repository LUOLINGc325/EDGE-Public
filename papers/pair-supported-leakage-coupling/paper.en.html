Minimum-distinct couplings: a sharp support threshold

Matching structure, uniform ternary hardness, robust positive-channel gaps, and harmonic profile bounds

Status and contribution scope

This manuscript presents a pair-supported analysis, a computational boundary, and quantitative extensions. External peer review and end-to-end Lean verification have not been completed. The inspected-source novelty assessment is limited: the official full text of the EFC submission [8] was unavailable, and no claim of exhaustive global priority is made.

The general minimum-distinct objective, exponential request-set formulations, logarithmic-order approximation, and probabilistic graph-coloring method have prior sources. In particular, the correlated-rounding results of Ma [12] and the probabilistic-satisfiability construction of Georgakopoulos, Kavvadias and Papadimitriou [13] are essential comparisons. Our principal result concerns the exact boundary in maximum column support, including constant additive separation and robustness to positive perturbation. The harmonic column-profile bound is a secondary refinement of an already studied approximation problem.

Abstract

For finitely many discrete distributions, let C(P)C(P) be the least expected number of distinct outputs in a joint coupling and let T(P)T(P) be the sum of the columnwise maxima. We determine a sharp computational boundary for attaining the lower bound C(P)≥T(P)C(P)\ge T(P). When every output column has support at most two, C(P)C(P) equals the input count minus a capacitated matching-polytope optimum. Odd-set inequalities completely characterize equality, and the exact worst additive and multiplicative gaps at fixed input count follow from fractional-matching half-integrality. At maximum column support three, equality testing is NP-complete, even with every row uniform on exactly three labels. The reduction preserves a no-instance gap of at least 1/31/3; a common positive perturbation retains a gap of at least 1/61/6 while making every matrix entry positive. The hardness construction explicitly preserves all prescribed one-third marginals and adapts classical probabilistic-satisfiability ingredients.

For general channels, we also construct one coupling whose label-yy occurrence probability is at most min(1,∑jP(j)(y)/j)\min(1,\sum_j P_{(j)}(y)/j), where the entries of column yy are sorted in decreasing order. Consequently the same coupling simultaneously satisfies every nonnegative label-cost bound and gives a harmonic guarantee in the maximum column support. The general logarithmic order was already established in correlated rounding; our statement refines its dependence on the column profile. A one-sided truncation interval, an exact positive four-row family, and finite rational diagnostics complement the general proofs. The exact worst ratio at support three and a general dense-channel optimum remain unresolved.

3. Exactly which channels attain the leakage bound?

We use Edmonds’ classical matching-polytope theorem [3], with its full attribution:

𝖬(G)={x≥0:∑e∋ixe≤1(i∈[n]),x(E(S))≤|S|−12(|S| odd)}. \mathsf M(G)=\left\{x\ge0: \sum_{e\ni i}x_e\le1\ (i\in[n]),\quad x(E(S))\le\frac{|S|-1}{2}\ (|S|\text{ odd})\right\}. \tag{9}

The graph theorem is an imported result. The contribution considered here is its exact coupling representation and the consequences established below.

Corollary 2 (complete zero-gap criterion). Under (PS),

C(P)=T(P)⇔w(E(S))≤|S|−12for every odd S⊆[n]. \boxed{C(P)=T(P)\iff w(E(S))\le\frac{|S|-1}{2}\quad\text{for every odd }S\subseteq[n].} \tag{10}

Indeed, (6) vanishes precisely when the optimizer can equal ww coordinatewise. The degree constraints for ww already follow from (4). Equation (9) leaves exactly the displayed odd-set constraints.

Equivalently, the exact gap is the minimum amount of overlap that must be removed to satisfy these constraints:

Δ(P)=min{d(E):0≤d≤w,d(E(S))≥w(E(S))−|S|−12for all odd S}. \Delta(P)=\min\left\{d(E):0\le d\le w,\quad d(E(S))\ge w(E(S))-\frac{|S|-1}{2}\ \text{for all odd }S\right\}. \tag{11}

This is an optimization over simultaneous constraints. It cannot generally be replaced by the largest single positive defect. Two disjoint triangles with all capacities 1/21/2 have total gap 11, while every individual odd-set defect is at most 1/21/2.

Corollary 3 (four-input formula). For n=4n=4 under (PS),

C(P)=T(P)+max(0,max|S|=3{w(E(S))−1}). C(P)=T(P)+\max\left(0,\max_{|S|=3}\{w(E(S))-1\}\right). \tag{12}

Only triples give nontrivial odd-set constraints. At most one can be violated: the sum of the weights of triangles 123123 and 124124 is dw(1)+dw(2)≤2d_w(1)+d_w(2)\le2, and relabeling treats every pair of distinct triples. If one triple violates by δ>0\delta>0, remove total weight δ\delta from its edges. This is possible, since its weight is 1+δ1+\delta, and it cannot worsen any other upper constraint. Equation (11) proves (12).

This four-input consequence is closely related to, rather than independent of, the sufficient condition in [2, Theorem 3]. Under (PS), that paper’s NijN_{ij} becomes wijw_{ij} and its second-largest column sum becomes WW. Its condition says that the sum of the maxima in the three opposite-edge pairs is at most one. The eight choices of one edge from each pair give the four stars and four triangles. Stars already obey the degree constraints; the condition is therefore exactly the triangle criterion in this subclass. We establish its necessity here and quantify the failure through (12).

5.1. A standard half-integrality fact, with proof

Let

𝖥(G)={w≥0:dw(i)≤1 for every i}. \mathsf F(G)=\{w\ge0:d_w(i)\le1\text{ for every }i\}.

Its extreme points consist of disjoint unit-weight edges and vertex-disjoint odd cycles whose edges all have weight 1/21/2, with zero weight elsewhere. This is the classical fractional-matching half-integrality description. We include the elementary argument used here.

At an extreme point, remove zero edges and remove each unit edge with its endpoints. The latter cannot touch another positive edge. Discard isolated vertices from this argument. In a remaining connected component every edge has weight strictly between zero and one. Call a vertex tight when its weighted degree is one. If the edge-incidence matrix restricted to tight vertices has a nonzero kernel vector, perturbing by sufficiently small positive and negative multiples preserves tight equations, slack inequalities, and positivity. Extremality therefore requires at most as many edges as tight vertices.

A tree component has at least two leaves; both are slack because their single edge has weight below one. It has v−1v-1 edges but at most v−2v-2 tight vertices, a contradiction. Any component with more than vv edges also contradicts the rank bound. A remaining component must therefore have exactly vv edges and all vv vertices tight. No vertex can have unweighted degree one; average degree is two, so the component is a cycle. An even cycle admits an alternating perturbation. On an odd cycle the equations wi−1+wi=1w_{i-1}+w_i=1 uniquely force wi=1/2w_i=1/2. Conversely, unit edges and odd half-cycles are uniquely fixed on their positive support by the tight equations, proving the description.

10.4. Rational certificates and membership in NP

For the restricted class, equality has a rational witness consisting of supported tuples and their nonnegative probabilities. Its linear system contains at most 3N3N marginal equations, normalization, and the equation that expected cost equals TT. A feasible system has a basic feasible solution with at most 3N+23N+2 atoms. Each atom requires NN label indices. Coefficients are integers between zero and NN; right-hand sides have polynomial bit length. Cramer’s rule and the determinant bound give polynomial bit length for every basic weight. A verifier checks weights, normalization, all marginals, and the expected distinct count by rational arithmetic in polynomial time. This is the standard sparse-certificate principle used in probabilistic satisfiability [13, Proposition 1 and Corollary 1].

The same argument, with at most NM+2NM+2 equations and the input rational denominators, puts the general rational equality problem in NP. Together with Sections 10.2–10.3 and the matching algorithm, this completes Theorem 9. ▫\square

13. Related work, exact contribution boundaries, and open questions

13.1. Information-theoretic attainability

Makur and Singh [1, Theorem 4 and Proposition 1; 2, Theorem 3] establish the max-Doeblin/minimum-distinct connection, a general sufficient attainability condition, the three-input optimum, and a stronger sufficient condition for four inputs. The matching result solves an arbitrary-input structural class. Theorem 9 then identifies the immediately adjacent hard class in the natural column-incidence parameter. This is a computational classification of the attainability question, not a solution of every dense-channel optimum or of the benchmark papers’ separate network-contraction theory.

The four-input specialization overlaps substantially with [2]: Section 3 explains exactly how its sufficient condition becomes necessary under (PS). The concept of simultaneous maximal coupling and its pairwise incompatibilities are also prior work [9,10]. Under (PS), equality is precisely simultaneous saturation of all pair overlaps. Neither that concept nor a three-cycle obstruction is a novelty claim.

13.2. Correlated rounding and the inherited approximation scale

Ma [12] studies the same categorical-marginal objective: items correspond to rows, fulfillment centers to labels, and the number of activated centers to the distinct count. His Theorem 1 gives a per-label 1+logn1+\log n guarantee; nonnegative weighted bounds follow immediately. Lemma 4 and Corollary 1 explain the set-cover connection and logarithmic-order lower bound. Section 4 provides a general exponential-size request-set optimization. These facts correct an omission from the earlier project positioning and are central prior work here.

Theorem 13 refines that approximation statement to the exact harmonic factor and the entry-sensitive profile ∑jp(j),y/j\sum_jp_{(j),y}/j. Its parameter rr counts rows containing a label; the separate sparsity guarantee in [12] counts labels available to a row. Poisson clocks, shared randomness, positive association, set-cover obstructions, and the existence of a general coupling LP are not new mechanisms. The random-incidence lower construction records explicit parameters within a classical technique, with [15] providing a close comparison. Its logarithmic order is supporting context, not the main originality claim.

13.3. Probabilistic consistency and fractional coloring

The base color indicators, one-third marginals, and cyclic-color averaging in Section 10.2 are adapted from [13]. General marginal-consistency NP-hardness and the sparse-certificate principle likewise predate this work. Occurrence copying is a standard bounded-occurrence idea. The proof obligation particular to the present theorem is to impose support at most three while maintaining every categorical marginal, and then turn failure of exact selection into a fixed additive distinct-count defect.

There is an equivalent fractional-coloring description of the uniform ternary equality problem. Form the conflict graph on output labels, joining two labels whenever a row contains both. Every row induces a triangle. An exact selection is an independent set meeting every specified triangle. A balanced distribution on exact selections, multiplied by three, is a fractional coloring of total weight three covering every vertex once.

Conversely, suppose independent sets have nonnegative weights of total at most three and cover every label with weight at least one. Any specified triangle forces total weight at least three, since an independent set meets it in at most one vertex. At total weight three, its three cover inequalities must all be tight and every positive-weight independent set must meet that triangle. Applying this to every row and normalizing the weights by three gives the balanced distribution. Unused labels were removed, so every vertex belongs to a specified triangle. Thus equality is equivalent to fractional chromatic number at most three for this conflict graph.

If column support is at most three, every vertex belongs to at most three specified triangles and has degree at most six. Classical weighted fractional-coloring complexity is established in [7, Section 7]; general fixed-threshold hardness is also standard [16, Theorem 3.9.2]. We claim no new general hardness principle in that framework. The restricted triangle-incidence representation, the adjacent matching regime, and the quantitative cost consequences are the assertions proved here. The searched sources did not supply the same restricted theorem, but that is a bounded literature assessment rather than an exhaustive priority result.

13.4. Other settings and the unavailable EFC manuscript

Structured color-complement distributions appear in the grand-coupling analysis of [4]. Tree-coupled policy evaluation [5] and min-Doeblin contraction curves [6] address different surrounding applications. None of their broad applications is inherited without separate hypotheses. No measured savings in adaptive language-model evaluation, fulfillment systems, or an end-to-end implementation are claimed.

The full text of the related EFC submission [8] was unavailable. Observed secondary discussion concerns a general request-set program, monotone costs, and a small-total-alphabet special case; such a description does not establish either equivalence or non-equivalence to our precise column-support statements. We do not certify its unseen contents, claim that its full-text comparison has been completed, or depend on it for any proof. Generic formulation priority is in any event excluded by the directly inspected work [12].

The novelty assessment is limited to the incremental statements above and the sources available for comparison.

13.5. Remaining mathematical questions

The exact worst ratio for maximum column support three is open here. The profile theorem gives H3=11/6H_3=11/6; a four-row example gives a lower bound 3/23/2: take four labels and let row ii be uniform on the three labels other than ii. At least two labels are needed in every supported tuple, while T=4/3T=4/3. To attain cost two, choose an unordered pair of labels uniformly among the six pairs; a row missing one chosen label uses the other, and each remaining row chooses between the pair by a fair coin. Every row is uniform on its three allowed labels, and both chosen labels occur. Thus C=2C=2. This example does not prove that 3/23/2 is the universal support-three bound.

A general efficient characterization of exact equality would contradict Theorem 9 unless P=NP\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}. Useful subclasses inside support three, tighter instance-dependent bounds, and stronger noise regimes remain legitimate structural questions. The positive-channel theorem uses an inverse-polynomial perturbation; a dimension-independent noise threshold or a constant multiplicative hardness factor is not established.
