Gradual stability transitions for nonregular median estimation

Research manuscript, 26 November 2026. Mathematical results and proofs are presented for independent research review. This manuscript has not undergone external peer review. Lean formalization is a separate, still outstanding deliverable.

Abstract

Does average-case algorithmic stability necessarily produce an abrupt transition from uninformative estimation to the unconstrained statistical rate? We give a negative answer for an entire nonparametric class of median problems. Fix r>1r>1 and 0<c≤2−r−10 \lt c\le 2^{-r-1}. Consider all continuous distributions on [−1,1][-1,1] whose unique median qq satisfies a two-sided polynomial CDF margin of order rr and constant cc throughout the support. For every sample size n≥1n\ge1 and stability budget β≥0\beta\ge0, the squared-error minimax risk is, up to constants depending only on r,cr,c,

min{1,n−1/r+(nβ)−2/(r−1)}. \min\{1,\ n^{-1/r}+(n\beta)^{-2/(r-1)}\}.

The same rate holds for pairwise leave-one-out average stability, every finite power version, and expected worst-case replacement stability. Constraints hold on arbitrary data sets and allow randomized, order-dependent procedures. The lower bound converts average deletion stability into a local Lipschitz bound on the expected response of a Bernoulli experiment. A fixed conditional sampling kernel transfers this obstruction to a least-favorable median submodel. The upper bound uses a measurable countable metric envelope that simultaneously preserves a prescribed Lipschitz budget and the functional’s original Hölder modulus. We also prove a nonasymptotic modulus-and-approximation sandwich for every continuous functional on an interior Bernoulli interval. For nonregular medians, consistency requires and is achieved by nβ→∞n\beta\to\infty, while the ordinary rate requires a larger budget of order n−(r+1)/(2r)n^{-(r+1)/(2r)}. The resulting polynomially wide intermediate regime answers the existence question in Chakraborty, Luo and Barber [1, §8.3].

Introduction and contribution

An estimator can be perfectly stable by ignoring its observations. The statistical question is therefore how much accuracy is compatible with a specified limit on data sensitivity. We use the fixed-data, shared-seed definitions of [1]: in the average constraint one averages changes across pairs of deletions from the same n+1n+1 observations, then takes a supremum over the original data set. This is different from averaging stability over random data.

The primary result is Theorem 10.1. It concerns the whole class defined in (10.2), not just a parametric example or a selected family of algorithms. The class permits arbitrary continuous CDFs, including singular distributions where admissible. Its least-favorable members have a locally flat CDF at the median. Their inverse CDF responds to small probability changes with a fractional power. This creates two distinct budget scales: one permits consistency and the other permits the ordinary statistical rate. A deterministic symmetric estimator attains the upper rate simultaneously for every stability constraint considered here.

The reusable lower-bound result is Theorem 2.1. On an interior Bernoulli experiment, the mean response of every average-stable estimator must have Lipschitz constant at most a fixed multiple of nβn\beta. The distance of the target to such functions is therefore an unavoidable bias. We prove the reduction for arbitrary randomization and input ordering; these details are necessary because the original randomized stability classes are not literally nested at the worst-case endpoint. Lemma 9.1 supplies the matching upper-bound mechanism in Hölder metric models, with explicit measurability conditions and no algorithmic-efficiency claim.

The closest stability work [1] already provides the decision-theoretic framework, general lower bounds, and worst-case coupling tools. Its latest checked version, v2 dated 22 August 2026, retains the average-stability gradual-transition question. We resolve that existence question; we do not characterize the still-open intermediate regime of its nonparametric regression example. Classical modulus methods [2,4], scalar metric envelopes, and private-median extension methods [3] provide important context. These ingredients themselves are not claimed as new. The precise comparisons and the scope of the literature audit appear in Section 11 and the accompanying novelty report.

The reader can start with the class and result in Section 10, then consult Sections 3–6 for the average-stability obstruction, Section 8 for its transport to mixtures, and Section 9 for the constructive upper bound. All asymptotic comparisons keep the model constants fixed. The paper proves rates, rather than leading minimax constants.

3. Removing randomization and ordering

Assume the target values lie in a compact interval [m,M][m,M]. Projecting T onto this interval cannot increase squared loss at any target in the interval. Since interval projection is 1-Lipschitz, it does not increase any SsS_s or SwcS_{\mathrm{wc}}. Thus we may assume T bounded and avoid integrability qualifications.

Define

For SsS_s, a common permutation of n positions does not in general commute with two different deletions. The following N-position coupling is the correct argument.

10.5 Interpretation and exact range of claims

The theorem determines rates up to constants depending on r and c; it does not determine a leading asymptotic minimax constant.

For each fixed r>1r>1, the risk is bounded away from zero when nβn\beta stays bounded, decreases as (nβ)−2/(r−1)(n\beta)^{-2/(r-1)} in the intermediate regime, and reaches the ordinary rate n−1/rn^{-1/r} once

β≳n−(r+1)/(2r). \beta\gtrsim n^{-(r+1)/(2r)}.

For 1≪nβ≪n(r−1)/(2r)1\ll n\beta\ll n^{(r-1)/(2r)}, the intermediate risk tends to zero but is asymptotically larger than the unconstrained rate. This is a polynomially wide, gradual phase transition even under average-case stability, and its rate is shared by all the finite-s constraints and the weak worst-case endpoint. Consistency is equivalent to nβn→∞n\beta_n\to\infty.

More explicitly, put βn=n−γ\beta_n=n^{-\gamma} and γ0=(r+1)/(2r)\gamma_0=(r+1)/(2r). The theorem gives the risk exponent

Rn,βn≍n−er(γ),er(γ)={1/r,γ≤γ0,2(1−γ)/(r−1),γ0<γ<1,0,γ≥1. R_{n,\beta_n}\asymp n^{-e_r(\gamma)},\qquad e_r(\gamma)=\begin{cases} 1/r,&\gamma\le\gamma_0,\\ 2(1-\gamma)/(r-1),&\gamma_0<\gamma<1,\\ 0,&\gamma\ge1. \end{cases} \tag{10.17}

This also verifies the gradual-transition interpretation under the two-regime definition of [1]. Choose two distinct fixed exponents strictly between γ0\gamma_0 and 1. Both associated risks tend to zero and are larger than n−1/rn^{-1/r} by a polynomial factor. Neither budget can eventually lie well below or well above a proposed sharp threshold, even allowing any fixed powers of logn\log n. Yet their ratio is a nonzero power of nn, so they cannot both stay within such a logarithmic window. If a proposed threshold oscillates, the same argument applies on a subsequence. Thus no single sharp threshold up to polylogarithmic factors describes this family.

The class includes distributions with ordinary positive density at their median as well as the least-favorable flat-median mixtures. Its worst-case behavior is governed by the latter. No assertion is made about adaptation to an unknown r or c, confidence intervals, exact constants, or the computational implementation of the countable-envelope estimator.

Their constraint is local differential privacy, which compares distributions of privatized outputs. The present constraint controls changes using a common random seed. Neither theorem is asserted to imply or dominate the other. Their scope across functional models is broader; the contribution here is a distinct stability obstruction and a complete nonregular-median risk curve.

Median extension precedent. Tzamos, Vlatakis-Gkaragkounis and Zadik [3] obtain nearly tight private median rates and an efficient procedure under a positive-density assumption near the median. They already use an extension argument from typical samples. Our class instead includes medians with vanishing density, and our budget is the same-seed stability parameter rather than a differential-privacy parameter. We do not claim that using an extension to estimate a median is new, and we make no efficiency claim for (10.5).

Later ensembling work. Barber and Samworth [5] give general stability guarantees through a covariance operator associated with an averaging scheme. Their framework is broader as a stability construction, but it does not determine the nonregular-median minimax risk or remove the accuracy cost quantified here. We do not claim averaging or conditioning as a new principle.

Approximation precedent. The uniform approximation formula and the power-law error of Lipschitz approximation are classical approximation-theory ingredients; see [6] for a direct study of moduli and Lipschitz approximation, and [7] for the foundational scalar extension method. Section 5 supplies the needed short proof rather than treating the formula as an original contribution.

Claim boundaries. The candidate new contribution is the complete risk law (10.3), supported by the general average-stability approximation obstruction and its precise treatment of randomization, ordering, and conditional sampling. The envelope formula, Bernstein differentiation, testing inequalities, and empirical-process moment proof are supplied as proof ingredients. No assertion is made about unknown-margin adaptation, unbounded support, all possible quantiles, multivariate medians, confidence intervals, exact leading constants, or the behavior of the classes as r↓1r\downarrow1 or c↓0c\downarrow0.
