Sharp Spectral Dissipation and Unstable Thermodynamic Identification

26 November 2026

Abstract

We establish a sharp nonlinear relation between entropy production, total exit rate, oscillation frequency, and the girth of a finite Markov network with uniform stationary distribution. The argument retains the current weights in a cycle decomposition and applies to every eigenmode, including nonnormal generators. A uniformly driven cycle attains the bound. For a reversible sampled transition matrix, the discrete frequencies of its matrix logarithms turn this inequality into a positive, observable dissipation gap. We then solve the entire reversible-observation fiber within the homogeneous two-layer abelian model: the symmetric rates are uniquely determined, and the remaining generators are a finite set of truncated integer resonances with specified parity. Finally, on a fixed ten-state Petersen network we construct an exact observation with a finite set of compatible positive entropy production rates, while every positive-radius observation neighborhood allows unbounded entropy. The topology, symmetric rates, stationary law, total rate, and a positive mixing bound remain fixed. Consequently every uniformly valid finite-sample entropy upper confidence limit is infinite with at least its nominal coverage probability at that exact observation. We compute the nonzero observation derivative and the exact Fisher information, derive the optimal fixed-risk sample complexity proportional to e4E/se^{4E/s} for specified hypotheses, and obtain a sharp logarithmic divergence law for the robust upper entropy envelope at its critical observation radius. Along this explicit family, HH exact entropy values coexist with inverse Fisher information growing doubly exponentially in HH, while a positive mixing bound persists. Classical spectral, cycle, matrix-logarithm, and statistical tools and the inherited four-topology classification are distinguished from these extensions.

1. Problem, principal conclusions, and prior work

An irreducible continuous-time Markov chain can be irreversible even when its transition matrix at one positive observation time is reversible. This complicates thermodynamic inference: a symmetric observed kernel does not by itself prove that the underlying entropy production vanishes. We ask which quantitative information survives, first for general uniformly stationary networks and then for a structured class where every exact compatible generator can be identified.

Our principal result is the bound (3.1). It holds for any connected finite support of girth gg, with no normality or simple-spectrum assumption. In particular, without information about girth the optimal quadratic coefficient is 24/n24/n in σT>24ω2/n\sigma T>24\omega^2/n for ω>0\omega>0; the strict inequality has that sharp infimum. The nonlinear bound is stronger and records the rate-feasibility boundary.

For a symmetric positive definite observation P=eΔQP=e^{\Delta Q}, every nonreversible embedding has an imaginary eigenvalue of magnitude at least 2π/Δ2\pi/\Delta. Equations (4.1)–(4.2) therefore give a positive entropy gap using −logdetP-\log\det P, nn, and Δ\Delta, with a stronger version when a girth constraint is genuinely imposed on the alternative embeddings. If the principal logarithm is not a Markov generator, every compatible finite-entropy generator lies above this positive gap. These conclusions do not require enumeration of all logarithms.

13. Scope and unresolved extensions

The general sharp frontier requires a uniform stationary law. A sharp analogue depending on an arbitrary specified stationary distribution is not established here. The girth-based spectral frontier is attained on cycles; this does not assert that all frequencies of a long cycle can simultaneously satisfy the resonances of a symmetric sample. Sharpness of the topology-free positive sampling gap and sharpness of the general spectral bound have therefore been stated separately.

The finite exact fiber is a theorem about the labelled homogeneous two-layer model. It does not turn the unrestricted logarithm fiber into a finite set. The statistical obstruction assumes uniform coverage over a strict-rate class with no known lower bound on reverse rates. It is not an impossibility statement for better-resolved observations, rate-ratio priors, or all possible observation schemes. The critical positive error radius itself is deliberately excluded from Theorem 8.1.

The unequal-spoke classification and stability of exact topology selection remain unresolved. We preserve the original symmetric-spoke problem rather than treating preliminary necessary conditions for a larger model as a completed classification.

A7. Graph checks and scope

Each listed graph is connected and cubic. In every case the two step sets {±a},{±b}\{\pm a\},\{\pm b\} are disjoint. A cycle with two spokes and only two layer edges would require a step shared by the layers, so a mixed four-cycle is impossible. A simple cycle with four or more spokes has length at least eight, since between two consecutive spokes it must contain a layer edge. A mixed three-cycle is excluded by the same counting. The order-10 graph has a five-cycle within a layer. Each of the other graphs is bipartite, by the parity of the cyclic coordinate and the opposite choice in the other layer. The order-16 and order-24 cyclic examples have mixed six-cycles with two layer edges in each layer because 2b=−2a2b=-2a. The product-group example has cycles of length six within each layer.
