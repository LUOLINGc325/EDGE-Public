The exact loss of covariance intersection under graph-structured uncertainty

Abstract

An undirected graph specifies which sensor-error cross-covariances may be nonzero. We determine the worst loss of safe scalar covariance inflation against all conservative linear unbiased estimators. For every fixed graph the worst optimized trace ratio equals the least uniform Loewner factor. Both equal an explicit finite-dimensional maximum of a mixed-to-directional quadratic ratio, stabilize in a dimension of order the square root of the number of sensors, and are computable real algebraic numbers. A state-coordinate change converts an extremal mixed covariance witness into an ordinary trace example without rounding. Clique inequalities characterize safety precisely on perfect graphs. Over the class of perfect graphs with clique number at most kk, the sharp universal factor is k/(1+(k−1)2/π)k/(1+(k-1)2/\pi). For k≥2k\ge2 this factor is sharp even over connected graphs with intersecting maximal cliques. For any fixed graph the limit over disjoint copies is an exact finite Gaussian variational formula and is attained asymptotically in dimension at most the size of one copy. The scalar inflation family is lossless precisely for one complete bipartite component with arbitrary isolated vertices, including the graph with no edges. The three minimal obstructions already have strict trace loss in dimension two.

Reading guide

Covariance intersection estimates a common state from measurements whose errors can be correlated. Each scalar weight inflates one known sensor covariance before applying the best linear unbiased rule. A weight is safe when its inflated independent model dominates every joint covariance permitted by the graph. The four-vertex path gives a concrete example with overlapping groups. For nonnegative weights, its three edge inequalities w1+w2≤1w_1+w_2\le1, w2+w3≤1w_2+w_3\le1, and w3+w4≤1w_3+w_4\le1 describe all safe weights. Nevertheless, Section 4 constructs a conservative estimator whose reported trace is strictly smaller than that of any safe inflation.

The exact price of a graph compares the two optimized traces over all admissible observation models. The same number is the least factor for which each conservative estimator can be matched by some safe inflation rule in every state direction at once. Section 2 compares a single state direction with a positive semidefinite mixture of directions. Section 6 shows how to turn a worst mixture into an actual observation model and makes the two meanings of the price agree. Sections 7 and 8 determine sharp bounds for overlapping cliques and for many disjoint copies of an arbitrary graph, respectively. The auxiliary Gaussian vectors in these proofs describe state directions. The sensor errors themselves only need the stated covariance constraints.

5. A sharp price for independent correlation groups

For a union of cliques, errors in different groups have zero cross-covariance; within a group all admissible cross-covariances are unknown. Let k≥1k\ge1 be an upper bound for the number of sensors in any group. We allow any finite number of groups, any observation dimension and positive-definite marginals.
