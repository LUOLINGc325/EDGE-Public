# Abstract

We study tomography of the time of the first counted quantum jump using its Laplace transform at finitely many frequencies. At each frequency the data are a single effect, determined by allowing arbitrary initial states. We give an exact positive semidefinite realization test and describe its full solution set by a simultaneous commutant. Two samples never identify the Hamiltonian and total jump rate matrix when that rate matrix is positive definite. Three prescribed distinct positive samples suffice on an open dense set, and we give a qubit example in which two samples suffice on the positive semidefinite boundary. For arbitrary systems, a Krylov dimension gives a finite number of samples determining the entire first-count transform and its exact remaining Hamiltonian ambiguity.

# 1. First-count data and results

Work on a finite-dimensional Hilbert space of dimension \(d\). A Hermitian Hamiltonian \(H\) and a completely positive counted jump map \(J\) give total rate matrix \(G=J^*(I)\ge0\), with the star denoting the Hilbert–Schmidt adjoint. All jumps are counted. The unnormalized no-jump evolution is generated by \(-\mathcal A\), where
\[
 \mathcal A(X)=i[H,X]+\tfrac12\{G,X\}.
\]
Let \(T_1\) be the first count time, with \(\exp(-sT_1)=0\) when the count never occurs. For \(s>0\), define the effect \(B_s\) by
\[
 \mathbb E_\rho \exp(-sT_1)=\operatorname{tr}(\rho B_s)
\]
for each initial density matrix \(\rho\). Data for all \(\rho\) identify the matrix \(B_s\). This measures the timing distribution at the selected Laplace point. The full map recording the post-count state is a different observation.

Put \(F=G/2-iH\) and define \(\mathcal L(X)=FX+XF^\dagger=\mathcal A^*(X)\). The survival matrix is
\[
 M_s=\frac{I-B_s}{s}=(sI+\mathcal L)^{-1}(I).                              \tag{1}
\]
In expressions involving a superoperator, \(sI\) denotes \(s\) times its identity map. Physically \(0\le B_s<I\), so \(M_s\) is positive definite.

**Theorem 1.** Given effects \(0\le B_j<I\) at positive frequencies \(s_j\), let \(M_j=(I-B_j)/s_j\). They are realizable exactly when a complex matrix \(F\) satisfies
\[
 F+F^\dagger\ge0,\qquad
 F M_j+M_j F^\dagger=I-s_j M_j\quad\text{for every }j.                      \tag{2}
\]
If \(F_0\) is one feasible matrix, all feasible ones are
\[
 F=F_0+M_1^{1/2}Y M_1^{-1/2},
 \quad Y^\dagger=-Y,\quad [Y,C_j]=0\ (j\ge2),\qquad
 C_j=M_1^{-1/2}M_jM_1^{-1/2},
 \tag{3}
\]
subject to the same positivity in (2).

**Theorem 2.** Let \(d\ge2\). One time sample never identifies \((G,H)\) up to a scalar Hamiltonian shift. For \(G>0\), two samples also never identify it. For any three fixed distinct positive frequencies, three samples identify \((G,H)\) up to a scalar shift on an open dense, full-measure subset of pairs with \(G>0\). Two samples can already identify the pair at some points with singular \(G\).

**Theorem 3.** For a given system, write
\[
 \mathcal O=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}\{I,\mathcal L(I),
          \mathcal L^2(I),\ldots\},\qquad \ell=\dim_{\mathbb R}\mathcal O .
\]
Any \(\ell\) distinct positive samples determine the entire curve \(B_s\), with \(\ell\le d^2\). The full curve determines \(G\), and two Hamiltonians with that rate give the same full curve exactly when their difference commutes with every element of \(\mathcal O\). The timing observations determine the jump map only through \(G=J^*(I)\).

# 2. Realization and finite-sample ambiguity

For a given model the probability of no count by time \(t\) is \(\operatorname{tr}[e^{-t\mathcal A}(\rho)]\). Integrating its survival function against \(s e^{-st}\), or integrating the first-count law by parts, gives (1). Equivalently, the Laplace kernel of the full post-count map is \(R_s=J(sI+\mathcal A)^{-1}\), so \(B_s=(sI+\mathcal L)^{-1}G\) and \((sI+\mathcal L)I=sI+G\). The operator exponential of \(-\mathcal L\) acts by \(X\mapsto e^{-tF}X e^{-tF^\dagger}\). Since \(F+F^\dagger\ge0\), the matrix exponential \(e^{-tF}\) is a contraction for \(t\ge0\) by differentiating the squared norm of \(e^{-tF}v\). Thus the resolvent identity
\[
 (sI+\mathcal L)^{-1}(X)
     =\int_0^\infty e^{-st}e^{-tF}X e^{-tF^\dagger}\,dt
\]
holds. It gives \(B_s\ge0\) and \(M_s>0\). For the strict positivity of \(M_s\), the integrand for \(X=I\) is positive definite for every finite \(t\). Equation (1) then yields \(B_s<I\).

Equation (2) is necessary. If it holds, put \(G=F+F^\dagger\), \(H=(F^\dagger-F)/(2i)\), and choose \(J(X)=\sqrt G\,X\sqrt G\). The construction has \(\mathcal L(X)=FX+XF^\dagger\) and the inverse above exists. Equations (2) give exactly the specified matrices in (1). This also shows that any completely positive \(J\) with the given \(G\) gives the same timing curve.

For a particular feasible \(F_0\), its difference \(\Delta F\) to another solution must satisfy \(\Delta F M_j+M_j\Delta F^\dagger=0\). Multiplying the \(j=1\) relation by \(M_1^{-1/2}\) on both sides says \(Y=M_1^{-1/2}\Delta F M_1^{1/2}\) is skew-Hermitian. The other relations become \(Y C_j+C_jY^\dagger=0\), equivalently commutation. This proves (3). The scalar directions \(Y=i\alpha I\) give Hamiltonian shifts and are always present.

With only one sample, a non-scalar Hermitian matrix commuting with \(M_1\) always exists for \(d\ge2\), including when \(M_1\) is scalar. Using \(i\) times that matrix as \(\Delta F\) leaves \(G\) unchanged and preserves the sample. With two samples the single Hermitian \(C_2\) similarly has a non-scalar skew-Hermitian matrix in its commutant. At a positive definite \(G\), a sufficiently small real multiple of the resulting \(\Delta F\) preserves positive definiteness in (2). It is a genuine ambiguity beyond a scalar Hamiltonian shift. This argument uses positive definiteness for the two-sample assertion.

# 3. Three samples identify the generic system

Fix distinct positive frequencies \(s_1,s_2,s_3\). It suffices to produce one physical pair \(G>0,H\) where the simultaneous commutant of \(C_2,C_3\) is scalar, and then make the condition generic. Use
\[
 Z_s=M_{s_1}^{-1}M_s.
\]
It is simultaneously similar to the normalized Hermitian matrices because \(C_s=M_{s_1}^{1/2}Z_sM_{s_1}^{-1/2}\).

Let \(G=\operatorname{diag}(g_1,\ldots,g_d)\) with positive distinct \(g_i\), and put \(H=\epsilon H_0\) where \(H_0\) is Hermitian with all off-diagonal entries nonzero. At \(\epsilon=0\),
\[
 (M_s)_{ii}=\frac1{s+g_i},\qquad
 (Z_s)_{ii}=z_{s,i}=\frac{s_1+g_i}{s+g_i}.
\]
In particular the \(Z_{s_2}\) eigenvalues are simple. For \(i\ne j\) set \(\bar g=(g_i+g_j)/2\). A prime denotes the derivative at \(\epsilon=0\). Differentiating the Lyapunov equation in (2) gives
\[
 (M'_s)_{ij}=
 \frac{i(H_0)_{ij}(g_i-g_j)}
      {(s+g_i)(s+g_j)(s+\bar g)} .
\]
Using the derivative of \(M_{s_1}^{-1}M_s\) and the difference of the diagonal entries then yields, for \(s\ne s_1\),
\[
 \frac{(Z'_s)_{ij}}{z_{s,i}-z_{s,j}}
 =-i(H_0)_{ij}\,
       \frac{s+s_1+g_i+\bar g}{(s+\bar g)(s_1+\bar g)}.                    \tag{4}
\]
For clarity the diagonal difference is
\((s-s_1)(g_i-g_j)/((s+g_i)(s+g_j))\), and in the numerator the simplification uses
\[
 (s_1+g_i)(s_1+\bar g)-(s+g_i)(s+\bar g)
       =-(s-s_1)(s+s_1+g_i+\bar g).
\]
For every pair \(i\ne j\), the scalar fraction on the right of (4) varies strictly with \(s\), since it is
\[
 \frac{1}{s_1+\bar g}\left(1+\frac{s_1+g_i}{s+\bar g}\right).
\]
Thus
\[
 [Z_{s_2},Z_{s_3}]'_{ij}
  =(z_{s_2,i}-z_{s_2,j})(z_{s_3,i}-z_{s_3,j})
     \left(
        \frac{(Z'_{s_3})_{ij}}{z_{s_3,i}-z_{s_3,j}}
       -\frac{(Z'_{s_2})_{ij}}{z_{s_2,i}-z_{s_2,j}}
     \right)
\]
is nonzero. In a smoothly varying eigenbasis of \(Z_{s_2}\) equal to the coordinate basis at zero, the commutator has the same first derivative because the commutator itself vanishes at zero. For all sufficiently small nonzero \(\epsilon\), \(Z_{s_2}\) is simple and the commutator is nonzero in every off-diagonal position in that basis. Therefore \(Z_{s_3}\) also has every off-diagonal position nonzero there. An operator commuting with \(Z_{s_2}\) must be diagonal in its eigenbasis, and commutation with \(Z_{s_3}\) makes all those diagonal entries equal. This gives the required scalar commutant for a physical pair.

As functions of the real entries of Hermitian \(G,H\), the matrices \(M_s\), and hence the matrices \(Z_s\), have rational entries with denominators nonzero on the domain \(G>0\). This follows by solving (1) as a finite linear system and then inverting \(M_{s_1}\). The map
\[
 X\longmapsto \bigl([X,Z_{s_2}],[X,Z_{s_3}]\bigr)
\]
on all complex matrices has rank at most \(d^2-1\). A minor of that rank is nonzero at the constructed pair. Clearing denominators makes its vanishing a proper polynomial condition in the real parameters. Its zero set has empty interior and measure zero, and the maximal-rank set is open. This proves the generic assertion. On that set (3) has only the scalar directions, so \((G,H)\) is uniquely identified up to the stated shift among all feasible models.

## A singular rate that is identifiable at two points

Take
\[
 F=\begin{pmatrix}1/2&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 G=F+F^\dagger=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The corresponding \(H=(F^\dagger-F)/(2i)\) is Hermitian. Directly solving (1) at \(s_1=1,s_2=2\) gives
\[
 M_1=\begin{pmatrix}11/18&-1/9\\-1/9&7/9\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 M_2=\begin{pmatrix}9/25&-1/25\\-1/25&23/50\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 M_2M_1^{-1}-M_1^{-1}M_2
   =\begin{pmatrix}0&-6/625\\6/625&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
In particular \(C_2\) is a nonscalar Hermitian \(2\)-by-\(2\) matrix. The skew-Hermitian operators in its commutant are \(i(\alpha I+\beta C_2)\) for real \(\alpha,\beta\). By (3) their shifts of \(F\) are \(i\alpha I+i\beta M_2M_1^{-1}\). The rate matrix is then
\[
 G+i\beta(M_2M_1^{-1}-M_1^{-1}M_2).
\]
Its determinant is negative for every \(\beta\ne0\), since its second diagonal entry is zero and the off-diagonal entry is nonzero. Thus only scalar shifts remain feasible. This proves the existence assertion on the boundary in Theorem 2.

# 4. Determining the full curve from finitely many samples

The space \(\mathcal O\) of Theorem 3 lies in the real space of Hermitian matrices and is invariant under \(\mathcal L\). Its first \(\ell\) powers of \(\mathcal L\) on \(I\), from degrees \(0\) through \(\ell-1\), form a basis. Indeed the first linear dependence among successive powers forces all later powers into the same span. For \(s>0\), \(sI+\mathcal L\) is invertible and restricts invertibly to \(\mathcal O\) by invariance and finite dimension. Thus \(M_s\) lies in \(\mathcal O\).

For any \(\ell\) distinct positive frequencies \(s_1,\ldots,s_\ell\), apply the invertible map \(\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}(s_j I+\mathcal L)\) on \(\mathcal O\) to each survival matrix. The image of \(M_{s_i}\) is
\[
 \prod_{j\ne i}(s_j I+\mathcal L)(I).
\]
The polynomials \(\prod_{j\ne i}(s_j+z)\), as \(i\) varies, form a basis of polynomials of degree below \(\ell\), by evaluation at the distinct values \(-s_j\). Since the first \(\ell\) powers on \(I\) form a basis as well, the matrices \(M_{s_i}\) span \(\mathcal O\).

If a candidate physical generator \(\widetilde{\mathcal L}\) has the same samples, its difference from \(\mathcal L\) vanishes on each \(M_{s_i}\) by their resolvent equations. It therefore vanishes on \(\mathcal O\), and any \(M_s\in\mathcal O\) solves the same resolvent equation for the candidate. Its resolvent is invertible, so the full curves agree. In particular, since \(I\in\mathcal O\), the rate matrix \(G=\mathcal L(I)\) is determined. The difference of generators with the same rate is \(-i[\widetilde H-H,\cdot]\). Vanishing on \(\mathcal O\) is precisely the commutant condition in Theorem 3. Conversely, that condition with equal \(G\) gives the same entire curve by the same resolvent argument. This proves Theorem 3. For example, for a scalar rate \(G=g I\), \(\mathcal O\) has dimension one and \(B_s=gI/(s+g)\) for every \(H\). In dimension one the same formula shows that one sample identifies \(g\), while \(H\) is already scalar.

# 5. Pinned comparison

The pinned first-passage manuscript gives a realization test for a completely positive post-count Laplace kernel at one frequency. Timing tomography supplies its adjoint applied to the identity, and the post-count output state is unobserved. This supplement classifies feasibility and ambiguity from those timing data at several frequencies, proves the exact generic three-sample threshold and a two-sample boundary example, and characterizes the data needed to recover the complete timing curve.
