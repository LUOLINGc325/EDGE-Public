1. Problem, model and contribution boundary

Fix a prime power qq and positive integers n,mn,m, and put d=min(n,m)d=\min(n,m). The input and output alphabets are the n×mn\times m matrices over 𝔽q\mathbb F_q. Independently of the input XX, draw TT from an arbitrary probability law π\pi on {0,…,d}\{0,\ldots,d\}; conditional on T=tT=t, draw WW uniformly among rank-tt matrices; and draw AA uniformly from GLn(𝔽q)\operatorname{GL}_n(\mathbb F_q), independently of (T,W)(T,W). The channel is

Y=A(X+W),Cq(n,m,π)=maxPXIq(X;Y). Y=A(X+W),\qquad C_q(n,m,\pi)=\max_{P_X} I_q(X;Y).

The realization of TT is not revealed to the transmitter or receiver.

Random error rank is established prior work. Silva, Kschischang and Kötter [SKK10, Section VI-D] study variable error rank R≤tR\le t and an entropy penalty H(R)H(R) in comparison with a maximum error-rank budget. In a different, purely multiplicative model Y=GXY=GX, Nóbrega, Silva and Uchôa-Filho [NSU13, Theorem 5] prove that the best constant-input-rank distribution is within logq(min(n,m)+1)\log_q(\min(n,m)+1) of capacity. Their Theorem 7 also gives a large-field formula with a maximum outside an expectation over transfer rank. These structural ideas predate the present work; the model of [NSU13] has no additive noise WW.

The model above is the Gamma channel of Blackburn and Claridge [BC19]. Their article proves that an input distribution uniform conditional on its rank attains capacity and provides an exact finite optimization. Its concluding Open Problem 2 asks for useful capacity asymptotics, specifically mentioning binomial error ranks. Their later article [BC25] treats the fixed-error-rank additive-multiplicative matrix channel, identifies its deterministic leading capacity, and proves an O(ℓlogℓ)O(\ell\log\ell) error in a fixed-field proportional-growth regime. We do not claim the Gamma model, random-error-rank modeling, uniform-given-rank symmetry, the constant-rank entropy comparison, the subspace-counting method, or the fixed-tt leading kernel as new.

The scalar optimization used below has established precedents. Scarf’s mean-variance inventory model [S58] supplies the classical stop-loss moment bound and two-point extremizer. Das, Dhara and Natarajan [DDN21] analyze moment ambiguity, regular variation and contamination effects in robust newsvendor models. These methods do not by themselves control the mutual information of the present hidden-rank matrix channel. Our use of minimax interchange is an application of Sion’s classical theorem [Si58]. We do not claim stop-loss optimization, moment ambiguity, heavy-tail integration, or generic contamination sensitivity as new.

The results connect finite-field matrix geometry to capacity optimization at scales where the full error-rank tail matters:

  1. A nonasymptotic approximation has additive error Oq(m)O_q(m), uniformly over every dimension, admissible input and error rank, and error-rank law. It yields the arbitrary-law n2n^2-normalized limit and the binomial three-regime expansion.
  2. Near the geometric threshold, capacity reduces to a strongly convex stop-loss objective with error O(n+an2)O(n+a_n^2). Its minimizer controls every exact best fixed rank to error O(n+an2)O(\sqrt{n+a_n^2}) under explicit second-moment and nondegeneracy assumptions.
  3. Stretched-exponential and regularly varying tail conditions yield distinct moving-threshold laws, with matching optimal-rank limits and legal integer-valued examples. The binomial nlnnn\ln n correction is a special case of the stretched-exponential law.
  4. Under a mean and variance budget, a common fixed input rank and an admissible integer two-point error law give a sharp information saddle expansion with remainder O(n+vn)O(n+v_n). At the threshold, its correction is 34(Nnvn)2/3\frac34(N_nv_n)^{2/3}.
  5. A rare-contamination construction preserves the exact mean, leading variance, Gaussian limit, convergence of standardized second moments, and vanishing total-variation distance to a binomial law, while changing the capacity correction from order nlnnn\ln n to order n2θn^{2\theta} for 1/2<θ<2/31/2\mathrel{<}\theta\mathrel{<}2/3.

All input-rank localization assertions refer to an achieving fixed-rank design or an exact optimizer within fixed-rank input laws. They do not assert that every exactly capacity-achieving input distribution has a deterministic rank. The information maximin studied below is not identified with an operational compound-channel coding capacity.

3. Symmetry and the exact orbit entropy identity

Let U=row(X)U=\operatorname{row}(X) and V=row(Y)V=\operatorname{row}(Y). The conditional output law depends on XX only through UU. Indeed, if X′=NXX'=NX for invertible NN, take W′=NWW'=NW and A′=AN−1A'=AN^{-1}. Then

A′(X′+W′)=A(X+W), A'(X'+W')=A(X+W),

and (A′,W′)(A',W') has the required law. Given the row space of X+WX+W, multiplication by uniform AA produces a uniform n×mn\times m matrix with that row space. Thus the matrix channel carries exactly the same mutual information as the induced subspace channel:

Iq(X;Y)=Iq(U;V). I_q(X;Y)=I_q(U;V).

Right multiplication by GLm(𝔽q)\operatorname{GL}_m(\mathbb F_q) acts transitively on subspaces of each dimension and preserves the channel. Averaging any input distribution over this action cannot reduce mutual information, by concavity in the input law and equivariance of the channel. We may therefore assume that UU is uniform conditional on K=dimUK=\dim U. Since KK is a function of UU,

Iq(U;V)=Iq(K;V)+𝔼KIq(U;V∣K). I_q(U;V)=I_q(K;V)+\mathbb E_K I_q(U;V\mid K).

16. Interpretation, parameter ranges and limitations

The uniform theorem gives a scalar approximation to Gamma-channel capacity with explicit additive error Oq(m)O_q(m). In proportional growth, it identifies the n2n^2-normalized capacity limit for every convergent scaled error-rank law. A zero normalized limit requires a separate analysis to identify the first nonzero asymptotic scale. Example 2.1 shows that even the leading limit can depend on the entire error-rank distribution, despite identical means.

For binomial errors, substituting the mean rank retains the n2n^2 term but can miss the dominant subleading loss and the input-rank offset. The boundary-limited regime selects a Gaussian quantile and incurs an n3/2n^{3/2} correction. At the threshold, the rank offset reaches the moderate-deviation scale nlnn\sqrt{n\ln n}. Theorems 4–6 express this sensitivity through an explicit stop-loss objective and distinguish stretched-exponential and power-law tails. Their tail hypotheses are stronger than a central limit theorem. Theorem 8 shows that exact means, matching leading variances, the same CLT, second-moment convergence and vanishing total variation do not suffice to fix the critical correction.

Theorem 7 concerns a finite one-use information game under a mean and variance budget. Exact equality of its maximin and minimax values is classical convex-concave minimax. The common fixed-rank strategy and integer two-point adversary achieve its explicit asymptotic value with error O(n+vn)O(n+v_n); they are not asserted to form an exact finite-size saddle. The asymptotic window is n=o(vn)\sqrt n=o(v_n), vn=o(n2)v_n=o(n^2), with bounded dn/(Nnvn)1/3d_n/(N_nv_n)^{1/3} and mean fraction bounded away from zero and one. The signed correction can be negative relative to NnbnN_nb_n.

All claims have their stated parameter scope. The binomial theorem assumes fixed q,p,βq,p,\beta and β≥1\beta\ge1. The local reduction requires a nondegenerate positive limiting tail and a uniform second-moment bound. Each tail theorem imposes an additional moving-scale tail condition, a controlling tail envelope, and an error scale smaller than its retained correction. The rare-contamination construction uses even nn and the specified open exponent interval; its variance agreement is asymptotic, not exact. The explicit integer-valued examples include the effects of truncation, rounding and centering.

The results do not determine exact finite-nn capacity, optimize the universal constants in Theorem 1, provide efficient encoders or decoders, cover adversarial error sequences or noninvertible transfer matrices, or establish finite-blocklength error exponents. They do not identify an operational compound-channel coding capacity, nor assert that every exact capacity optimizer uses a single input rank.
