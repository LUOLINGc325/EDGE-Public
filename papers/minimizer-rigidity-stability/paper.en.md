# Abstract

A tight polygon in an ordered spectral cost gives a sharp lower bound on the operator-norm error of approximation by quantum transport costs. We show that it also restricts every nearly optimal generator. Between any two spectral blocks on the polygon, the off-diagonal block of a Lipschitz function of the generator is bounded by an explicit fourth root of the excess error. A square-root bound holds when a third polygon block lies between the two. At the optimum the corresponding cross spectral measures vanish. When one tight polygon uses all levels, every generator preserves all spectral blocks, every optimal cost has prescribed cross-level blocks, and simple spectra give a unique optimal cost. In the sublinear exponent range we also classify the possible individual generators along this full chain.

# 1. Setting and results

Let \(P_1,\ldots,P_n\) be nonzero mutually orthogonal projections summing to \(I\) on a complex Hilbert space. Work on the tensor product with its conjugate space and use \(B^{\mathsf T}\) for the induced transpose. For a symmetric array \(a_{ij}\) with \(a_{ii}=0\) and \(a_{ij}\ge0\), write
\[
 D=\sum_{i,j}a_{ij}P_i\otimes P_j^{\mathsf T},
 \qquad
 G_p(B)=|B\otimes I-I\otimes B^{\mathsf T}|^p
 \quad (0<p\le1).
\]
The approximation costs \(C\) are finite sums \(\sum_{\ell}G_p(B_\ell)\) of bounded selfadjoint generators. Positive coefficients can be absorbed into the generators. The pinned polygon theorem applies when \(a\) is superadditive on ordered intervals and satisfies the ordered four-point inequality:
\[
 a_{ik}\ge a_{ij}+a_{jk}\ (i<j<k),\qquad
 a_{il}+a_{jk}\ge a_{ik}+a_{jl}\ (i<j<k<l).
\]
It gives the optimal norm error \(\delta\) as the maximum of zero and
\[
 \frac{a_{i_0i_m}-\sum_{r=1}^{m}a_{i_{r-1}i_r}}{m+1}
 \qquad (i_0<\cdots<i_m,\ m\ge2).
\]
It also gives an optimal cost generated by nested projections. Call a chain **tight** when its displayed ratio is \(\delta\). All the stability conclusions below refer to a fixed tight chain; its existence is part of each hypothesis.

**Theorem 1.** Suppose \(\|D-C\|\le\delta+\varepsilon\) with \(\varepsilon\ge0\). Take distinct chain indices \(i<j\) and put \(r=2\) if the chain has a third index strictly between them, and \(r=4\) otherwise. For any generator \(B_\ell\) with spectral diameter \(L_\ell>0\) and any real Lipschitz function \(f\) on its spectral interval,
\[
 \|P_i f(B_\ell)P_j\|
 \le \operatorname{Lip}(f)\,L_\ell^{1-p/r}
              \left(\frac{(m+1)\varepsilon}{1+p}\right)^{1/r}.
 \tag{1}
\]
For scalar generators the cross block is zero. At exact optimality \(P_i E_{B_\ell}(A)P_j=0\) for every Borel spectral set \(A\), where \(E_{B_\ell}\) is the spectral resolution.

**Theorem 2.** Suppose the chain \(1,\ldots,n\) is tight, with \(n\ge3\), and \(C\) is optimal. Every generator commutes with every \(P_i\). Put \(\gamma_i=a_{i,i+1}+\delta\) for \(i<n\). The restriction of \(C\) to \(P_i\otimes P_j^{\mathsf T}\), for \(i<j\), is the scalar \(\sum_{k=i}^{j-1}\gamma_k\). The transpose order has the same scalar. On \(P_i\otimes P_i^{\mathsf T}\), the restriction is positive with norm at most \(\delta\). In particular if every \(P_i\) has rank one, there is a unique optimal cost and it is the nested-projection cost with cut weights \(\gamma_i\).

Under the full-chain hypothesis, every allowed tuple of spectral values of an individual generator's block restrictions is monotone when \(p=1\), and has at most one adjacent change when \(0<p<1\). The orientation of monotonicity may depend on the tuple. In particular for simple spectra the \(p=1\) generators are monotone in the block order, in either direction. Those with \(p<1\) are constant or have exactly one cut between two scalar values. Section 4 makes the sublinear statement explicit for degenerate blocks as well.

# 2. From operator error to scalar triangle defect

Fix unit vectors \(x_{i_k}\) in the ranges of the chain projections. For a generator \(B_\ell\), draw independent spectral samples \(Z^{(\ell)}_{i_k}\) with distributions \(\langle x_{i_k},E_{B_\ell}(\cdot)x_{i_k}\rangle\). For distinct \(i,j\) on the chain, the spectral theorem gives
\[
 c_{ij}:=\langle x_i\otimes\overline{x_j},
                       C(x_i\otimes\overline{x_j})\rangle
       =\sum_\ell \mathbb E|Z^{(\ell)}_i-Z^{(\ell)}_j|^p.
\]
The hypothesis gives \(|a_{ij}-c_{ij}|\le\delta+\varepsilon\). By tightness,
\[
 0\le\sum_{k=1}^m c_{i_{k-1}i_k}-c_{i_0i_m}
        \le (m+1)\varepsilon.                                             \tag{2}
\]
The left side is the sum of expected metric defects of the individual sampled chains, since \(|s-t|^p\) is a metric. The defect is the sum of successive distances minus the distance between the two ends. It is nonnegative and dominates the defect on any ordered subchain. To see the latter, group the original legs inside the subchain's segments and use the triangle inequality on the portions before and after the subchain. Thus (2) bounds the sum of the expected defects of any fixed ordered triple of chain indices.

If \(\varepsilon=0\), this calculation also forces each chain-edge scalar \(c_{i_{k-1}i_k}\) to equal \(a_{i_{k-1}i_k}+\delta\), and the closing scalar to equal \(a_{i_0i_m}-\delta\). These equalities hold for every choice of the unit vectors. On the corresponding product tensor vectors the norm error attains either \(+\delta\) or \(-\delta\). Positivity of \(\delta I\pm(D-C)\) gives the same eigenvalue identity on each such vector, and their linear span is dense in the corresponding tensor block. Consequently these tensor blocks are already scalar restrictions of \(C\), even when the tight chain is shorter than the full chain.

# 3. Controlling the off-diagonal spectral measure

For the two selected indices \(i,j\), fix unit vectors \(x_i,x_j\), and consider one generator \(B\). Choose any third index of the chain, using one strictly between \(i,j\) if possible. In this triple the three vectors are orthogonal because their projection blocks are distinct. Write \(F_i,F_j\) for the two distribution functions and let
\[
 \mu(t)=\langle x_i,E_B((-\infty,t])x_j\rangle .
\]
Cauchy–Schwarz for the spectral projection and for its complement gives
\[
 |\mu(t)|^2\le
       \min\bigl(F_i(t)F_j(t),(1-F_i(t))(1-F_j(t))\bigr).                   \tag{3}
\]
For the complement bound, the cross matrix element changes sign, since \(\langle x_i,x_j\rangle=0\).

At exponent one, the metric defect of an ordered triple can be computed by integrating over real cuts. Each distance \(|x-y|\) is the integral of the indicator that a cut separates \(x,y\). At a cut, the defect of two consecutive legs minus the endpoint distance is two if the middle point lies alone on one side, and zero otherwise. With independent samples, when \(i,j\) are the two ends, the probability of this cut event is a convex combination of \(F_iF_j\) and \((1-F_i)(1-F_j)\). By (3) it is at least \(|\mu(t)|^2\). When one of \(i,j\) is the middle point, that probability is a convex combination of \(F_i(1-F_j)\) and \((1-F_i)F_j\). These lie in \([0,1]\), so their minimum is at least their product. By (3) their product is at least \(|\mu(t)|^4\). If the spectrum of \(B\) lies in \([b,b+L]\), the expected exponent-one triple defect is therefore at least
\[
 2\int_b^{b+L}|\mu(t)|^r\,dt .
\]

For \(0<p\le1\), the scalar exponent-\(p\) triple defect on the same interval is at least \((1+p)L^{p-1}/2\) times the exponent-one defect. If the middle point lies between the ends, the exponent-one defect is zero. Otherwise, let \(u\ge0\) be its distance to the nearer end and \(v\ge0\) the endpoint distance, so \(u+v\le L\). The exponent-one defect is \(2u\) and the exponent-\(p\) defect is
\[
 u^p+(u+v)^p-v^p\ge (1+p)L^{p-1}u .
\]
This follows from \(u^p\ge L^{p-1}u\) and the integral of the derivative \(p t^{p-1}\) over \([v,v+u]\). Denote the expected exponent-\(p\) triple defect of this generator by \(\Delta_{p,B}\). We have proved
\[
 (1+p)L^{p-1}\int_b^{b+L}|\mu(t)|^r\,dt\le\Delta_{p,B}.                   \tag{4}
\]

The complex cross spectral measure has total mass zero. For a Lipschitz \(f\) on \([b,b+L]\), absolute continuity and integration against this measure give
\[
 \langle x_i,f(B)x_j\rangle=-\int_b^{b+L}f'(t)\mu(t)\,dt.
\]
Hölder's inequality and (4) yield
\[
 |\langle x_i,f(B)x_j\rangle|^r
       \le\frac{\operatorname{Lip}(f)^r L^{r-p}}{1+p}\,\Delta_{p,B}.        \tag{5}
\]
Equation (2) bounds \(\Delta_{p,B}\) by \((m+1)\varepsilon\) uniformly over the chosen vectors, proving (1). For fixed \(x_i,x_j\), the normalized quantities in (5), divided by \(\operatorname{Lip}(f)^rL^{r-p}\) for nonconstant \(f\), also sum across generators to at most \((m+1)\varepsilon/(1+p)\). This follows by using the same vectors on the triple and the sum bound in (2).

At \(\varepsilon=0\), (4) makes \(\mu\) zero almost everywhere. Its right continuity and total mass zero imply that it is zero at every real cut, including both interval endpoints. Thus the cross spectral measure vanishes. Taking arbitrary unit vectors on the two blocks proves the spectral assertion of Theorem 1.

The two exponents of the spectral estimate are sharp if the available input is only the expected scalar triple defect. For the adjacent-pair example, take three rank-one coordinate blocks and rotate the first two eigenvectors of a binary projection so their probabilities of the low eigenvalue are \(1-t,t\). Take the third coordinate to have the high eigenvalue with certainty. The expected defect is \(2t^2\), while the cross matrix element has magnitude \(\sqrt{t(1-t)}\). For the endpoint example, rotate the first and third coordinates instead and take the middle coordinate to have the high eigenvalue with certainty. The defect is \(2t(1-t)\) and the same cross magnitude applies. Since distances here are zero or one, both computations hold for all \(0<p\le1\). These examples concern the scalar-defect bound.

# 4. Full-chain equality

If every index occurs on the tight chain, Theorem 1 shows that all cross spectral measures of a generator vanish between distinct blocks. The projections sum to the identity, so each generator preserves the entire block decomposition, as does \(C\) on the corresponding product tensor blocks.

For any product unit vectors on two different blocks \(i<j\), extend them to a choice of product unit vectors on the full chain. The sum of the individual expected defects of its subchain from \(i\) to \(j\) is zero by (2) and subchain domination. Hence
\[
 c_{ij}=\sum_{k=i}^{j-1}c_{k,k+1}
       =\sum_{k=i}^{j-1}(a_{k,k+1}+\delta),
\]
independent of the vectors. A selfadjoint operator whose expectations on all product unit vectors in a tensor block equal a scalar is that scalar times the identity on the block. This follows by applying polarization first in one tensor factor and then in the other. The same conclusion holds in the reverse block order by swapping the spectral samples. The target vanishes on each same-level tensor block, so there \(C\) is positive with norm at most \(\delta\). If the blocks have rank one, every generator's cost on each such block vanishes. The cross-level scalar values are generated by \(\sum_{k<n}\gamma_k G_p(\sum_{i>k}P_i)\), proving the claimed uniqueness as an operator. One family with a full tight chain for every \(n\ge3\) is \(a_{ij}=\max(|i-j|-1,0)\). The chain with \(m\) edges has deficit \(m-1\), so its normalized deficit is maximized at \(m=n-1\).

For an individual generator, every tuple drawn from the spectra of its successive block restrictions must attain scalar chain triangle equality. Indeed, in finite dimension a tuple of eigenvalues is realized by choosing a unit eigenvector in each block. For an arbitrary bounded restriction, an open neighborhood of any of its spectral values has a nonzero spectral projection. Choose unit vectors in these spectral ranges and use continuity and nonnegativity of the defect. If the tuple had positive defect, sufficiently small neighborhoods would give positive expected defect, contradicting (2) at \(\varepsilon=0\). At \(p=1\), equality in the scalar triangle inequality along the real line is equivalent to monotonicity of the tuple, in either direction. If \(0<p<1\), the presence of two nonzero successive distances would make the sum of their \(p\)th powers strictly greater than the \(p\)th power of the sum of distances, hence greater than the endpoint distance to that power. Equality is then equivalent to at most one adjacent change.

For \(0<p<1\), this scalar condition gives the following explicit list of generator restrictions along a full tight chain.

- If all blocks are scalar, their values are constant or make one change.
- If one block is nonscalar and is an endpoint, the other blocks share a common scalar. The endpoint's spectrum is unrestricted by chain equality.
- If one block is nonscalar and is internal, all blocks to its left share a scalar value \(u\) and all to its right share a scalar value \(v\ne u\). The nonscalar block has spectrum contained in \(\{u,v\}\).

To exclude two nonscalar blocks, select any third block. If it lies between them, choose both nonscalar values different from the chosen third value. The resulting triple has two changes. If the third is outside them, choose the nearer nonscalar value different from the third value and the farther one different from the nearer value. Again there are two changes. A change in a scalar run to either side of a nonscalar block could similarly be supplemented by a change at that block. This forces each scalar run to be constant. An internal nonscalar block must then choose between the two distinct neighboring constants, since any other value gives two changes. Conversely each listed generator form satisfies scalar chain equality. The list describes the constraint on individual generators. The cross-level values and same-level norm restrictions of Theorem 2 apply to their summed cost.

On degenerate spectra the freedom on the same-level tensor blocks can occur. For instance, take \(n=3\), \(a_{12}=a_{23}=1\), \(a_{13}=4\), so \(\delta=2/3\) and \(\gamma_1=\gamma_2=5/3\). Choose a nonzero proper subprojection \(Q\) of \(P_2\). For \(0<t\le\delta/2\), reduce each nested cut weight by \(t\) and add
\[
 t G_p(P_3+Q)+t G_p(P_3+P_2-Q).
\]
These two projections contribute \(t\) to each adjacent block cost and \(2t\) to the closing cost, restoring exactly the cross-level values. The additional cost on \(P_2\otimes P_2^{\mathsf T}\) has eigenvalues zero or \(2t\), and the other same-level costs are zero. The resulting cost is optimal and differs from the nested one.

# 5. Pinned comparison and scope

The pinned polygon manuscript proves the exact norm distance and gives a commuting optimizer built from nested cuts. Its lower-bound proof uses the scalar metric triangle inequality for independent spectral samples. This supplement uses the slack of the same inequality to control the cross spectral measure of every generator, with explicit bounds in the excess norm error. It then determines the exact cross-level costs and generator restrictions under a full tight chain. The approximation class throughout is finite sums of bounded generators, and the comparison is to the pinned repository version.
