# Exact charging power from separable initial states in a complete-graph quantum battery

*A finite-size resolution of the uncorrelated-initialization question*

AI-assisted research manuscript

24 November 2026 · Research draft v1

## Abstract

We determine the largest instantaneous charging power obtainable from initially separable battery–auxiliary states in the complete-graph noncommuting battery of Mathew [1]. The battery starts entirely in its ground-energy eigenspace; its internal state and the auxiliary internal state are otherwise unrestricted. Write the number of graph vertices as $M=2m$, and put $q=\lfloor m/2\rfloor$. Let $\mathcal S_-$ denote the set of these separable ground-sector initial states. For the Hamiltonians as defined in [1], we prove the exact formula

$$
\max_{\rho_0\in\mathcal S_-}\ \max_{t\geq0}|P_{\rho_0}(t)|
=2\sqrt2\left(1+\frac{2q}{m}\right).
\tag{1}
$$

An explicit product state attains this bound, including within a prescribed auxiliary fermion-parity sector. The unrestricted joint ground-sector benchmark is $2\sqrt2\,m$. Thus, for $m\geq3$, initial battery–auxiliary entanglement is necessary for that benchmark, and classical initial correlations cannot replace it. After the source paper's energy-norm normalization, the separable optimum scales as $N^{3/2}$ rather than the unrestricted ground-sector $N^2$, where $N=m(2m-1)$ is the number of battery spins. The proof reduces the transition probability to an inner product of two real antisymmetric correlation matrices. Their paired singular values produce both the sharp bound and its odd–even correction. This is a model-specific result, not a general necessity theorem for entanglement in quantum batteries.

## 1. Question, scope, and contribution

Mathew [1] constructs a noncommuting spin battery with an auxiliary-assisted charger. Its maximal commutator norm is dynamically attainable by a suitable joint ground-sector state. The conclusion explicitly leaves open whether the same enhancement is attainable from an initially uncorrelated battery–auxiliary state. We answer that question for the same Hamiltonians and ground-sector preparation, and solve the larger optimization over initially separable states.

“Uncorrelated” means a tensor-product density operator $\sigma_B\otimes\eta_A$. “Separable” also permits convex mixtures $\sum_\ell w_\ell\sigma_{B,\ell}\otimes\eta_{A,\ell}$, including classical correlations. Neither restriction prohibits correlations or entanglement among the battery spins themselves, or within the auxiliary subsystem. The restriction applies only at the initial time. Entanglement generated by the charging dynamics is fully allowed.

Our contributions are the exact finite-size separable optimum, a saturating product-state construction compatible with auxiliary parity, and the resulting capacity-fraction and power-scaling consequences. The underlying model, its unrestricted benchmark, Clifford algebra, and correlation-matrix contraction bounds are existing ingredients. We give self-contained proofs of the algebra and inequalities needed here. Related quantum-battery work [2–4] studies different Hamiltonians or different resource partitions; in particular, charging advantage does not universally require entanglement [4].

The result does not address arbitrary noncommuting batteries, finite-temperature preparation, perturbed Hamiltonians, open-system dynamics, a different battery–auxiliary split, or the separate open question of the tightest general battery-locality bound. No global priority claim or external peer-review claim is made.

## 2. Model and precise theorem

### 2.1. Original spin and auxiliary operators

Fix an integer $m\geq1$ and set $M=2m$. A spin-$1/2$ resides on every edge of $K_M$, so

$$
N=\binom{2m}{2}=m(2m-1).
$$

With $X_e,Y_e,Z_e$ denoting Pauli matrices, define the noncommuting star operators

$$
P_a=\left(\prod_{b<a}Z_{e_{ab}}\right)
     \left(\prod_{b>a}X_{e_{ab}}\right),
\qquad H_B=\sum_{a=1}^{2m}P_a.
\tag{2}
$$

Each pair of distinct stars shares exactly one edge carrying $X$ in one star and $Z$ in the other. Consequently $P_a^2=I$ and $\{P_a,P_b\}=0$ for $a\ne b$.

Let $Y_r=Y_{e_{2r-1,2r}}$ for $1\leq r\leq m$. On a separate auxiliary Hilbert space take Hermitian Majorana operators $\chi,\gamma_1,\ldots,\gamma_m$ obeying the canonical anticommutation relations. Define

$$
Q_r=i\chi\gamma_r,\qquad
H_C=\sum_{r=1}^m Y_r\otimes Q_r.
\tag{3}
$$

The $Q_r$ are Hermitian involutions with $\{Q_r,Q_s\}=2\delta_{rs}I$. They are fermion-parity even. The auxiliary realization is a usual full fermionic Fock representation, with one unused Majorana added when necessary to make the number of physical Majoranas even. The theorem remains valid after restricting to either total auxiliary parity sector. Section 5 supplies the explicit realization and preparation.

The battery and auxiliary operators commute with one another. Since distinct $Y_r$ commute,

$$
H_B^2=2mI,\qquad H_C^2=mI.
\tag{4}
$$

Write

$$
G=\frac{H_B}{\sqrt{2m}},\qquad
\Pi_\pm=\frac{I\pm G}{2}.
$$

Thus $\Pi_-$ is the battery ground-energy projector. In tensor expressions we suppress $\otimes I_A$ on battery projectors. The initial state is a density operator satisfying $\rho_0=\Pi_-\rho_0\Pi_-$. The direct-charging evolution and power, with $\hbar=1$, are

$$
\rho(t)=e^{-itH_C}\rho_0 e^{itH_C},\qquad
P_{\rho_0}(t)=\frac{d}{dt}\operatorname{Tr}(H_B\rho(t)).
\tag{5}
$$

### 2.2. Main theorem

**Theorem 1 (sharp separable initialization bound).** Put

$$
q=\left\lfloor\frac m2\right\rfloor,
\qquad a_m=1+\frac{2q}{m}.
\tag{6}
$$

For the model (2)–(5), every separable initial state supported in the battery ground-energy sector satisfies

$$
|P_{\rho_0}(t)|\leq 2\sqrt2\,a_m\,
|\sin(2\sqrt m\,t)|.
\tag{7}
$$

There is a product initial state in the same ground sector attaining equality for every $t$. Its auxiliary factor can have either prescribed total fermion parity. In particular,

$$
P_{\mathrm{sep}}^{\max}=
\begin{cases}
4\sqrt2,&m\text{ even},\\
4\sqrt2-2\sqrt2/m,&m\text{ odd}.
\end{cases}
\tag{8}
$$

The maximum over all joint ground-sector initial states is

$$
P_{\mathrm{joint}}^{\max}=2\sqrt2\,m.
\tag{9}
$$

Both maxima occur at $t_* =\pi/(4\sqrt m)$, as well as at subsequent absolute-power peaks. The odd formula includes $m=1$. For $m=1,2$ the product and joint optima coincide; for every $m\geq3$ they differ strictly.

## 3. Algebraic reduction to one transition weight

### 3.1. Two Clifford families on the battery

Set

$$
B_r=\frac{P_{2r-1}+P_{2r}}{\sqrt2},
\qquad R_r=iB_rY_r.
\tag{10}
$$

The matching-edge operator $Y_r$ anticommutes with $B_r$ and commutes with $B_s$ for $s\ne r$. Direct multiplication gives

$$
\{B_r,B_s\}=2\delta_{rs}I,\quad
\{R_r,R_s\}=2\delta_{rs}I,\quad
\{B_r,R_s\}=0.
\tag{11}
$$

In particular, all $B_r,R_r$ are Hermitian involutions; they form $2m$ Clifford generators. Also

$$
Y_r=-iB_rR_r,\qquad
G=\frac1{\sqrt m}\sum_{r=1}^m B_r,\qquad
\{G,R_r\}=0.
\tag{12}
$$

For example, $B_rR_r=iY_r$, while $R_rB_r=-iY_r$; for different indices, the sole sign change comes from interchanging the two $B$ operators. These observations verify every cross relation in (11).

Define

$$
K=\sum_{r=1}^m R_r\otimes Q_r,
\qquad T=\Pi_+H_C\Pi_-.
\tag{13}
$$

From $[G,Y_r]=-2iR_r/\sqrt m$ and $\{G,R_r\}=0$,

$$
T=-\frac i{\sqrt m}K\Pi_-,\qquad
T^\dagger T=\frac1m\Pi_-K^2\Pi_-.
\tag{14}
$$

The projectors in the second identity are essential on the full Hilbert space. Only after restricting the domain to the ground sector can it be abbreviated as $K^2/m$. Each $R_r\otimes Q_r$ commutes with every other such product, and $K^2$ commutes with $G$.

### 3.2. Exact evolution for every ground-sector state

The pure-state version of this time dependence is already given in [1], Supplementary Material, equations (111)–(113), with $k=mw_g$. We restate it and extend it linearly to mixed states; the new optimization is the sharp separable bound on $k$ proved below.

By (4),

$$
e^{-itH_C}=\cos(\sqrt m\,t)I
-\frac{i\sin(\sqrt m\,t)}{\sqrt m}H_C.
\tag{15}
$$

Define the nonnegative transition weight

$$
k(\rho_0)=\operatorname{Tr}(\rho_0T^\dagger T).
\tag{16}
$$

Applying $\Pi_+$ on the left of (15) to an initial ground-sector state removes the identity term. Hence the excited-energy probability is exactly

$$
p_+(t)=\frac{k(\rho_0)}m\sin^2(\sqrt m\,t).
\tag{17}
$$

There are only two battery energies, $\pm\sqrt{2m}$. Therefore, for pure or mixed $\rho_0$,

$$
\begin{aligned}
E_B(t)&=-\sqrt{2m}
+2\sqrt{2m}\,\frac{k(\rho_0)}m\sin^2(\sqrt m\,t),\\
P_{\rho_0}(t)&=2\sqrt2\,k(\rho_0)\sin(2\sqrt m\,t).
\end{aligned}
\tag{18}
$$

Thus the entire state optimization is the optimization of one time-independent scalar $k$. No assumption that the state remains separable during charging has been made.

## 4. Sharp upper bound for separable preparation

### 4.1. Correlation matrices of an arbitrary Clifford family

For Hermitian operators $X_1,\ldots,X_m$ satisfying $\{X_r,X_s\}=2\delta_{rs}I$ and any density operator $\omega$, define

$$
\Gamma_{rs}=\frac i2\operatorname{Tr}\bigl(\omega[X_r,X_s]\bigr).
\tag{19}
$$

This is a real antisymmetric matrix. For $r\ne s$, $\Gamma_{rs}=i\operatorname{Tr}(\omega X_rX_s)$, and its diagonal is zero. We use “correlation matrix” rather than assuming a Gaussian state or subtracting first moments.

The block structure and contraction condition are standard in fermionic correlation-matrix theory; see, for example, Bravyi [5], Section IV. The argument below proves the needed bound for arbitrary states and for odd or even $m$, without using Gaussian-state reconstruction or Wick's rule.

Here $\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}$ is the largest singular value, $\|A\|_F^2=\sum_{r,s}|A_{rs}|^2$, and for real matrices $\langle A,B\rangle_F=\sum_{r,s}A_{rs}B_{rs}$.

**Lemma 2.** Every matrix (19) satisfies

$$
\|\Gamma\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq1,
\qquad \|\Gamma\|_F^2\leq2\lfloor m/2\rfloor.
\tag{20}
$$

**Proof.** The Gram matrix $A_{rs}=\operatorname{Tr}(\omega X_rX_s)=\delta_{rs}-i\Gamma_{rs}$ is positive semidefinite: for $z\in\mathbb C^m$,

$$
z^*Az=\operatorname{Tr}\left[\omega
\left(\sum_r z_rX_r\right)^\dagger
\left(\sum_s z_sX_s\right)\right]\geq0.
$$

Its entrywise conjugate $I+i\Gamma$ is also positive semidefinite. Thus the Hermitian matrix $i\Gamma$ has spectrum in $[-1,1]$. A real antisymmetric matrix has an orthogonal block form comprising blocks $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&s_j\\-s_j&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ and, if needed, a zero block. There are at most $q=\lfloor m/2\rfloor$ nonzero blocks, and $|s_j|\leq1$. Hence $\|\Gamma\|_F^2=2\sum_j s_j^2\leq2q$. This standard correlation-matrix argument does not require Gaussianity. $\square$

### 4.2. Product states

Take $\rho_0=\sigma_B\otimes\eta_A$ with $\sigma_B$ supported in $\Pi_-$. Let $\Gamma^B$ be (19) for the $R_r$, and $\Gamma^A$ the corresponding matrix for the $Q_r$. Expanding (14) gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
k(\sigma_B\otimes\eta_A)
&=\frac1m\operatorname{Tr}\bigl((\sigma_B\otimes\eta_A)K^2\bigr)\\
&=1-\frac2m\sum_{r<s}\Gamma^B_{rs}\Gamma^A_{rs}\\
&=1-\frac1m\langle\Gamma^B,\Gamma^A\rangle_F.
\end{aligned}
\tag{21}
$$

The minus sign follows from
$R_rR_s=-i(iR_rR_s)$ and $Q_rQ_s=-i(iQ_rQ_s)$.
By Cauchy–Schwarz and Lemma 2,

$$
k\leq1+\frac{\|\Gamma^B\|_F\|\Gamma^A\|_F}{m}
\leq1+\frac{2q}{m}=a_m.
\tag{22}
$$

### 4.3. Separable mixtures

Suppose $\rho_0=\sum_\ell w_\ell\sigma_{B,\ell}\otimes\eta_{A,\ell}$ is separable, with $w_\ell>0$, and $\rho_0$ has ground-sector support. Then

$$
0=\operatorname{Tr}(\rho_0\Pi_+)
=\sum_\ell w_\ell\operatorname{Tr}(\sigma_{B,\ell}\Pi_+).
$$

Every summand is nonnegative; therefore each battery factor is itself supported in $\Pi_-$. For a positive matrix, zero expectation of a projector implies zero support on its range, as follows from $\|\sigma^{1/2}\Pi_+\|_F^2=0$. Applying (22) to each factor and using linearity of $k$ proves $k(\rho_0)\leq a_m$. Together with (18), this proves the upper bound in Theorem 1.

## 5. Saturation, physical representation, and auxiliary parity

The upper bound would not settle the optimization without a state in the actual model attaining it. We now construct such a state.

### 5.1. Battery preparation

For $1\leq j\leq q$, set

$$
L_j^B=iR_{2j-1}R_{2j}.
$$

These are commuting Hermitian involutions and commute with $G$. Fix arbitrary signs $\varepsilon_j\in\{\pm1\}$ and consider

$$
E_B=\Pi_-\prod_{j=1}^q\frac{I+\varepsilon_jL_j^B}{2}.
\tag{23}
$$

This is a nonzero orthogonal projector in the original edge-spin representation. To verify nonzeroness, note the following elementary Clifford trace fact. Every nonempty product of distinct generators among the $2m$ operators $(B_1,\ldots,B_m,R_1,\ldots,R_m)$ has zero trace. For an even-length product, conjugation by a participating generator changes its sign. For an odd-length product, an unused generator does so; such a generator exists because $2m$ is even. Trace is invariant under conjugation.

Expanding (23), its only term with nonzero trace is the identity term. Terms involving $G$ expand into nonempty products containing one $B_r$ and an even number of distinct $R_s$. Consequently,

$$
\operatorname{Tr}E_B=\frac{2^N}{2^{q+1}}>0.
\tag{24}
$$

Choose any density operator supported on this projector, for example $\sigma_B=E_B/\operatorname{Tr}E_B$. It has

$$
\Gamma^B_{2j-1,2j}=\varepsilon_j.
$$

Every other off-diagonal entry is zero. Indeed, the corresponding bilinear anticommutes with at least one fixed $L_j^B$, so its expectation in this eigenspace vanishes. At most one $R_r$ is unpaired, leaving no exceptional unpaired–unpaired off-diagonal entry.

### 5.2. Auxiliary preparation with fixed total parity

The auxiliary bilinears obey

$$
L_j^A=iQ_{2j-1}Q_{2j}=i\gamma_{2j-1}\gamma_{2j}.
\tag{25}
$$

There are $q$ disjoint pairs among the $\gamma_r$. If $m$ is odd, the unused pair is $(\chi,\gamma_m)$. If $m$ is even, introduce an unused Majorana $\gamma_0$ and take the unused pair to be $(\chi,\gamma_0)$. In both cases the auxiliary system has $2q+2$ physical Majoranas, or $q+1$ fermionic modes. The extra operator never appears in $H_C$.

Prepare the $q$ bilinears in (25) with eigenvalues $-\varepsilon_j$. The eigenvalue of the unused-pair bilinear can be chosen to make the total fermion parity either specified value. All $q+1$ bilinears commute, and their joint eigenspace is one-dimensional in the full Fock representation. Let $\eta_A$ be the resulting pure-state density operator. Then

$$
\Gamma^A=-\Gamma^B,\qquad
\|\Gamma^A\|_F^2=\|\Gamma^B\|_F^2=2q.
\tag{26}
$$

As on the battery, all unpaired off-block entries vanish by anticommutation with a fixed pair bilinear. These preparations commute with total auxiliary parity, so saturation does not exploit parity-forbidden superpositions.

### 5.3. Attainment

For $\rho_0=\sigma_B\otimes\eta_A$, equations (21) and (26) give $k=a_m$. Equation (18) now proves equality in (7) for every time. Pure battery vectors in the range of (23) work as well, so a pure product state also suffices.

## 6. Unrestricted joint benchmark and physical consequences

### 6.1. Recovery of the known joint benchmark

Since $\|H_C\|=\sqrt m$, the transition weight satisfies $k\leq m$. For completeness, the same algebra constructs a state achieving equality. The $m$ involutions $F_r=R_r\otimes Q_r$ commute. Their common $+1$ eigenspace is nonzero: expanding $\prod_r(I+F_r)/2$, every nonidentity term has zero battery trace by the Clifford trace fact, even after restricting the auxiliary space to fixed parity.

Choose a normalized vector $v$ in that eigenspace. Since $G$ anticommutes with every $F_r$, $Gv$ belongs to the common $-1$ eigenspace and is orthogonal to $v$. Therefore

$$
g=\frac{v-Gv}{\sqrt2}
$$

is a normalized battery-ground-sector vector with $K^2g=m^2g$. By (14), $k(|g\rangle\langle g|)=m$. This recovers (9), already established for the source model in [1], and confirms compatibility with fixed auxiliary parity.

### 6.2. Chargeable fraction and entanglement witness

Define stored energy by $\Delta E_B(t)=E_B(t)-E_B(0)$, and spectral capacity by $2\sqrt{2m}$. Equation (17) gives the exact maximal capacity fraction accessible from separable ground-sector preparation:

$$
f_{\mathrm{sep}}^{\max}
=\frac{\max\Delta E_B}{2\sqrt{2m}}
=\frac{a_m}{m}
=\frac{m+2\lfloor m/2\rfloor}{m^2}.
\tag{27}
$$

The energy maximum occurs at $t_E=\pi/(2\sqrt m)$; this is distinct from the first power peak $t_*=\pi/(4\sqrt m)$. Equation (27) is stored energy divided by spectral capacity, not ergotropy, thermodynamic efficiency, or an experimentally measured yield. The same number equals $P_{\mathrm{sep}}^{\max}/P_{\mathrm{joint}}^{\max}$. It is one at $m=1,2$ and asymptotic to $2/m$.

For $m\geq3$, observation of power exceeding (7), under the stated Hamiltonian and ground-sector assumptions, certifies that the initial state was entangled across $B:A$. Failure to exceed the bound does not certify separability. This witness concerns initialization; the subsequent evolution is allowed to generate entanglement even from a product state.

### 6.3. Resource normalization and scaling

Under the unscaled local-energy convention of (2)–(3), the source model's commuting-battery comparison value is $4\sqrt m$ [1]. Our separable bound obeys $2\sqrt2\,a_m\leq4\sqrt m$ for all $m$, with equality only at $m=2$. Thus separable initialization cannot reproduce the source model's parametrically growing violation of that comparison value.

Now apply the source paper's separate norm rescalings to the noncommuting model:

$$
\widetilde H_B=\frac{N}{\sqrt{2m}}H_B,
\qquad \widetilde H_C=\frac{N}{\sqrt m}H_C.
\tag{28}
$$

Both norms become $N$. Eigenvectors and the allowed initial-state set remain unchanged; the oscillation frequency changes from $\sqrt m$ to $N$. From (18),

$$
\widetilde P_{\mathrm{sep}}^{\max}
=\frac{2N^2}{m}a_m,
\qquad
\widetilde P_{\mathrm{joint}}^{\max}=2N^2.
\tag{29}
$$

Since $N=m(2m-1)$ and $a_m\to2$,

$$
\widetilde P_{\mathrm{sep}}^{\max}
\sim4\sqrt2\,N^{3/2},
\qquad
\widetilde P_{\mathrm{joint}}^{\max}=2N^2.
\tag{30}
$$

Adding the $O(m)$ auxiliary degrees of freedom does not change these exponents. The conclusion is the loss of the growing enhancement and of the quadratic exponent under this specified normalization; it is not the absence of every possible constant-factor advantage under other comparisons.

## 7. Verification and evidentiary boundaries

The proof above is the all-$m$ argument. The accompanying code checks exact Clifford identities in finite instances and independently constructs matrices for the original edge-spin Hamiltonians at small $M$, together with larger reduced Clifford realizations.
