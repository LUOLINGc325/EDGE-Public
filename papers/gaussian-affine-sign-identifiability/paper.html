Gaussian scale calibration with finite affine-sign and offset budgets

Abstract

A homogeneous sign identifies a half-space containing a Gaussian observation and is invariant to simultaneous rescaling of the observation and its mean. Affine signs can identify absolute covariance scale, even when their total offset is small. We determine the minimax squared covariance error when each observation may give at most KK simultaneous signs with total absolute offset at most B≤1B\le1. For K≥2K\ge2, the error is of order dmin{1,(nB)−2+(nBmin(d,K))−1}d\min\{1,(nB)^{-2}+(nB\sqrt{\min(d,K)})^{-1}\}. For K=1K=1, it is of order dmin{1,(nB2)−1}d\min\{1,(nB^2)^{-1}\}. These rates hold with an unknown location whose standardized coordinates are bounded. The centered Fisher information is of order Bmin(d,K)B\sqrt{\min(d,K)} for K≥2K\ge2 and B2B^2 for K=1K=1. The isotropic upper bounds use a fixed schedule of arrays. The main array uses an affine comparison and a count of homogeneous comparisons involving the same coordinate. An order-statistic calibration keeps the error from estimating the other coordinate means within the minimax rate. For arbitrary centered Gaussian covariance with spectrum in a fixed positive interval, the K≥2K\ge2 risk is the sum of the sharp scalar calibration rate and the shape cost d4/(nmin{K,d}2)d^4/(n\min\{K,d\}^2), capped at order dd. The K=1K=1 full-covariance rate is of order min{d,d4/(nB2)}\min\{d,d^4/(nB^2)\}. For two or more comparisons, homogeneous signs first estimate shape and the offset comparisons then estimate scale with a bound robust to shape error. The one-comparison design uses nonzero offsets to estimate covariance directly. If the location is unrestricted in the isotropic model, every finite protocol has minimax error of order dd, even with arbitrarily many finite comparisons per sample.

Reading guide

A homogeneous comparison only reports which side of a hyperplane through the origin contains the sample. Such comparisons can reveal covariance shape because the scale cancels. An affine comparison moves the hyperplane and can also reveal scale. The two budgets limit the total offset and the number of comparisons on each sample.

Sections 1–8 treat scale when the covariance is isotropic, including the effect of an unknown mean. Sections 9 and 10 treat centered Gaussian vectors with a general covariance in a prescribed spectral range. They show how the cost of learning shape and the cost of calibrating scale combine, including the separate regime with one comparison per sample.
