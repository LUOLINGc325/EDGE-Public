# Log-free sparse goodness-of-fit by one augmented binary classifier

## Abstract

Gerber, Han and Polyanskiy leave open the removal of extra logarithmic factors from classifier-accuracy testing. We treat known-reference goodness-of-fit in their sparse discrete regime, allowing an independent randomized augmentation of every example. Split each symbol into reference-dependent copies, then add independent Poisson noise to equalize the training counts under the null. The occupied augmented symbols form a single binary classifier. We prove high-confidence separation and a small reference mass, yielding the optimal-order sample size $\sqrt{k\log(1/\delta)}/\epsilon^2$ without a logarithm of the alphabet size. The classifier is hard on the augmented alphabet. A hard classifier fixed on the original alphabet, with no fresh augmentation at prediction, is a distinct restriction.

## 1. Problem and claim

Let $p,q$ be distributions on $[k]$, with $q$ known. For $0<\epsilon\le1$ test $p=q$ against $\operatorname{TV}(p,q)\ge\epsilon$. Each original observation may be preprocessed using randomness independent between observations. Training selects one set of augmented symbols, and the test uses the difference of empirical fractions of the two independent holdout sets labeled one, one from $p$ and the other simulated from $q$.

**Theorem.** For every integer $1\le n\le k$, the algorithm below uses $3n$ original observations from $p$ and $n$ simulated holdout observations from $q$. The maximum of its two error probabilities is at most
$$5\exp\{-n^2\epsilon^4/(92160 k)\}.$$
Consequently, whenever $\lceil\sqrt{92160 k\log(5/\delta)}/\epsilon^2\rceil\le k$, the error is at most $\delta$ with $O(\sqrt{k\log(1/\delta)}/\epsilon^2)$ observations. For small separation in the sparse regime, this matches the worst-reference minimax order from Gerber, Han and Polyanskiy. The point is the single classifier construction.

## 2. Augmentation, training and decision rule

For symbol $i$ let $r_i=\max(1,\lceil kq_i\rceil)$. Replace that symbol in each example by a uniformly sampled pair $(i,j)$, $1\le j\le r_i$. The augmented laws $p',q'$ have masses $p_i/r_i,q_i/r_i$ at each such pair. The new alphabet size $d=\sum_i r_i$ is at most $2k$, $q'_j\le1/k$ throughout, and
$$\operatorname{TV}(p',q')=\operatorname{TV}(p,q).$$
Here and below a single subscript $j$ can index an augmented symbol.

Reserve $2n$ observations for training and $n$ from $p$ for testing. Draw $L\sim\operatorname{Pois}(n)$ independently and accept the null if $L>2n$. Otherwise use the first $L$ training observations and augment each as above. To the count of each augmented symbol $j$, add an independent synthetic count of mean $n(1/k-q'_j)$, distributed as a Poisson variable. Let $S$ be the augmented symbols with positive resulting count. If $|S|>4n$, accept the null. For the two independent augmented holdouts let $\widehat p(S)$ and $\widehat q(S)$ be their fractions in $S$. Reject exactly when
$$|\widehat p(S)-\widehat q(S)|\ge\eta,\qquad \eta=\frac{n\epsilon^2}{48 k}. \tag{1}$$

The synthetic counts depend only on the known null law. They are generated by the algorithm and are not observations. On a given augmented example the fitted rule is the deterministic binary classifier $\mathbf1_S$. On an original example, a fresh independent clone label implements its randomized prediction.

## 3. Training separation

For analysis, an untruncated Poisson training length on an infinite sequence can be coupled with the actual procedure: they agree whenever $L\le2n$. In the untruncated model the padded symbol counts are independent Poisson variables of mean
$$\lambda+y_j,\qquad
\lambda=n/k\le1,\quad y_j=n(p'_j-q'_j),\quad y_j\ge-\lambda,\quad \sum_j y_j=0.$$
Let $B_j$ be their occupancy indicators, $T=\sum_j y_j B_j$, and $\mu=E T$. Since the mean occupancy probability equals $1-e^{-\lambda-y_j}$, cancellation gives
$$
\mu=\sum_j \mu_j,\qquad
\mu_j=e^{-\lambda}y_j(1-e^{-y_j})
\ge \tfrac16\min\{y_j^2,|y_j|\}. \tag{2}
$$
For $y\in[0,1]$, use the chord lower bound $1-e^{-y}\ge(1-e^{-1})y$. For positive $y\ge1$ the factor $1-e^{-y}$ is at least $1-e^{-1}$. For negative $y$ the bound follows from $e^{|y|}-1\ge|y|$ and $|y|\le1$. Also $e^{-\lambda}\ge1/3$ and $1-e^{-1}\ge1/2$.

If $\operatorname{TV}(p,q)\ge\epsilon$, then $\sum_j|y_j|\ge2n\epsilon$. At least half this mass is in the terms with $|y_j|\le1$, or at least half is in the other terms. In the former case Cauchy--Schwarz gives $\sum_{|y_j|\le1} y_j^2\ge n^2\epsilon^2/d$. In the latter the linear terms contribute at least $n\epsilon$. Since $d\le2k$, $n\le k$ and $\epsilon\le1$, (2) gives
$$\mu\ge n^2\epsilon^2/(12k). \tag{3}$$

For every $0\le s\le1/2$, the lower-tail moment bound is
$$E\exp[-s(y_j B_j-E(y_jB_j))]\le\exp(6s^2\mu_j). \tag{4}$$
To prove it when $|y_j|\le1$, center the summand, whose absolute value is at most $|y_j|$. Use $e^t-1-t\le t^2 e^{|t|}/2$ to bound the expectation by $1+s^2 y_j^2$, then apply (2). The only remaining case is $y_j>1$. Write $D_j=1-B_j$, with mean $\pi_j=e^{-\lambda-y_j}\le e^{-y_j}$. The exponent on the left is $s y_j(D_j-\pi_j)$. For this centered Bernoulli, its moment is at most
$$
\exp[\pi_j(e^{s y_j}-1-s y_j)]
 \le\exp[\tfrac12 s^2 y_j^2 e^{-y_j/2}]
 \le\exp[\tfrac12 s^2 y_j]\le\exp(6s^2\mu_j),
$$
using $y e^{-y/2}\le 1$ and (2). Independence and Markov at $s=1/48$ then give
$$\Pr(T<\mu/2)\le\exp(-\mu/128).$$
Thus the separation of the single classifier on the augmented laws, $\Delta=p'(S)-q'(S)=T/n$, satisfies
$$\Pr\!\left(\Delta<\frac{n\epsilon^2}{24k}\right)
\le \exp[-n^2\epsilon^2/(1536k)] \tag{5}$$
in the alternative. The augmented reference mass is at most $|S|/k$. The sum of padded counts is Poisson with mean $d\lambda\le 2n$, so $|S|>4n$ has probability at most $\exp(-n/2)$ by a Markov bound at generating argument two. The original $L$-draw exceeds $2n$ with probability at most $\exp(-n/3)$ in the same way. These loose constants use $\log 2\ge2/3$.

## 4. Finite-sample holdout errors

Conditional on the fitted set and the training guards, both holdout fractions are Bernoulli averages. For Bernoulli mean $\theta$ and sample size $n$, Bernstein gives either one-sided deviation at level $h$ bounded by $\exp[-n h^2/(2(\theta+h/3))]$.

Under the null their means coincide and are at most $4n/k$. By splitting each tail of their difference at $\eta/2$, its absolute value exceeds or equals $\eta$ with probability at most
$$4\exp[-n\eta^2/(8(4n/k+\eta/6))]
\le4\exp[-n^2\epsilon^4/(92160k)].$$
The training guards always accept the null, so this also bounds the unconditional type-I error.

Under the alternative, on the good training event the means differ by $\Delta\ge2\eta>0$. If the test accepts, the empirical difference misses this positive separation by at least $\Delta/2$, so one of the two Bernoulli averages deviates adversely by at least $\Delta/4$. The larger mean is at most $q'(S)+\Delta$. Thus the conditional probability of accepting is at most
$$2\exp[-n\Delta^2/(32(q'(S)+(13/12)\Delta))].$$
The fraction $\Delta^2/(q'(S)+(13/12)\Delta)$ is increasing for positive $\Delta$. Substitute $\Delta=2\eta$ and $q'(S)\le4n/k$ to upper bound this by $2\exp[-n^2\epsilon^4/(92160 k)]$. Add the three training failure probabilities from (5), the count guard and the Poisson length guard. Each is at most $\exp[-n^2\epsilon^4/(92160 k)]$ since $n\le k$ and $\epsilon\le1$. This proves the theorem. $\square$

## 5. Relation to the open problem

The source's sparse GoF result in Proposition 12 of the proceedings (Proposition 5 in the author's version) uses $O(\log k)$ groups of unequal reference masses and chooses a held-out separating set from them. Symbol splitting is a standard distribution-testing tool. Here, known-reference synthetic counts equalize null occupancy on the flattened alphabet. Consequently the expectation in (2) sums contributions from all levels together, with one classifier, and a one-sided exponential moment pays for the rare errors on heavy alternative symbols.

The additional independent label per example requires no new draw from $p$ or $q$ and preserves total variation exactly. It does broaden the original-alphabet hypothesis class of fitted classifiers: on the original symbol the prediction can be randomized afresh. The result proves optimal order for the sparse known-reference setting with this preprocessing. It leaves the fixed hard classifier on the unaugmented alphabet and the unknown-reference testing problems open.
