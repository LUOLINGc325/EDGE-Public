Sharp Monotone Support Paths in Finite Moment Optimization

26 November 2026

Abstract

Consider a probability distribution on an ordered finite set, and maximize its right endpoint mass subject to prescribed generalized moments. We study the optimizer when the reference distribution defining those moments varies in strict monotone likelihood-ratio order. For a moment matrix with a constant row and strictly positive ordered maximal minors, we prove that every admissible paired basis has an interval of feasibility, that optimizing pair indices move coordinatewise to the right, and that a suffix-block pivot gives the successor at every simultaneous or degenerate transition. With n+1n+1 grid points and kk nonconstant moment constraints, the number of positive-length regimes is at most ⌊k/2⌋(n−k)+1\lfloor k/2\rfloor(n-k)+1. This bound is attained for every 1≤k<n1\leq k\mathrel{<}n within the class of ordinary integer-grid moments. A complementary-grid inequality strengthens the pivot rule for binomial-normalized log-convex carriers: a departing pair cannot have an adjacent pair on its right. For binomial reference laws this proves the interior support-order and unit-pivot assertions in the nonboundary range of recent conjectures on kk-wise independent bits, while identifying the necessary endpoint and dimension qualifications. The resulting complete binomial phase diagram has polynomial algebraic output and bit complexity in the expanded grid dimensions. A positive, strictly stochastically increasing family on four points can instead have arbitrarily many support changes. Exact finite certificates supplement the proofs.

1. Problem and contribution

Sharp probability bounds with partial moment information are classical linear programs. Their paired extremal supports, interpolation formulas, and generalized-convex dual certificates are established components of discrete moment theory [P90, CD08, GP09, NP13]. The question here concerns the whole path of the optimizer as the moment vector changes. A fixed-input solution method does not by itself bound the number of bases encountered along a parameter curve.

A concrete motivation comes from the maximum probability that nn bits are all one, when any kk of them are independent and each bit has mean pp. Berend, Ernst, Kontorovich, and Kumar [BEKK26] give exact formulas and numerical support-path conjectures. Hermann [H26] proves a reflection relation and explicitly distinguishes endpoint degeneracy from interior feasibility. We retain those distinctions. In particular, we do not claim that a basis is feasible on a connected subset of the entire closed interval [0,1][0,1]; that statement is false.

The contribution is a full strict-MLR path theorem for the classical sign-consistent moment configuration, including its degenerate successor and a sharp regime count. The second structural ingredient is a positivity inequality that forbids block pivots for binomial-normalized log-convex carriers. Together these give a complete algebraic sweep for the binomial problem. The static paired-support pattern, the matrix class itself, interpolation, reflection, and the standard real-algebraic subroutines are not new claims. The proofs below separate these inputs from the path arguments.

2. Ordered moment configurations

2.1. Definitions

Fix integers 1≤k<n1\leq k\mathrel{<}n. Let AA be a real (k+1)×(n+1)(k+1)\times(n+1) matrix with ordered columns A0,…,AnA_0,\ldots,A_n. Assume its first row is all ones and

det(Ai0,…,Aik)>0whenever 0≤i0<⋯<ik≤n. \det(A_{i_0},\ldots,A_{i_k})>0 \quad\text{whenever }0\leq i_0\mathrel{<}\cdots\mathrel{<}i_k\leq n. \tag{2.1}

A common negative sign can be made positive by reversing one nonconstant row. The assumption concerns maximal minors only; positivity of all lower-order minors is unnecessary. Matrices with this sign consistency and their relation to alternating configurations are classical [Pe95, GP09]. The polynomial moment matrix Ari=xirA_{ri}=x_i^r on any strictly increasing real grid satisfies (2.1) by the Vandermonde formula.

For a positive probability vector μ=(μ0,…,μn)\mu=(\mu_0,\ldots,\mu_n), define

VA(μ)=max{qn:qi≥0,Aq=Aμ}. V_A(\mu)=\max\{q_n:q_i\geq0,\ Aq=A\mu\}. \tag{2.2}

The first row imposes ∑iqi=1\sum_iq_i=1. The feasible set is nonempty and compact. Its data are the generalized moments AμA\mu; no shape restriction is imposed on the competing law qq.

Put m=⌊k/2⌋m=\lfloor k/2\rfloor. An upper paired basis has the following form:

k=2m:B={a1,a1+1,…,am,am+1,n},0≤a1,ar+1≥ar+2,am≤n−2;k=2m+1:B={0,a1,a1+1,…,am,am+1,n},1≤a1,ar+1≥ar+2,am≤n−2. \begin{split} k=2m:&\quad B=\{a_1,a_1+1,\ldots,a_m,a_m+1,n\},\quad 0\leq a_1,\ a_{r+1}\geq a_r+2,\ a_m\leq n-2;\\ k=2m+1:&\quad B=\{0,a_1,a_1+1,\ldots,a_m,a_m+1,n\},\quad 1\leq a_1,\ a_{r+1}\geq a_r+2,\ a_m\leq n-2. \end{split} \tag{2.3}

When k=1k=1, the second line means B={0,n}B=\{0,n\}. The movable paired part is denoted JJ. Its available-node count is M=nM=n for even kk and M=n−1M=n-1 for odd kk, so

M−2m=n−k.M-2m=n-k. \tag{2.4}

For j∈Bj\in B define the cardinal coefficient and the interpolatory mass by

ℓB,j(i)=(AB−1Ai)j,βB,j(μ)=∑i=0nμiℓB,j(i). \ell_{B,j}(i)=(A_B^{-1}A_i)_j, \qquad \beta_{B,j}(\mu)=\sum_{i=0}^n\mu_i\ell_{B,j}(i). \tag{2.5}

The basis is feasible if all these masses are nonnegative, and nondegenerate if all are positive. The ordering of coordinates in ABA_B is the increasing ordering of BB.

2.2. Cardinal signs and a static certificate

Lemma 2.1 (cardinal signs). For an upper paired basis, a left pair node jj satisfies ℓB,j(i)≥0\ell_{B,j}(i)\geq0 for i≤ji\leq j and ℓB,j(i)≤0\ell_{B,j}(i)\leq0 for i>ji>j. For a right pair node, ℓB,j(i)≤0\ell_{B,j}(i)\leq0 for i<ji\mathrel{<}j and ℓB,j(i)≥0\ell_{B,j}(i)\geq0 for i≥ji\geq j. At every i∉Bi\notin B the relevant inequality is strict. Also ℓB,n\ell_{B,n} is nonnegative everywhere and strictly positive at nn and outside BB. For odd kk, the same endpoint assertion holds for ℓB,0\ell_{B,0}.

Proof. At basis columns the coefficients are Kronecker coordinates. Otherwise Cramer’s rule expresses ℓB,j(i)\ell_{B,j}(i) as the determinant obtained by replacing column jj by AiA_i, divided by the positive determinant of ABA_B. Sorting the replacement columns changes the sign once for each basis index strictly between ii and jj. An adjacent pair contributes an even number of crossings unless its partner lies between ii and jj. Counting these partners gives the two asserted single-change patterns. For the right endpoint, all intervening movable indices form complete pairs, hence the sign is positive. For the left endpoint in the odd case the same argument applies. No numerator vanishes outside BB, by (2.1). ▫\square

Lemma 2.2 (classical endpoint certificate). Every feasible upper paired basis represents the unique optimizer of (2.2). Its endpoint mass is

VA(μ)=βB,n(μ)=∑iμiℓB,n(i)>0.V_A(\mu)=\beta_{B,n}(\mu)=\sum_i\mu_i\ell_{B,n}(i)>0. \tag{2.6}

For odd kk, the mass at zero is positive as well.

Proof. Equation (2.5) gives ABβB=AμA_B\beta_B=A\mu, and the constant row gives ∑j∈BβB,j=1\sum_{j\in B}\beta_{B,j}=1. The function ℓB,n\ell_{B,n} belongs to the row span of AA and majorizes the indicator of index nn. Therefore, for every feasible qq,

qn≤∑iqiℓB,n(i)=∑iμiℓB,n(i)=βB,n.q_n\leq\sum_iq_i\ell_{B,n}(i)=\sum_i\mu_i\ell_{B,n}(i)=\beta_{B,n}.

The law on BB attains equality. The majorant has strict slack at every index outside BB, so any optimal law must be supported on BB. Invertibility of ABA_B then proves uniqueness. Endpoint positivity follows from Lemma 2.1 and the positivity of μ\mu. ▫\square

This lemma is a self-contained version of the familiar static dual argument. Existence of a feasible upper paired basis for every positive μ\mu will follow constructively from the path proof.

3. Strict likelihood-ratio paths

3.1. One crossing for each mass

Let II be a real interval, and let t↦μtt\mapsto\mu_t be continuous with every coordinate positive. It is strictly MLR increasing if, for every s<ts\mathrel{<}t in II,

i↦μt(i)μs(i)is strictly increasing.i\longmapsto \frac{\mu_t(i)}{\mu_s(i)}\quad\text{is strictly increasing}. \tag{3.1}

In particular, every family

μt(i)=wiethi∑r=0nwrethr,wi>0,h0<⋯<hn,t∈ℝ,\mu_t(i)=\frac{w_i e^{t h_i}}{\sum_{r=0}^n w_r e^{t h_r}},\qquad w_i>0,\quad h_0\mathrel{<}\cdots\mathrel{<}h_n,\quad t\in\mathbb R, \tag{3.2}

satisfies this condition. Smoothness is not assumed in (3.1).

Lemma 3.1 (oriented zero). For a fixed upper paired basis, if a left-node mass vanishes at t0t_0, it is positive at every earlier parameter and negative at every later parameter in II. A right-node mass has the opposite orientation. Consequently each movable mass has at most one zero.

Proof. Suppose βB,j(μt0)=0\beta_{B,j}(\mu_{t_0})=0 and t>t0t>t_0. With Ri=μt(i)/μt0(i)R_i=\mu_t(i)/\mu_{t_0}(i), subtracting RjR_j times the vanishing expectation gives

βB,j(μt)=∑iμt0(i)(Ri−Rj)ℓB,j(i). \beta_{B,j}(\mu_t)=\sum_i\mu_{t_0}(i)(R_i-R_j)\ell_{B,j}(i). \tag{3.3}

For a left node every summand is nonpositive, by Lemma 2.1. Every off-basis index contributes strictly negatively. There is such an index since k<nk\mathrel{<}n. Hence the sum is negative. A decreasing likelihood ratio gives positivity for t<t0t\mathrel{<}t_0. For a right node all signs reverse. Two different zeros are therefore impossible. ▫\square

Corollary 3.2 (interval feasibility). For each upper paired basis, the set

{t∈I:βB,j(μt)≥0 for all j∈B}\{t\in I:\beta_{B,j}(\mu_t)\geq0\text{ for all }j\in B\} \tag{3.4}

is an interval, possibly empty or a singleton, closed relative to II. All its masses are positive in the interior of any positive-length feasible interval.

Proof. Each left-node nonnegativity set is an initial interval and each right-node nonnegativity set is a final interval. This follows from continuity and the zero orientation, also when there is no zero. Their intersection is an interval. A zero cannot occur in its positive-length interior because it would create a negative mass on one side. Endpoint masses never vanish. ▫\square

3.2. The successor at simultaneous degeneracies

A maximal occupied block of JJ is a maximal run of consecutive available indices belonging to JJ. Every such block has even length. Consider a finite right endpoint t0∈intIt_0\in\operatorname{int}I of a positive-length feasible interval. Let ZZ be the set of zero masses in JJ at t0t_0.

Lemma 3.3 (suffix-block successor). The set ZZ is nonempty and consists only of left pair nodes. In each occupied block meeting ZZ, let aa be its first zero left node and let ee be the block’s last node. Remove aa and add e+1e+1. The resulting paired set J+J^+, together with the fixed endpoint or endpoints, is feasible and nondegenerate immediately to the right of t0t_0. It is the unique nondegenerate successor.

Proof. If all masses were positive at t0t_0, continuity would extend feasibility to its right. A vanishing right-node mass would have been negative immediately to the left by Lemma 3.1. Hence ZZ is nonempty and contains only left nodes.

A block reaching the last available node cannot meet ZZ. For any left node in such a terminal block, there is no off-basis index to its right; all indices to its right are basis nodes or fixed endpoints. Its cardinal coefficient is thus nonnegative everywhere and equals one at its own index. Its expectation under a positive law is strictly positive. Therefore every required index e+1e+1 exists and is not in JJ.

Replacing aa by e+1e+1 shifts the pairs beginning at a,a+2,…,e−1a,a+2,\ldots,e-1 to pairs beginning at a+1,a+3,…,ea+1,a+3,\ldots,e. It preserves the paired pattern. Changes in different old blocks have distinct new nodes and do not conflict, even if two resulting blocks merge.

At t0t_0 only zero-mass nodes have been removed. The old optimizing law is supported on the new basis and matches Aμt0A\mu_{t_0}. Its new interpolatory masses therefore equal the old masses at retained indices, with zero at each new index. Any old zero retained in a shifted suffix is now a right pair node; newly added nodes are also right nodes. No zero is left before the first shifted node in an affected block. Thus every zero in the new basis is a right-node zero. Lemma 3.1 makes all of them positive to the right, and continuity preserves positivity of the other masses locally. Lemma 2.2 excludes a second nondegenerate basis there, since its positive support would differ from that of the unique optimizer. ▫\square

The rule may move several adjacent pairs at once even when only one index leaves the basis. It must not be replaced by an unproved single-pair rule for arbitrary carriers.

3.3. Existence, the complete sweep, and its length

Theorem 3.4 (sharp bounded monotone path). Under (2.1), every positive reference law has a feasible upper paired basis and a unique endpoint optimizer. Along every continuous strict-MLR path, the pair-start vector of its nondegenerate optimizing basis is coordinatewise nondecreasing. The path has at most

Rn,k=⌊k2⌋(n−k)+1R_{n,k}=\left\lfloor\frac{k}{2}\right\rfloor(n-k)+1 \tag{3.5}

positive-length regimes. Every finite right transition is given by Lemma 3.3. For (3.2), the sweep covers all real parameters and begins and ends at the leftmost and rightmost paired bases. The bound is sharp over the class for each 1≤k<n1\leq k\mathrel{<}n.

Proof of existence and the upper bound. First consider (3.2). In the leftmost paired basis, every omitted index lies to the right of every movable selected index. For a movable node jj, the cardinal expectation is its own positive mass μt(j)\mu_t(j) plus a fixed finite linear combination of omitted masses. Each ratio μt(i)/μt(j)\mu_t(i)/\mu_t(j) for such an omitted ii tends to zero as t→−∞t\to-\infty. Hence every movable mass is positive for all sufficiently negative tt. The fixed endpoint masses are always positive. The analogous argument at +∞+\infty applies to the rightmost basis.

Begin in the leftmost basis and extend its feasible interval. If its right endpoint is finite, apply Lemma 3.3. Each successor increases every pair-start coordinate weakly and at least one strictly. Define the integer potential

Φ(J)=∑r=1mar.\Phi(J)=\sum_{r=1}^m a_r. \tag{3.6}

For even kk, its minimum is ∑r=1m2(r−1)\sum_{r=1}^m2(r-1) and its maximum is ∑r=1m(n−2m+2(r−1))\sum_{r=1}^m(n-2m+2(r-1)). For odd kk, both expressions acquire mm from the left shift of the available grid, while nn is replaced by n−1n-1 in the available-node count. In both cases the total difference is

Φmax−Φmin=m(M−2m)=m(n−k).\Phi_{\max}-\Phi_{\min}=m(M-2m)=m(n-k). \tag{3.7}

Every transition increases Φ\Phi by at least one. Thus the sweep must reach a basis whose feasible interval is unbounded to the right after at most m(n−k)m(n-k) transitions. It cannot stop earlier at a finite parameter, by Lemma 3.3. The known positivity of the rightmost basis at large parameters and uniqueness in Lemma 2.2 identify the final basis. The sweep covers all of ℝ\mathbb R.

For an arbitrary positive law μ\mu, apply this construction to μieti\mu_i e^{t i} and evaluate at t=0t=0. This proves static existence, and Lemma 2.2 proves uniqueness. It also supplies at every positive law a basis feasible just to its right along this artificial tilt, with any zero masses only at right nodes, and the analogous left-side basis.

Now take a general continuous strict-MLR path. At any t0t_0, use the artificial right-side basis just described. Its zero right-node masses become positive for all t>t0t>t_0 by Lemma 3.1, while its other masses remain positive locally by continuity. Thus it is the nondegenerate basis immediately to the right on the actual path as well. The same holds on the left. This excludes an interval consisting only of degenerate solutions, establishes local coverage, and reduces every transition to Lemma 3.3. The globally increasing integer potential limits all transitions by (3.7), so no accumulation or reentry is possible. This proves (3.5). Attainment is established in Theorem 6.1 below. ▫\square

For a general MLR family, concentration at the endpoints alone is not an initialization condition: a selected mass could decay faster than an omitted tail. Formula (3.2) provides the required relative-mass separation. No such endpoint hypothesis is needed for the truncated path bound on an arbitrary interval II.

Corollary 3.5 (static comparative statics). If positive laws μ,ν\mu,\nu satisfy strict MLR order and have nondegenerate upper paired bases, the pair starts for μ\mu do not exceed those for ν\nu coordinatewise.

Proof. Set hi=log(νi/μi)h_i=\log(\nu_i/\mu_i) and interpolate by μs(i)∝μieshi\mu_s(i)\propto\mu_i e^{s h_i}, 0≤s≤10\leq s\leq1. This is a strict-MLR path joining the laws. Apply Theorem 3.4. At degenerate endpoints an arbitrary zero-weight completion is not asserted to obey this comparison. ▫\square

4. A common extremizer for generalized-convex payoffs

Define the payoff cone

𝒞A={g∈ℝn+1:every ordered (k+2)-minor of [A;g] is nonnegative}. \mathcal C_A=\{g\in\mathbb R^{n+1}:\text{every ordered }(k+2)\text{-minor of }[A;g]\text{ is nonnegative}\}. \tag{4.1}

Proposition 4.1 (static comparison and its path consequence). The endpoint optimizer from Theorem 3.4 maximizes ∑igiqi\sum_i g_iq_i simultaneously for every g∈𝒞Ag\in\mathcal C_A under Aq=AμAq=A\mu. If all the relevant augmented minors are strictly positive, this optimizer is unique for that payoff. Hence the MLR support path is shared by the canonical common extremizer for this whole cone.

Proof. Interpolate gg on a feasible paired basis BB by pB=gBAB−1Ap_B=g_BA_B^{-1}A. For an omitted index ii, the determinant with columns BB followed by ii is

det(AB)(gi−pB(i)).\det(A_B)\big(g_i-p_B(i)\big). \tag{4.2}

Sorting these columns moves ii across an odd number of basis indices: the right endpoint and a number of complete pairs. Its sign is therefore negative. The sorted augmented determinant is nonnegative, so gi−pB(i)≤0g_i-p_B(i)\leq0. At BB equality holds. Thus pB≥gp_B\geq g is a row-span majorant, attained by the law on BB. Strict off-basis inequality gives uniqueness by the same argument as Lemma 2.2. ▫\square

The static assertion is the classical generalized-convex extremal principle; we include its proof to specify the cone and avoid attributing newness to the matrix assumption. The new path conclusions do not apply to arbitrary payoffs. For polynomial moments, the cone is the nonnegative (k+1)(k+1)st divided-difference cone. For nonpolynomial Chebyshev systems the same determinant description applies without choosing derivatives or a polynomial basis.

5. Binomial-normalized carriers and unit-pair pivots

7. The complete binomial phase diagram

7.1. Reduction from independent subsets of bits

Let M(n,k,p)M(n,k,p) denote the largest probability that all nn bits are one among laws whose every subset of at most kk bits is independent with mean pp. The standard exchangeability reduction [BEKK26] identifies it with the endpoint moment problem for Bin(n,p)\operatorname{Bin}(n,p).

For completeness, symmetrize any bit law under coordinate permutations; this preserves the constraints and the all-ones event. If SS is its number of ones, then

𝔼(S)r=(n)rpr,0≤r≤k,\mathbb E(S)_r=(n)_r p^r,\qquad0\leq r\leq k, \tag{7.1}

where (x)r=x(x−1)⋯(x−r+1)(x)_r=x(x-1)\cdots(x-r+1). Conversely, given a law of SS satisfying (7.1), choose a uniform subset of size SS to carry the ones. For any rr distinct specified coordinates the probability they are all one is 𝔼(S)r/(n)r=pr\mathbb E(S)_r/(n)_r=p^r. Inclusion-exclusion gives pa(1−p)bp^a(1-p)^b for every pattern with a+b≤ka+b\leq k. Thus the resulting bits are kk-wise independent. Since falling factorials span the polynomials through degree kk, (7.1) is equivalent to matching the ordinary moments of Bin(n,p)\operatorname{Bin}(n,p), and the objective is Pr(S=n)\Pr(S=n).

Corollary 7.1 (binomial support path). For 0<p<10\mathrel{<}p\mathrel{<}1 and 1≤k<n1\leq k\mathrel{<}n, the nondegenerate optimizing paired supports for M(n,k,p)M(n,k,p) are coordinatewise increasing and have at most Rn,kR_{n,k} regimes. ∑rdr=m(n−k),#regimes=1+m(n−k)−∑r(dr−1).\sum_r d_r=m(n-k),\qquad \#\text{regimes}=1+m(n-k)-\sum_r(d_r-1). \tag{7.2}

Proof. The binomial likelihood ratio is strictly increasing in the index when pp increases. Theorem 3.4 gives the path and upper bound, and (5.1)–(5.4) with hi=1h_i=1 allow Theorem 5.2 in the specified range. A unit shift raises the pair-start potential by one. Telescoping from the first to last basis proves (7.2). ▫\square

For even k≤n−2k\leq n-2, this proves the interior positive-length interpretation of the order and single/simultaneous unit-pivot assertions in [BEKK26, Conjectures 5.2, 5.4, 5.5] and makes its Proposition 5.8 unconditional. The source’s conditional counting idea is retained and credited; the new ingredient is the proof of the transitions. The exceptions in Section 8 prevent a claim of literal validity over all endpoint-inclusive or boundary-dimensional statements.

7.2. An exact sweep

For any current paired set JJ, compute the polynomials λj(p)\lambda_j(p) from (5.2) under the appropriate transformed binomial law. Each left-node mass has at most one zero in (0,1)(0,1). Starting with the leftmost basis, isolate its outgoing left-node roots strictly beyond the current boundary. The least such root is the next transition; if no such root exists, the final interval ends at one. At that algebraic root determine all zero left masses exactly. Use the suffix-block rule, or its unit-pair specialization when applicable. Repeat until the rightmost basis is reached.

For each regime, output its paired support, the full original weights βj(p)\beta_j(p), the endpoint-value polynomial, and algebraic descriptions of both boundaries. Record the zero set at each transition. The matching laws at the boundary coincide by Lemma 2.2. Supply δ0\delta_0 at p=0p=0 and δn\delta_n at p=1p=1, because the mean constraint forces those endpoint laws. No basis-completion claim at a degenerate endpoint is needed.

The full weights can be obtained by taking binomial expectations of the ordinary degree-kk cardinal polynomials. In particular,

M(n,2m,p)=𝔼QJ(S)QJ(n),M(n,2m+1,p)=𝔼[SQJ(S)]nQJ(n), M(n,2m,p)=\frac{\mathbb E Q_J(S)}{Q_J(n)},\qquad M(n,2m+1,p)=\frac{\mathbb E[SQ_J(S)]}{nQ_J(n)}, \tag{7.3}

where in the second expression JJ is the unshifted paired part. These are valid on their corresponding regimes, not globally for a fixed JJ.

Theorem 7.2 (polynomial algebraic output and bit complexity). The complete binomial phase diagram can be computed exactly in a number of bit operations polynomial in nn and kk, with algebraic endpoints and rational-coefficient weight polynomials. The statement uses the expanded grid size; it does not assert complexity polynomial in logn\log n.

Proof. For k=1k=1 the basis is {0,n}\{0,n\} with masses 1−p,p1-p,p, and the claim is immediate. Otherwise let d=2m−1≥1d=2m-1\geq1. Each transformed cardinal polynomial is Pj(x)/DjP_j(x)/D_j, where PjP_j is a monic product of dd integer linear factors whose roots have magnitude at most nn, and DjD_j is a nonzero product of dd integer differences. The sum of the absolute coefficients of PjP_j is at most (n+1)d(n+1)^d, and |Dj|≤nd|D_j|\leq n^d.

Write Pj(x)=∑r=0dcr(x)rP_j(x)=\sum_{r=0}^d c_r(x)_r. Conversion from powers to falling factorials uses Stirling numbers of the second kind. For indices at most dd, these are at most ddd^d; therefore the integers crc_r have bit length O(dlog(nd))O(d\log(nd)). For X∼Bin(N,p)X\sim\operatorname{Bin}(N,p),

Djλj(p)=∑r=0dcr(N)rpr.D_j\lambda_j(p)=\sum_{r=0}^d c_r(N)_r p^r. \tag{7.4}

Since (N)r≤nd(N)_r\leq n^d, the integer coefficients on the right have bit length O(klog(nk))O(k\log(nk)). Its degree is at most k−1k-1 for even kk and k−2k-2 for odd kk. The analogous full cardinal construction has degree at most kk and the same coefficient-size order.

There are at most O(nk)O(nk) regimes by Theorem 3.4, and at most mm outgoing left-root candidates per regime. Integer-polynomial square-free factorization, gcd, real-root isolation, and comparison of real algebraic roots take polynomial bit complexity in degree and coefficient length using standard subresultant and isolation algorithms [BPR06, EMT08]. For comparison between roots of different polynomials, a gcd determines common roots, and isolation can be refined to disjoint rational intervals for distinct roots. Root-separation bounds for their product require only a polynomial number of bits. Thus every transition and every simultaneous zero set is decidable in polynomial time. Coefficient construction, support updates, and the output of O(nk)O(nk) sets of O(k)O(k) degree-kk polynomials are polynomial as well. ▫\square

This is an all-parameter output bound. It does not improve the known polynomial solvability of a single fixed-pp linear program, and does not assert a bit bound for arbitrary real grids, arbitrary real matrix entries, or arbitrary real sufficient statistics in (3.2). For those inputs Theorem 3.4 is a structural theorem.

8. Necessary qualifications and failure under stochastic order

8.1. Closed parameter endpoints

For n=4,k=2n=4,k=2 and B={0,1,4}B=\{0,1,4\}, direct interpolation against the binomial law gives

The endpoint caution is already present in [H26, Remark 4]; (8.1) is an explicit elementary illustration.

10. Scope and remaining questions

The general theorem concerns finite ordered one-dimensional configurations satisfying (2.1), positive reference laws, and strict MLR variation. It permits arbitrary simultaneous degeneracies and applies to all generalized-convex payoffs through a common canonical extremizer. It does not cover an arbitrary matrix, a multivariate grid without the ordered-minor structure, or an arbitrary right-hand-side path. The FSD example explains one concrete failure of such an extension.

For ordinary moments, the unit-pair result uses a sufficient condition on the transformed carrier. Sharpness of the universal count is established over the class, not for every fixed nonpolynomial configuration. The bit bound concerns binomial inputs measured in the expanded grid dimensions. Reflection belongs to [H26]. Rational exceptional points and the maximal-spread conjectures in [BEKK26] remain open here. No claim of global priority or journal acceptance follows from the literature comparison. This manuscript has not undergone external peer review.
