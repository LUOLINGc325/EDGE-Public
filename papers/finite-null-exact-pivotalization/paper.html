Exact pivotalization for finite composite tests

Finite-sample guarantees and minimax-optimal universal adaptation

Research manuscript, 26 November 2026. Written mathematical results; no proof-assistant verification has been performed. All logarithms are natural.

Abstract

Consider a fixed finite collection of null distributions and a specified alternative. An e-variable is exact and pivotal when its mean is one under each null and its entire null distribution is independent of which null is true. We give an explicit construction with quantitative efficiency. For pairwise distinct probability measures P1,…,PL,QP_1,\ldots,P_L,Q on a standard Borel space, suppose Q≪PiQ\ll P_i and D(Q‖Pi)<∞D(Q\Vert P_i)\lt \infty. With a common independent randomization source, let ℓn\ell_n be the optimal exact-pivotal expected log e-value and VnV_n the ordinary valid optimum for the null set {Pi⊗n}\{P_i^{\otimes n}\}. Writing Tn=minilog(q⊗n/pi⊗n)T_n=\min_i\log(q^{\otimes n}/p_i^{\otimes n}), we construct an exact-pivotal e-variable with expected logarithm at least 𝔼QTn−εn\mathbb E_QT_n-\varepsilon_n, where an explicit Hellinger-affinity bound gives εn=O(nhn/2)\varepsilon_n=O(nh^{n/2}), h<1h\lt 1. Consequently 0≤Vn−ℓn≤logL+εn0\le V_n-\ell_n\le\log L+\varepsilon_n.

The construction preserves high likelihood scores and compensates their null output laws inside regions that identify the separate nulls. A finite matrix inverse makes the compensation nonnegative and exact. No likelihood-ratio moment generating function is needed for the main bound. Finite second moments yield the same Gaussian-maximum second-order term for exact-pivotal and ordinary e-power. We characterize when that term vanishes, give a sufficient condition for exponential convergence to the single-nearest-null oracle, and establish a local near-tie transition on fixed finite alphabets. An implementation based on empirical types has polynomial arithmetic cost in sample size when alphabet size and the number of nulls are fixed. The additive constant logL\log L is an upper bound, not asserted to be sharp.

On a fixed finite alphabet of size KK with full-support nulls, we further construct one exact-pivotal statistic that does not know the alternative. For every fixed alternative outside the null family, its regret relative to the alternative-specific ordinary optimum is at most logL+logCn,K+o(1)\log L+\log C_{n,K}+o(1), where Cn,KC_{n,K} is the classical multinomial normalized-maximum-likelihood constant and logCn,K=(K−1)logn/2+O(1)\log C_{n,K}=(K-1)\log n/2+O(1). On every full-dimensional compact cube contained in one strict KL nearest-null region and avoiding the nulls, a matching lower bound applies to all ordinary valid randomized statistics. The entire common null law therefore imposes no additional leading minimax learning cost.

1. The question and the contribution

An ordinary e-variable E≥0E\ge0 satisfies 𝔼PiE≤1\mathbb E_{P_i}E\le1 for every null. Large observed values provide evidence against the null family. Its expected logarithm under a specified alternative is commonly called e-power, the growth-rate objective studied in [5,10]. Exactness strengthens the inequalities to equalities. Pivotality requires one common distribution under all nulls, not merely equal means.

Zhang, Ramdas and Wang [1] developed existence and construction theory for these requirements, including the SHINE procedure and special symmetric Gaussian solutions. Their Section 6 also characterizes existence against finite composite alternatives; existence alone does not quantify the regret of one statistic over an unknown alternative. He and Cheng [2] subsequently proved first-order asymptotic losslessness for fixed finite families under joint atomlessness, including support configurations and infinite-divergence cases beyond our assumptions. Their argument uses the large-sample matrix-majorization theorem of Farooq et al. [9] and does not state a quantitative sample threshold or the finite-sample envelope bound considered here. We address that quantitative gap. The first-order limit itself is prior work.

The principal contribution is Theorem 2.1: a finite-sample, whole-distribution calibration construction for general finite-KL experiments. It bounds the additional exact-pivotal restriction relative to the ordinary optimum by a constant independent of sample size, with an exponentially vanishing remainder and an explicit threshold. The positive measure compensation in Lemma 3.1 is the reusable part of the argument. The central-limit and likelihood-max mechanisms used subsequently are classical; the new connection is their transfer to the exact-pivotal optimum through this quantitative construction.

A second principal contribution is the universal construction and matching minimax comparison in Theorems 8.2–8.3. One statistic, independent of the unknown alternative, is exact and has an identical entire distribution under all nulls while attaining the classical optimal logarithmic learning coefficient. The result concerns arbitrary fixed finite collections of full-support nulls, with a substantive pivotality constraint when L≥2L\ge2. Normalized maximum likelihood, information-radius lower bounds and the coefficient itself are classical [12–14,17]. Ordinary universal inference [15] and simple-null relative-GROW/NML theory [16] already provide alternative adaptation without this multiple-null distribution constraint.

Combining a common null law with alternative learning also has important structured precedents. Henzi and Law [18] use randomized sequential ranks to reduce the composite independence null to a common uniform law, then apply multinomial prediction; their Remark 2 discusses Jeffreys-prior growth optimality and universal coding. Pérez-Ortiz et al. [19] establish absolute and relative GROW optimality of maximal-invariant likelihood ratios in suitable group models. Our added result is the quantitative guarantee for an arbitrary fixed finite null family without such rank or group structure, measured against its original-experiment, alternative-specific ordinary optimum. We do not claim the general idea of learning after a pivotal reduction as new.

Recent work on infinite-family binary pivots [3], growing Gaussian grids [4], ordinary log-optimal e-values [5], constrained e-values [6], and GROW duality [7] concerns adjacent constraints or optimization problems. None of the results inspected in those sources supplies the fixed-family construction and bound proved below. Pan, Li and Tan [8] study second-order composite sequential testing and a central limit theorem for a maximized log-likelihood statistic. Their Proposition 6 in arXiv v2 has a scalar normal limit under its regularity assumptions; we do not attribute the tied-minimizer coefficient below to that proposition. The literature comparison is bounded by the versions and material recorded in the accompanying source record; it is not a universal priority claim.

1.1 Experiment and randomization

Fix L≥1L\ge1 and pairwise distinct probability measures P1,…,PL,QP_1,\ldots,P_L,Q on a standard Borel space 𝒳\mathcal X. Repeated copies of a null are removed. Throughout the main theorem,

Q≪Pi,0<di:=D(Q‖Pi)<∞,r:=minidi. Q\ll P_i,\qquad 0\lt d_i:=D(Q\Vert P_i)\lt \infty, \qquad r:=\min_i d_i. \tag{1.1}

The observation is Xn=(X1,…,Xn)X^n=(X_1,\ldots,X_n), iid under the applicable distribution. We explicitly allow U∼Unif[0,1]U\sim\operatorname{Unif}[0,1], independent of XnX^n under every hypothesis. All e-variables may be measurable functions of (Xn,U)(X^n,U). Standard Borel kernels can therefore be implemented as randomized statistics. This is not an assertion about deterministic statistics on an arbitrary finite sample space. Under the stronger joint-atomlessness condition of [1,2,11], the same construction can use the common random source encoded in the experiment; ordinary atomlessness alone is not that condition.

The null family at sample size nn is precisely

𝒫n={P1⊗n,…,PL⊗n}. \mathcal P_n=\{P_1^{\otimes n},\ldots,P_L^{\otimes n}\}. \tag{1.2}

The results also hold for mixtures of these complete product laws. They do not automatically hold for iid sampling from every member of conv{Pi}\operatorname{conv}\{P_i\} or for arbitrary switching between nulls over time. The constructions in Sections 2–7 use the specified alternative; Section 8 treats an unknown alternative under finite-alphabet assumptions. A convex mixture of product laws and a product of a convex mixture are different experiments.

3. Positive compensation of complete output laws

8. Universal adaptation to an unknown alternative

The previous general-space construction knows the alternative. We now construct one statistic that does not. Fix an alphabet of size K≥2K\ge2, put d=K−1d=K-1, and let P1,…,PLP_1,\ldots,P_L be distinct full-support null distributions. 9. Scope and unresolved questions

The general standard-Borel construction is alternative-specific; Section 8 gives a single alternative-independent construction on a fixed finite alphabet with full-support nulls. Both apply to a fixed finite list of complete iid null laws. Neither constructs a process simultaneously valid at every stopping time. Separate fixed-nn e-variables cannot automatically be treated as one e-process.

The bound logL\log L is not proved to be the optimal worst-case additive price. An exact description of that price, finite-sample optimization of the output kernel, and sharp third-order terms remain open here. Our threshold can be conservative. An efficiently computable continuous-model implementation requires more than the existence of the stated measure operations.

The near-tie theorem keeps pairwise separation and alphabet size controlled. It supplies no uniform growing-family or merging-null limit. Infinite KL divergences, general composite alternatives outside the finite-alphabet setting of Section 8, and arbitrary dependent observations are outside the results as stated. The finite-KL assumption is sufficient for the main exponential envelope approximation; second moments and (5.6) are additional assumptions only where explicitly invoked.
