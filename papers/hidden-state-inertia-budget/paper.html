Hidden-state complexity from excursion moments: inertia bounds and a quantitative dissipation penalty

Wen Weixiang · 26 November 2026

Abstract

An entropy-minimizing fit to a partially observed Markov chain can acquire a positive dissipation floor solely because too few hidden states are allowed. We give an observable obstruction to low-dissipation realizations. For any finite stationary reciprocal continuous-time Markov chain with individually visible states and one hidden label, let RR be the matrix of hidden-excursion occurrence rates and LL its occurrence-weighted first-duration-moment matrix. If LL is symmetric with q≥1q\geq1 negative eigenvalues, any fixed hidden-state budget supporting exact-data models with entropy production tending to zero is at least rankR+q+1\operatorname{rank}R+q+1. More generally, we give a quantitative certificate in every visible dimension without exact symmetry: for r≤rankRr\leq\operatorname{rank}R and r≤K≤r+qr\leq K\leq r+q, the entropy is bounded below by sr(R)[νq(Ls)−∥La∥op]+2/[8m(K−r+1)PH2]s_r(R)[\nu_q(L_s)-\|L_a\|_{\mathrm{op}}]_+^2/[8m(K-r+1)P_H^2]. Here νq\nu_q is the magnitude of the qqth negative spectral direction, Ls,LaL_s,L_a are the symmetric and antisymmetric parts, and PHP_H is hidden mass. For every m≥2m\geq2 and 1≤q≤m−11\leq q\leq m-1, strictly positive full-rank data attain the sharp budget m+q+1m+q+1; its extremal case is 2m2m. For two visible states and at most three hidden states, we additionally prove a positive, computable entropy bound using the second singular value of RR, the negative part of the spectrum of the symmetric part of LL, and its row-column imbalance. A rational data set has feasible three-hidden-state models, all with entropy production greater than 1/750001/75000, while an explicit four-hidden-state family preserves every datum exactly and has entropy production tending to zero, with all exit rates at most 100100. The separation persists on a nonempty open subset of the positive consistency manifold. The proofs use a stationary-flow estimate for forward and backward hitting probabilities and require no bound on internal rates or a prescribed hidden topology.

Interpretation and verification scope. Entropy production throughout means stationary path irreversibility; a physical dissipation interpretation needs the usual thermodynamic modeling assumptions. The results are proved mathematically below. No proof-assistant verification is asserted.

1. Question, results, and relation to prior work

When only part of a stochastic network is visible, minimizing entropy production over all compatible microscopic models is a natural inference procedure. Its scientific interpretation depends on the model class. A restriction on the number of hidden states may exclude low-dissipation explanations even when a reduced model fits all supplied statistics exactly.

The present observation contract distinguishes mm individual visible states and merges every other state into one hidden label. The observations include stationary masses, coarse transition flows, the rates RijR_{ij} of excursions from visible state ii through the hidden set to visible state jj, and their first duration moments LijL_{ij}. Return excursions are included. Neither higher moments nor the entire observed path law are fixed.

Our main statements are as follows.

  1. A forward-backward hitting-probability estimate controls their Dirichlet mismatch by total entropy production, uniformly in the hidden topology and rates (Theorem 3.1).
  2. The rank of RR and negative inertia of symmetric LL give a necessary hidden budget (Theorem 4.1). A nonsymmetric quantitative certificate applies in every visible dimension and is stable under perturbations of the observed matrices (Theorem 4.3 and Corollary 4.4).
  3. For two visible states, a quantitative certificate applies to every model with at most three hidden states, including nonsymmetric observations (Theorem 5.1).
  4. Explicit compatible models show a genuine three-versus-four dissipation separation; the same mechanism gives a sharp m+q+1m+q+1 family for every full-rank inertia qq, including the extremal 2m2m case (Theorems 6.3, 7.1, and 7.2).
  5. A submersion argument and the nonsymmetric certificate give a full-dimensional robust separation within the consistency manifold (Theorem 8.1).

Skinner and Dunkel established optimization-based inference from incomplete transition statistics [1] and waiting-time-based dissipation bounds [2]. Nitzan, Ghosal, and Bisker studied topology reduction with transition and duration information [3]. Transition-based waiting-time inference also connects hidden cycles and irreversibility [4]. We do not claim these frameworks, the entropy formula, hitting probabilities, or phase-type moment identities as new.

The closest specific source is Petkov [5]. For positive two-visible-state data, it gives a four-hidden-state realization sequence attaining the unrestricted entropy infimum and explicitly leaves the minimal universal hidden budget open. The same source already proves that feasible two-hidden-state fits can be unsafe and that their overestimate can be arbitrarily large. Our feasible three-state obstruction shows that the universal four-state budget cannot be reduced. More substantially, the general inertia law, its dimension-dependent sharp family, and the quantitative certificate address how observed timing geometry forces microscopic complexity. The four-state sufficiency result of [5] remains its author’s contribution. Our concrete separation, general sharp family, and open-set separation are proved here without using that result as a black box.

Petkov’s companion work [6] concerns equilibrium realization of an entire observed path law and complete positivity of reflected observation matrices. Its fixed-law realization contract differs from the finite-moment contract here. Aznagulov [8] proves fixed-law attainment and architecture-independent positive floors using an entire observed sojourn density. Those results preserve the full observation law, whereas the families below preserve only the specified count and first-moment data. A fixed-law positive floor and a finite-moment zero infimum therefore concern different feasible sets. Work connecting committors and information thermodynamics [7] concerns another related use of hitting probabilities; the budget conclusions below are not consequences of identifying a reaction coordinate. The literature comparison is limited to the sources and searches documented with this manuscript, and does not certify global priority.

2. Observation model and elementary identities

2.1. Stationary-flow description

Let V={1,…,m}V=\{1,\ldots,m\} and HH be disjoint finite sets, with m≥2m\geq2 and H≠⌀H\ne\varnothing. A model is an irreducible continuous-time Markov chain on V∪HV\cup H, with row generator QQ and strictly positive stationary probability vector π\pi. Its support is reciprocal:

qxy>0⇔qyx>0(x≠y).q_{xy}>0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad q_{yx}>0\qquad(x\ne y). \tag{2.1}

Missing reciprocal pairs are permitted. Each model has finite rates, but sequences of models need not share a rate bound. Write nxy=πxqxyn_{xy}=\pi_xq_{xy} for x≠yx\ne y and nxx=0n_{xx}=0. Stationarity is equivalent to equality of each row and column sum of N=(nxy)N=(n_{xy}).

The entropy production is

σ=∑x<y(nxy−nyx)lognxynyx,\sigma=\sum_{x\lt y}(n_{xy}-n_{yx})\log\frac{n_{xy}}{n_{yx}}, \tag{2.2}

where absent pairs contribute zero. Natural logarithms are used. Define the hidden mass PH=∑h∈HπhP_H=\sum_{h\in H}\pi_h, visible masses Pi=πiP_i=\pi_i, and direct visible flows Fij=nijF_{ij}=n_{ij} for i≠ji\ne j in VV. Coarse visible-hidden flows sum the corresponding nihn_{ih} and nhin_{hi}.

Let Uih=nihU_{ih}=n_{ih}, Vhiout=nhiV_{hi}^{\mathrm{out}}=n_{hi}, D=diag(πh)D=\operatorname{diag}(\pi_h), and

J=D(−QHH).J=D(-Q_{HH}). \tag{2.3}

To avoid confusing the visible state set with the exit matrix, we write the latter as W=VoutW=V^{\mathrm{out}} from now on. The diagonal of JJ is total hidden outflow; its off-diagonal entries are −nhg-n_{hg}.

Lemma 2.1 (balance and absorption). The matrix JJ is invertible with entrywise nonnegative inverse, and

J𝟏=W𝟏,𝟏TJ=𝟏TU.J\mathbf1=W\mathbf1,\qquad \mathbf1^TJ=\mathbf1^TU. \tag{2.4}

Proof. A finite irreducible chain hits the nonempty visible set almost surely with finite mean time from any hidden state. For example, finite irreducibility supplies a positive probability, uniform over the finitely many starting states, of hitting it within a fixed time; iteration gives a geometric tail on that time grid. Thus the killed hidden semigroup decays exponentially. With M=−QHH=D−1JM=-Q_{HH}=D^{-1}J,

M−1=∫0∞e−MtdtM^{-1}=\int_0^\infty e^{-Mt}\,dt

exists and is nonnegative. Hence J−1=M−1D−1J^{-1}=M^{-1}D^{-1} is nonnegative. The first identity in (2.4) is the hidden row-sum identity; the second is hidden stationarity. ▫\square

2.2. Counts, moments, and hitting probabilities

An excursion begins on a jump from a visible state into HH and ends at the next visible visit, including a return to the initial state. Its duration excludes visible holding times. Let RijR_{ij} be its stationary occurrence rate, and let LijL_{ij} be that rate times its conditional mean duration. If a rate is zero, the corresponding first-moment entry is defined by the integrated occurrence measure and is zero.

Lemma 2.2 (observation identities). The exact count and first-moment matrices are

R=UJ−1W,L=UJ−1DJ−1W.R=UJ^{-1}W,\qquad L=UJ^{-1}DJ^{-1}W. \tag{2.5}

Proof. The occurrence-rate density matrix at duration tt is UeQHHtD−1WUe^{Q_{HH}t}D^{-1}W. Integrating it uses ∫0∞e−Mtdt=M−1\int_0^\infty e^{-Mt}dt=M^{-1}; multiplying by tt before integration uses ∫0∞te−Mtdt=M−2\int_0^\infty t e^{-Mt}dt=M^{-2}. The latter identity follows by integration by parts, justified by the exponential decay in Lemma 2.1. Substitute M−1=J−1DM^{-1}=J^{-1}D. ▫\square

Define matrices of forward and backward hitting probabilities by

C=J−1W,A=J−TUT.C=J^{-1}W,\qquad A=J^{-T}U^T. \tag{2.6}

Their rows chT,ahTc_h^T,a_h^T are indexed by hidden states; we regard ch,ahc_h,a_h as column vectors in ℝm\mathbb R^m.

Lemma 2.3 (probability factorization). Both AA and CC are entrywise nonnegative and row-stochastic. They are the next-visible distributions for the chain and its stationary time reversal. In particular,

R=UC,L=ATDC=∑hπhahchT,𝟏TL𝟏=PH.R=UC,\qquad L=A^TDC=\sum_h\pi_h a_hc_h^T,\qquad \mathbf1^TL\mathbf1=P_H. \tag{2.7}

Moreover, R𝟏=U𝟏R\mathbf1=U\mathbf1 and 𝟏TR=𝟏TW\mathbf1^TR=\mathbf1^TW.

Proof. Nonnegativity and row sums follow from Lemma 2.1. The killed stationary-flow matrix of the reversed chain is JTJ^T, and its exit matrix is UTU^T. Solving the corresponding Dirichlet problems gives (2.6). Substitution proves the factorizations and the three marginal identities. ▫\square

2.3. Exact realization by a single channel

We will use the following constructive fact, credited as a standard channel realization rather than as a new theorem.

Lemma 2.4 (rank-one channel). Let M=tabTM=tab^T be a nonzero nonnegative rank-one m×mm\times m matrix, with t=𝟏TM𝟏>0t=\mathbf1^TM\mathbf1>0 and probability vectors a,ba,b. Suppose ai>0a_i>0 exactly when bi>0b_i>0. A single hidden state with entrance flows taita_i, exit flows tbitb_i, mass τt\tau t, and mean holding time τ>0\tau>0 contributes MM to RR and τM\tau M to LL. Its entropy contribution is

𝒞(M)=t∑i:ai>0(ai−bi)logaibi.\mathcal C(M)=t\sum_{i:a_i>0}(a_i-b_i)\log\frac{a_i}{b_i}. \tag{2.8}

Scaling MM by a positive scalar scales 𝒞(M)\mathcal C(M) by that scalar. A symmetric channel has zero entropy production. For two visible states and positive cross entries,

𝒞(M)=(M12−M21)logM12M21.\mathcal C(M)=(M_{12}-M_{21})\log\frac{M_{12}}{M_{21}}. \tag{2.9}

Proof. Its killed flow is J=tJ=t, while U=taU=ta and W=tbTW=tb^T. Equations (2.5) yield the two contributions. The entrance and exit totals coincide, proving stationarity at the hidden state; (2.8) is the sum of its boundary-edge entropy terms. In dimension two, a1−b1=−(a2−b2)a_1-b_1=-(a_2-b_2) and M12/M21=a1b2/(a2b1)M_{12}/M_{21}=a_1b_2/(a_2b_1), giving (2.9). Symmetric one-coordinate channels also have zero entropy production, without a logarithm of zero. ▫\square

Several channels may be placed in parallel. The visible flow-balance condition is

∑j≠i(Fij−Fji)=(𝟏TR)i−(R𝟏)i.\sum_{j\ne i}(F_{ij}-F_{ji})=(\mathbf1^TR)_i-(R\mathbf1)_i. \tag{2.10}

Positive prescribed visible masses, hidden masses summing to PHP_H, and a connected reciprocal graph then give a genuine irreducible chain by dividing each stationary flow by its origin mass. This operation preserves the selected time unit.

3. Entropy controls forward-backward hitting mismatch

Form the full stationary-flow Laplacian

G=diag(N𝟏)−N,S=G+GT2,K=G−GT2.G=\operatorname{diag}(N\mathbf1)-N,\qquad S=\frac{G+G^T}{2},\qquad K=\frac{G-G^T}{2}. \tag{3.1}

For unordered supported pairs put sxy=(nxy+nyx)/2s_{xy}=(n_{xy}+n_{yx})/2 and jxy=nxy−nyxj_{xy}=n_{xy}-n_{yx}, with a fixed orientation x<yx\lt y. Stationarity gives K𝟏=0K\mathbf1=0, and

ℰ(f)=fTSf=∑x<ysxy(fx−fy)2.\mathcal E(f)=f^TSf=\sum_{x\lt y}s_{xy}(f_x-f_y)^2. \tag{3.2}

Lemma 3.1 (current inequality and cancellation). We have

∑x<yjxy2sxy≤σ.\sum_{x\lt y}\frac{j_{xy}^2}{s_{xy}}\leq\sigma. \tag{3.3}

For any real vectors u,vu,v,

uTKv=−12∑x<yjxy(ux−uy)(vx+vy).u^TKv=-\frac12\sum_{x\lt y}j_{xy}(u_x-u_y)(v_x+v_y). \tag{3.4}

Proof. For positive a,ba,b, set z=(a−b)/(a+b)z=(a-b)/(a+b). Then (a−b)log(a/b)=2(a+b)zartanhz≥2(a−b)2/(a+b)(a-b)\log(a/b)=2(a+b)z\operatorname{artanh}z\geq2(a-b)^2/(a+b), since zartanhz≥z2z\operatorname{artanh}z\geq z^2. Summing proves (3.3). Direct expansion gives uTKv=−12∑jxy(uxvy−uyvx)u^TKv=-\frac12\sum j_{xy}(u_xv_y-u_yv_x). The difference between that summand and the expression in (3.4) is jxy(uxvx−uyvy)j_{xy}(u_xv_x-u_yv_y); its sum vanishes because stationary currents have zero divergence. ▫\square

Theorem 3.1 (committor energy bound). For a fixed visible index ii, extend the forward and backward hitting probabilities c,bc,b to the full graph with the common boundary values 𝟏{i}\mathbf1_{\{i\}} on the visible states. Then

ℰ(c−b)≤σ.\mathcal E(c-b)\leq\sigma. \tag{3.5}

Consequently, with wh=12∑i(Uih+Whi)w_h=\frac12\sum_i(U_{ih}+W_{hi}),

wh(Chi−Ahi)2≤σfor every h,i.w_h(C_{hi}-A_{hi})^2\leq\sigma \quad\text{for every }h,i. \tag{3.6}

Proof. Write δ=c−b\delta=c-b and g=c+b−𝟏g=c+b-\mathbf1. Then δ\delta vanishes on the visible boundary and |gx|≤1|g_x|\leq1. The harmonic equations are (Gc)h=0(Gc)_h=0 and (GTb)h=0(G^Tb)_h=0. Multiplying by δ\delta and subtracting gives

ℰ(δ)=−δTK(c+b)=−δTKg.\mathcal E(\delta)=-\delta^TK(c+b)=-\delta^TKg.

ℰ(δ)≤∑x<y|jxy||δx−δy|≤σℰ(δ).\mathcal E(\delta)\leq\sum_{x\lt y}|j_{xy}|\,|\delta_x-\delta_y| \leq\sqrt{\sigma\mathcal E(\delta)}.

When the energy is positive, division and squaring give (3.5); the zero case is immediate. The visible-hidden boundary terms of (3.2) equal ∑hwhδh2\sum_h w_h\delta_h^2 and are part of a sum of nonnegative terms. This proves (3.6). ▫\square

No internal rate, conductance, or inverse of a small spectral gap appears in this estimate. In particular, it remains valid along sequences with diverging internal flows or vanishing stationary masses.

4. An inertia lower bound on hidden complexity

For a real symmetric matrix LL, let ν−(L)\nu_-(L) count strictly negative eigenvalues with multiplicity. An exact data set consists of compatible selected masses, coarse flows, RR, and LL. Let 𝒞k(d)\mathcal C_k(d) contain compatible finite reciprocal models with at most kk hidden states, and let Ik(d)=infQ∈𝒞k(d)σ(Q)I_k(d)=\inf_{Q\in\mathcal C_k(d)}\sigma(Q) when this class is nonempty. A zero-infimum assertion always requires a nonempty class.

Theorem 4.1 (rank-inertia obstruction). Suppose L=LTL=L^T and q=ν−(L)≥1q=\nu_-(L)\geq1. If there exists an exact-data sequence with at most kk hidden states and entropy production tending to zero, then

k≥rankR+q+1.k\geq\operatorname{rank}R+q+1. \tag{4.1}

Proof. Pass to a subsequence with a constant hidden state count h≤kh\leq k and label those states consistently. The hidden masses lie in a compact closed simplex. The entries of UU are bounded because its row sums equal those of the fixed RR. Each row of A,CA,C is a probability vector. Thus D,U,A,CD,U,A,C have a jointly convergent subsequence. This uses no compactness of Q,JQ,J, or the internal flows.

Let 𝒜\mathcal A be the indices with nonzero limiting entrance column, and let a=|𝒜|a=|\mathcal A|. From the exact identity R=UCR=UC, every limiting column outside 𝒜\mathcal A contributes zero, so rankR≤a\operatorname{rank}R\leq a. For h∈𝒜h\in\mathcal A, the entrance total has positive limit and whw_h is at least half that total. Equation (3.6) therefore makes the limiting rows aha_h and chc_h equal.

In the limit, (2.7) becomes

L=P+T,P=∑h∈𝒜πhchchT≽0,T=∑h∉𝒜πhahchT.L=P+T,\qquad P=\sum_{h\in\mathcal A}\pi_hc_hc_h^T\succeq0, \qquad T=\sum_{h\notin\mathcal A}\pi_ha_hc_h^T. \tag{4.2}

The matrix TT is entrywise nonnegative, has rank at most h−ah-a, and is symmetric because LL and PP are symmetric. It is nonzero, since otherwise LL could have no negative eigenvalue. The all-ones vector has strictly positive quadratic form under a nonzero symmetric nonnegative matrix, so TT has at least one positive eigenvalue. Hence

ν−(T)≤rankT−1≤h−a−1.\nu_-(T)\leq\operatorname{rank}T-1\leq h-a-1.

Adding a positive semidefinite matrix cannot increase negative inertia: on any subspace where L=P+TL=P+T is negative definite, TT is also negative definite. Therefore q≤ν−(T)≤h−a−1q\leq\nu_-(T)\leq h-a-1, giving h≥a+q+1≥rankR+q+1h\geq a+q+1\geq\operatorname{rank}R+q+1. ▫\square

Corollary 4.2 (positive floor below the budget). If 𝒞k(d)\mathcal C_k(d) is nonempty and k<rankR+ν−(L)+1k\lt \operatorname{rank}R+\nu_-(L)+1 under the preceding hypotheses, then Ik(d)>0I_k(d)>0.

Proof. Every finite reciprocal model has finite nonnegative entropy. If the infimum were zero, selecting entropy below 1/n1/n would contradict Theorem 4.1. No attainment of the infimum is needed or claimed. ▫\square

The extra +1+1 is essential: the residual nonnegative symmetric matrix in (4.2) needs a positive direction as well as the negative directions it supplies. The theorem is about approaching zero total entropy. It does not automatically bound excess entropy above a nonzero count-only floor.

4.2. A quantitative certificate in every visible dimension

The preceding obstruction has a quantitative form without exact symmetry. Write sr(R)s_r(R) for the rrth largest singular value, Ls=(L+LT)/2L_s=(L+L^T)/2, La=(L−LT)/2L_a=(L-L^T)/2, and let λq↑(Ls)\lambda_q^\uparrow(L_s) be the qqth eigenvalue in increasing order. Put

νq(Ls)=[−λq↑(Ls)]+.\nu_q(L_s)=[-\lambda_q^\uparrow(L_s)]_+. \tag{4.3}

Theorem 4.3 (general observable budget penalty). Let m≥2m\geq2, 1≤r≤rankR1\leq r\leq\operatorname{rank}R, 1≤q≤m−11\leq q\leq m-1, and r≤K≤r+qr\leq K\leq r+q be integers. Every compatible model with at most KK hidden states satisfies

σ≥Γr,q,K(R,L):=sr(R)8m(K−r+1)PH2[νq(Ls)−∥La∥op]+2.\sigma\geq\Gamma_{r,q,K}(R,L) :=\frac{s_r(R)}{8m(K-r+1)P_H^2} \big[\nu_q(L_s)-\|L_a\|_{\mathrm{op}}\big]_+^2. \tag{4.4}

The result is uniform over all finite hidden topologies, rates, and stationary mass allocations. A model with fewer than rr hidden states cannot fit RR. No symmetry of RR or LL is required.

Proof. Append algebraic zero terms to the factorizations (2.7) until there are KK terms; give each appended term arbitrary probability rows and zero mass, entrance, and exit flow. Sort the whw_h of (3.6) in nonincreasing order. Let uhu_h be entrance column hh. Removing the first r−1r-1 summands of R=∑huhchTR=\sum_hu_hc_h^T gives

sr(R)≤∥∑h=rKuhchT∥op≤∑h=rK∥uh∥1≤2∑h=rKwh≤2(K−r+1)wr.\begin{aligned} s_r(R)&\leq\left\|\sum_{h=r}^K u_hc_h^T\right\|_{\mathrm{op}} \leq\sum_{h=r}^K\|u_h\|_1\\ &\leq2\sum_{h=r}^K w_h \leq2(K-r+1)w_r. \end{aligned}\tag{4.5}

We used the singular-value lower bound on any rank-(r−1)(r-1) approximation, ∥ch∥2≤1\|c_h\|_2\leq1, and ∥uh∥1≤2wh\|u_h\|_1\leq2w_h. In particular wr>0w_r>0. Equation (3.6), summed over the mm visible coordinates, yields

∥ah−ch∥2≤2m(K−r+1)σsr(R),h≤r.\|a_h-c_h\|_2\leq \sqrt{\frac{2m(K-r+1)\sigma}{s_r(R)}},\qquad h\leq r. \tag{4.6}

Split the moment matrix as

L=P+E+B,P=∑h≤rπhchchT≽0,E=∑h≤rπh(ah−ch)chT,B=∑h>rπhahchT.\begin{aligned} L&=P+E+B,\\ P&=\sum_{h\leq r}\pi_hc_hc_h^T\succeq0,\\ E&=\sum_{h\leq r}\pi_h(a_h-c_h)c_h^T,\qquad B=\sum_{h>r}\pi_ha_hc_h^T. \end{aligned}\tag{4.7}

Then BB is entrywise nonnegative, rankB≤K−r≤q\operatorname{rank}B\leq K-r\leq q, and

e:=∥E∥op≤PH2m(K−r+1)σsr(R).e:=\|E\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq P_H\sqrt{\frac{2m(K-r+1)\sigma}{s_r(R)}}. \tag{4.8}

Let H=(B+BT)/2H=(B+B^T)/2 and a=∥La∥opa=\|L_a\|_{\mathrm{op}}. Because H=Ls−P−EsH=L_s-P-E_s with P≽0P\succeq0, if ν:=νq(Ls)>e\nu:=\nu_q(L_s)>e, the eigenvalue minmax principle implies that HH has at least qq eigenvalues at most −(ν−e)-(\nu-e).

The symmetric matrix HH is entrywise nonnegative. Its largest eigenvalue is at least the absolute value of its smallest eigenvalue: for a unit eigenvector vv associated with the smallest value, entrywise nonnegativity gives |v|TH|v|≥|vTHv||v|^TH|v|\geq|v^THv|, and the Rayleigh principle applies to |v||v|. Consequently HH has, in addition to the qq negative eigenvalues, a positive eigenvalue at least ν−e\nu-e. Hence sq+1(H)≥ν−es_{q+1}(H)\geq\nu-e.

On the other hand, BB has rank at most qq, so

sq+1(H)≤∥H−B∥op=∥Ba∥op≤∥La∥op+∥Ea∥op≤a+e.s_{q+1}(H)\leq\|H-B\|_{\mathrm{op}} =\|B_a\|_{\mathrm{op}} \leq\|L_a\|_{\mathrm{op}}+\|E_a\|_{\mathrm{op}} \leq a+e. \tag{4.9}

Thus ν≤a+2e\nu\leq a+2e. The same inequality is immediate when ν≤e\nu\leq e. Combining it with (4.8), taking the positive part, and squaring proves (4.4). The case K=rK=r is included: then B=0B=0, and any putative ν>e\nu>e already contradicts the displayed eigenvalue comparison. ▫\square

Corollary 4.4 (robust spectral complexity certificate). Suppose 1≤r≤rankR1\leq r\leq\operatorname{rank}R, 1≤q≤m−11\leq q\leq m-1, and νq(Ls)>∥La∥op\nu_q(L_s)>\|L_a\|_{\mathrm{op}}. Any exact-data sequence with a fixed hidden budget and entropy production tending to zero must have budget at least r+q+1r+q+1. For every r≤K≤r+qr\leq K\leq r+q, the nonempty class 𝒞K(d)\mathcal C_K(d) has the explicit lower bound (4.4). Proof. Budgets below rr violate the rank identity. The remaining excluded budgets have a strictly positive right side in (4.4). Singular values, eigenvalues of symmetric matrices, and the operator norm are continuous; PHP_H remains positive on a sufficiently small neighborhood. This proves the stability statement. It asserts continuity of the certificate; it does not assert smaller-budget feasibility of every perturbed datum. ▫\square

Taking r=rankRr=\operatorname{rank}R and L=LTL=L^T recovers Theorem 4.1 with an explicit estimate. Neither certificate is claimed uniformly stronger than the other for nonsymmetric data.

5. A computable certificate for three hidden states

This section assumes exactly two visible states. For an invertible RR, set λ=s2(R)>0\lambda=s_2(R)>0, its smaller singular value. Define

Ls=L+LT2,ν=[−λmin(Ls)]+,β=(L−LT)𝟏,L_s=\frac{L+L^T}{2},\qquad \nu=[-\lambda_{\min}(L_s)]_+,\qquad \beta=(L-L^T)\mathbf1,

Γ(R,L)=λ16PH2[ν−∥β∥2]+2,PH=𝟏TL𝟏.\Gamma(R,L)=\frac{\lambda}{16P_H^2} \big[\nu-\|\beta\|_2\big]_+^2, \qquad P_H=\mathbf1^TL\mathbf1. \tag{5.1}

Theorem 5.1 (observable three-state penalty). Every compatible reciprocal model with at most three hidden states satisfies

σ≥Γ(R,L).\sigma\geq\Gamma(R,L). \tag{5.2}

9. Verification, interpretation, and limits

The accompanying scripts use rational and symbolic arithmetic to check the finite channel identities, balances, determinants, entropy coefficients, and a nonzero submersion Jacobian minor. Numerical evaluations illustrate the certificate and stress-test the committor estimate on balanced nonreversible networks; they are diagnostics, not proofs of universal statements. The mathematical arguments above establish the universal claims.

The substantive conclusions concern model-class bias. A positive reduced-model entropy floor does not by itself lower-bound the true system’s entropy when the true state count may exceed the fitted budget. Our rational example makes that failure explicit even with an exact fit, nonempty reduced class, and a common exit-rate bound on the true-model sequence. The inertia theorem supplies an observable necessary complexity condition; it does not identify the actual microscopic topology.

The assumptions also delimit the result. We use finite stationary irreducible reciprocal chains, instantaneous reversal-even labels, and one aggregate hidden label. We do not cover odd time-reversal variables, nonstationary observations, arbitrary coarse partitions with several unresolved blocks, continuous-state dynamics, or rates with nonreciprocal support. No finite-sample uncertainty theory or experimentally validated estimator is supplied. The original inertia form uses a symmetric first-moment matrix, while Theorem 4.3 handles nonsymmetric moments in every visible dimension through a spectral gap relative to the antisymmetric norm. If that gap vanishes, this certificate gives no positive bound. The exact sharp families of Theorem 7.2 cover every full-rank inertia, but do not classify all compatible data. Full-dimensional feasibility of the smaller-budget class is established here only for the two-visible, three-hidden-state separation. We do not determine the exact value or attainment of I3I_3, and we do not claim the constant 1/161/16 is sharp. Formal verification remains future work.
