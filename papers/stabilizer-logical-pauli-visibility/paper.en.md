## Abstract

A product of random local Pauli bases can wholly erase a logical Pauli label, even when the full measurement setting and outcome are recorded. We determine the exact visibility range of a designated logical direction in every field-linear stabilizer code with $n$ sites of dimension $q$ and $r<n$ stabilizer constraints, for all prime powers $q$. Under independent uniform axes, the lower endpoint is $q^r/(q+1)^n$ and the upper endpoint is $1-(q/(q+1))^{r+1}$. We classify both endpoints, as well as the second-lowest value. For independent biased axes the upper endpoint depends only on the $r+1$ most concentrated site distributions. We classify its extremizers whenever each site has at least two possible axes. Allowing arbitrary correlations among axes cannot improve the worst-case visibility over codes and logical labels. Uniform product sampling is the unique minimax design. For a phase superposition on one encoded qudit with maximally mixed spectators, we derive the exact minimum normalized Fisher information, including singular operating phases, and classify the generic-phase extremizers. We give a quantitative bound on the distance from uniform for near-optimal correlated designs. For two commuting logical qubit labels, joint visibility is NP-complete to decide, and exact setting counts are #P-complete under parsimonious reductions. A single label always has a setting but its exact setting count is #P-complete under metric reductions, and remains #P-complete under Turing reductions for bounded stabilizer weight and incidence.

## 1. Framework and results

Let $q=p^m$ be a prime power, $V=(\mathbb F_q^2)^n$, and
$$[(a,b),(a',b')]=\sum_i(a_i b'_i-b_i a'_i).$$
Let $S\le V$ be isotropic of dimension $r$ with $0\le r<n$, and let $g\in S^\perp\setminus S$. The logical direction is the line generated by $g+S$ in the symplectic quotient $S^\perp/S$. Its $q$ labels are joint characters of the commuting Weyls on that line. Write $A=g+S$ and $D=S+\mathbb F_qg$. A setting consists of a line $M_i\le\mathbb F_q^2$ on each site, with $M=\bigoplus_i M_i$. Write
$$\mathcal H(A)=\{(M_i)_i:A\cap M\ne\varnothing\},\qquad N(A)=|\mathcal H(A)|.$$
Changing $g$ by an element of $S$ or by a nonzero scalar leaves $\mathcal H(A)$ invariant. Full support means all $n$ local components are nonzero. All probabilities in Sections 2 and 3 use independent uniform axes unless otherwise specified.

**Theorem 1 (Minimum and first gap).** The smallest visibility is $q^r/(q+1)^n$. Its equality condition is that every member of $A$ has full support, or, equivalently, $g_i\notin S_i=\operatorname{pr}_i S$ for every $i$. For $1\le r<n$, the second distinct visibility is
$$\frac{(q+1)q^{r-1}}{(q+1)^n}.$$
It occurs precisely when one site is a direct one-site stabilizer factor and the remaining affine coset is full-support. For $r=0$, the count is $(q+1)^z$ with $z$ the number of zero components of $g$. There is a second value if and only if $n\ge2$, and it occurs at $z=1$.

**Theorem 2 (Maximum with biased axes).** Suppose settings are independent, and put $m_i=\max_\ell\Pr(M_i=\ell)$. In decreasing order write $m_{(1)}\ge\cdots\ge m_{(n)}$. The largest visibility over all $S,g$ is
$$1-\prod_{j=1}^{r+1}(1-m_{(j)}).$$
If all $m_i<1$, equality holds exactly for the following configurations. The space $D$ is a direct sum of modal lines on $r+1$ sites whose maxima realize the top $r+1$ values, including ties. The hyperplane $S$ is the kernel of a functional with a nonzero coefficient on every one of these lines. In particular, for uniform axes the maximum is $1-(q/(q+1))^{r+1}$ with this classification for arbitrary local lines. The displayed endpoints are extremal values of a finite set of counts under uniform axes.

**Theorem 3 (Correlated design).** Over all joint laws $\mu$ on the settings, the unique maximizer of $\min_{S,g}\mu(\mathcal H(g+S))$ is the uniform joint law. The value is $q^r/(q+1)^n$.

**Theorem 4 (Logical phase).** In the stabilizer code, select a logical symplectic plane with $X$ and $Z$ representatives $x,z$ normalized by $[x,z]=1$. Fix a logical $Z$ vacuum $|0\rangle$ and additive logical $X$ translations $X(v)$, using $|v\rangle=X(v)|0\rangle$ as the logical computational basis. Prepare $(|0\rangle+e^{i\phi}|1\rangle)/\sqrt2$ in this frame and take the other logical qudits maximally mixed. Write $J$ for ordinary classical Fisher information from the setting and full outcome, with zero-mass terms omitted. The probe quantum Fisher information is one. Under uniform axes, the minimum $J$ over all such codes and planes at generic $\phi$ is $q^{r+1}/(q+1)^n$. At any fixed $\phi$ the exact minimum is
$$\frac{q^{r+1}}{(q+1)^n}
\begin{cases}
1-2/p & p\ \text{odd},\ \phi\in\pi+(2\pi/p)\mathbb Z,\\
1/2 & p=2,\ \phi\in(\pi/2)\mathbb Z,\\
1 & \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}$$
At generic $\phi$ the entire equality class has a local symplectic normal form. Write $X=(1,0)$, $Z=(0,1)$. After local symplectic coordinate changes, the representatives satisfy
$$\begin{aligned}
x&=(X,\ldots,X),\qquad z=(\alpha_i Z)_i,\qquad \sum_i\alpha_i=1,\\
S&=\{(c_i Z)_i:c\in C\},\\
C&\le\{c:\sum_i c_i=0\},\qquad \dim C=r.
\end{aligned}$$
At exceptional phases equality requires exactly the minimum-visibility condition for each transverse line with positive Fisher weight. Recording a uniformly random phase offset yields the generic value uniformly in $\phi$, with the same equality class. With this dither, the uniform joint setting law is also the unique minimax correlated design for phase information.

## 2. Minimum visibility and the second value

We prove $N(A)\ge q^r$ by induction, permitting any affine translate $h+T$ in the class $T$ isotropic and $h\in T^\perp$, including $h\in T$ and $\dim T$ equal to the number of sites. The zero-site case has one setting and one vector. For the inductive step, fix site $i$. Each line $\ell$ gives the affine slice
$$A_\ell=\pi_{\widehat i}\{a\in A:a_i\in\ell\},$$
where $\pi_{\widehat i}$ deletes the component at $i$. Counts add over the nonempty slices. Their direction spaces are isotropic and orthogonal to their affine representatives because the deleted components lie in the same line. If the slice contains zero, it still belongs to the inductive class.

If $S$ has a vector supported only on site $i$, write $\ell_0$ for the line it spans there. Isotropy and $g\perp S$ imply $S_i=\ell_0$ and $g_i\in\ell_0$. Subtracting the available single-site component splits off that line, so $A=\ell_0\times A'$ with residual dimension $r-1$. Every setting at site $i$ is then allowed by taking local component zero. Consequently $N(A)=(q+1)N(A')\ge(q+1)q^{r-1}$.

In the remaining case deletion is injective on each slice, since its kernel intersects $S$ trivially. The local affine image $g_i+S_i$ determines the number and dimensions of nonempty slices.

| Local image | Slice dimensions with multiplicity | Lower bound for count |
|---|---|---:|
| Nonzero point | One of dimension $r$ | $q^r$ |
| Zero point | $q+1$ of dimension $r$ | $(q+1)q^r$ |
| Affine line avoiding zero | $q$ of dimension $r-1$ | $q^r$ |
| Line through zero | One of dimension $r$ and $q$ of dimension $r-1$ | $2q^r$ |
| Plane | $q+1$ of dimension $r-1$ | $(q+1)q^{r-1}$ |

For an affine line avoiding zero, its $q$ points lie in distinct lines through zero. For the plane, restriction of the surjective local map to a prescribed line imposes one independent condition. The remaining rows follow by intersecting the point or line with $\ell$ and taking the inverse image. This closes the induction.

If some local affine image contains zero, the corresponding bound is strictly above $q^r$. Conversely, if every member of $A$ has full support, each determines exactly one setting. Distinct vectors differ at a coordinate where the local image is a line avoiding zero, so they determine distinct settings. Thus $N(A)=|A|=q^r$. To attain equality for arbitrary $r<n$, choose $g=(X,\ldots,X)$ and $S=\{(c_iZ)_i:c\in C\}$ with $C$ any $r$-dimensional subspace in the sum-zero hyperplane. This also gives an equivalent normal form for all minimizers. At sites with a nonzero $S_i$ choose a symplectic basis with $g_i$ as $X$ and $S_i$ spanned by $Z$. At inactive sites complete $g_i$ to such a basis. The pairing with $g$ now forces the sum-zero equation.

For $r\ge1$, the bounds at any coordinate containing a zero show that a nonminimum count is at least $(q+1)q^{r-1}$. The zero-point and through-zero-line rows, without a one-site factor, are strictly higher. The plane row is strictly higher too. Indeed, select $s,t\in S$ with independent images at the plane site $i$. Their nonzero local pairing at $i$ must cancel at other sites because $S$ is isotropic. At least one other site $j$ therefore has rank two. Choose $a\in A$ with $a_j=0$, and any $\ell$ containing $a_i$. The corresponding projected slice has a member with zero at $j$, so its count exceeds $q^{r-1}$. Only the one-site factor can attain the second count. Its product formula gives the stated residual equality criterion, and appending one local line to an extremizer on $n-1$ sites gives existence. For $r=0$, the single representative allows any line at its zero sites and forces one line at all other sites.

## 3. Upper visibility, including independent bias

Let $d=r+1$, and take $\lambda\in D^*$ with $\ker\lambda=S$ and $\lambda(g)=1$. A line generator $u_i$ determines the row $h_i(v)=[u_i,v_i]$ on $D$. Let $K$ be the common kernel of all selected rows. The product setting hits $A$ precisely when $\lambda|_K\ne0$, since a nonzero value can be scaled to $1$. In particular, collecting $d$ independent rows certifies failure.

Reveal rows one site at a time. Suppose $t<d$ independent rows have been revealed at distinct sites. Their common kernel $K$ has a nonzero vector, and no nonzero member of $K$ is supported solely at those sites. To see this, such a member $v$ has $v_i=b_i u_i$ there because $h_i(v)=0$. Isotropy of $D$ makes the functional $[v,\cdot]|_D=\sum_i b_i h_i$ identically zero, contradicting row independence unless $v=0$. Choose an unrevealed site $j$ with nonzero projection of $K$. The new row is independent of the old rows exactly when it does not vanish on $K$. If the local projection of $K$ is a line, the unique bad axis is that line. If it is a plane, no axis is bad. In either case the next step succeeds with conditional probability at least $s_j=1-m_j$.

For a remaining set $R$ of sites and $b$ required independent steps, put $B(R,b)=\min_{|I|=b,\ I\subset R}\prod_{i\in I}s_i$, with $B(R,0)=1$. Conditional induction bounds the chance of success for all $b$ steps below by $B(R,b)$. The first site $j$ has chance at least $s_j$, and the remaining lower bound is at least $B(R\setminus\{j\},b-1)$ for every successful first choice. Their product is at least $B(R,b)$. The axes stay independent at unobserved sites because the algorithm conditions only on previously exposed sites and their axes. Starting with $b=d$ gives the upper bound on visibility.

For attainment take $D$ to be the direct sum of modal lines on $d$ sites with largest maxima. Choose $\lambda$ with every coefficient nonzero, put $S=\ker\lambda$, and let $g$ have $\lambda(g)=1$. In this model a hit occurs exactly when at least one of the $d$ axes matches its modal line. This proves sharpness even if a site has $m_i=1$.

For rigidity assume all $m_i<1$, so $0<s_i<1$. If a site has local projection of $D$ of rank two, expose it first. The first row is always independent. The lower bound on failure becomes $B(R\setminus\{j\},d-1)>B(R,d)$, since $B(R,d)\le s_jB(R\setminus\{j\},d-1)$. Hence equality is impossible. All active sites have local rank one. Choose a basis of $D^*$ from the corresponding nonzero rows, one per site, and let $I$ be its sites. Its full-rank event has probability $\prod_{i\in I}(1-\Pr(M_i=D_i))\ge B(R,d)$. Equality in the visibility bound forces equality in both the product bound by $\prod_{i\in I}s_i$ and its comparison to $B(R,d)$. Thus $I$ has top maxima and every one of its active lines is modal. Were there another active site, its nonzero row would replace some row of this basis. The alternative basis has a positive-probability full-rank event with the replaced row zero. The replaced row is zero with positive probability because its axis is modal, and every other participating row is nonzero with positive probability because no axis is deterministic. This strictly raises failure. Thus exactly $d$ sites are active, and dimension implies $D$ is their full direct sum. For this direct sum the actual failure probability is the product of the nonmatching probabilities over the support of $\lambda$. All factors are strictly between zero and one on the modal sites, so omitting a coordinate gives strictly greater failure. This proves the classification.

## 4. Uniqueness among correlated settings

Let $\Omega$ be the setting space and let $G=\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb F_q)^n$ act by local coordinates. It acts transitively on $\Omega$ and preserves the family of $(S,g)$ under consideration. A fixed minimum hit set $B$ has $q^r$ elements. For any setting law $\mu$, averaging $\mu(hB)$ over $h\in G$ gives $q^r/|\Omega|$. This bounds the worst-case performance above. Uniform axes attain the bound by Theorem 1.

For uniqueness let $\mu$ attain the bound. Then $\mu(hB)=q^r/|\Omega|$ for every $h\in G$, since each is at least the orbit average. Choose an axis $a_i$ missed by $B$ at every site. This is possible because the minimum cosets have local images of size at most $q$ and avoid zero. The stabilizer of $a_i$ in $\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb F_q)$ acts transitively on all other $q$ axes. In coordinates with $a_i$ at infinity this is the group action containing all translations. Averaging $1_B$ over the product of these stabilizers gives $q^{r-n}$ times the indicator of the complement box $\prod_i\{\ell:\ell\ne a_i\}$. Applying the same identity in every $G$-translate shows that *every* complement box has mass $(q/(q+1))^n$. The linear map from masses on settings to complement-box masses is the $n$-fold tensor power of $J_{q+1}-I_{q+1}$, where $J_{q+1}$ is the all-ones matrix. The local eigenvalues are $q$ on constants and $-1$ on their orthogonal complement. The map is invertible over the reals, so $\mu$ is uniform.

## 5. Connection between visibility and the quantum measurement

We give the measurement reduction explicitly, including prime powers of characteristic two. Let $\chi(a)=\exp(2\pi i\,\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb F_q/\mathbb F_p}(a)/p)$. On the basis $|v\rangle$, $v\in\mathbb F_q$, use Weyl operators $W(a,b)|v\rangle=\chi(bv)|v+a\rangle$. Products of Weyls have commutation phase determined by the field pairing and $\chi$. Nonzero Weyl labels are traceless. On an isotropic $\mathbb F_q$-space $R$, the Weyls commute and can be rephased to a representation of its additive group. Choose an $\mathbb F_p$-basis of $R$, rephase its Weyls to have order dividing $p$, and extend by commuting products. This construction works also when $p=2$. For each character $\eta$ of $R$ its projector is
$$P_{R,\eta}=|R|^{-1}\sum_{v\in R}\overline{\eta(v)}\,\widehat W_R(v).$$
Its trace is $q^n/|R|$ by Weyl orthogonality. The code is any fixed character sector of $S$. Since $S$ is field-linear, every $v\notin S^\perp$ has some $s\in S$ with nontrivial trace commutation phase, by scaling $s$ in the field. Its Weyl therefore has zero compression to the code. On $S^\perp$ the compressed Weyls act up to scalar as the logical Weyls of $S^\perp/S$. One can construct a logical computational basis by choosing a symplectic complement of $S$ in $S^\perp$, fixing the commuting logical $Z$ eigenvalues, and generating their one-dimensional joint eigenspaces by the logical $X$ translations. Every eigenspace has dimension one by the projector trace formula applied to $S$ plus the logical $Z$ space. This identifies the code with $k=n-r$ logical qudits, with any given nondegenerate plane $E$ as one logical qudit and its symplectic complement as the spectators.

The complete product basis measurement at setting $M$ measures the characters of $M$. Its labels compatible with the code character on $K_0=M\cap S$ form a coset of the characters of $M/K_0$. Here phase choices for $M$ and $S$ only relabel compatibility. Set $b=\dim K_0$. The spaces $M\cap S^\perp$ and
$$H=((M\cap S^\perp)+S)/S$$
have dimensions $n-r+b$ and $k$. Indeed the pairing $S$ against $M$ has kernel $K_0$, since $M^\perp=M$. Dualizing the pairing gives the first dimension, and quotienting out $K_0$ gives the second. The space $H$ is thus logical Lagrangian. In the projector expansion for physical outcomes, terms outside $M\cap S^\perp$ vanish on the code, and the remaining summands group into classes with common factor $q^b$. Grouping yields a logical character of $H$ for every compatible physical label. Terms whose quotient class is outside $E$ have zero expectation in the maximally mixed spectators. The visible probe subgroup is consequently $H\cap E$, either zero or a single line since $E$ is a symplectic plane.

If this intersection is zero, the $q^{n-b}$ compatible physical labels are uniform with probabilities $q^{b-n}$, independent of the probe. Otherwise measurement of a selected logical line corresponds to its spectral probabilities $p_a$ repeated $q^{n-b-1}$ times with physical probabilities $q^{1+b-n}p_a$. This follows from the projector sum on that line and the equal number of extensions of each character to $M/K_0$, after the phase relabeling. Thus the intermediate repetitions preserve Fisher information and other inference based on the logical outcome. The line of $g+S$ belongs to $H$ exactly when $A$ hits. If one encodes any of the $q$ joint character labels of this line with maximally mixed spectators, the fixed-setting channel either reveals the full label or outputs a law independent of it. In a setting that measures a distinct logical line, its overlaps with the joint eigenbasis of $\{W(tg):t\in\mathbb F_q\}$ are uniform by the Weyl character sum. Including the setting hence gives an erasure experiment with reveal probability $\mu(\mathcal H(A))$.

As a comparison, if one may use a uniformly random *global* Lagrangian measurement, the induced $H$ is uniform on logical Lagrangians. The group preserving $S$ realizes all symplectic transformations on its quotient by extending any such transformation on a symplectic complement. Transitivity on logical Lagrangians and on nonzero logical vectors then gives the visibility $(q^k-1)/(q^{2k}-1)=1/(q^k+1)$ for each logical direction. This comparison uses a different setting space from Theorem 3.

## 6. Exact phase information, equality, and dither

The $Z$ line sees only the phase-independent probabilities of the computational labels. Each other line of $E$ has a representative $x+t z$, $t\in\mathbb F_q$. For slope $t$, the eigenvectors in the logical $Z$ basis have amplitudes $q^{-1/2}h_t(v)\chi(av)$, $a\in\mathbb F_q$, with
$$h_t(0)=1,\qquad h_t(v+w)=h_t(v)h_t(w)\chi(tvw).$$
For odd $p$ take $h_t(v)=\chi(tv^2/2)$. For $p=2$, choose an $\mathbb F_2$ basis $(e_i)$, choose $h_t(e_i)$ with $h_t(e_i)^2=\chi(te_i^2)$, and extend to $\sum_i c_i e_i$ by the product of basis phases raised to $c_i$ and the factor $\prod_{i<j}\chi(t e_i e_j)^{c_i c_j}$. The resulting identity follows by expanding the product, using $h_t(e_i)^2=\chi(te_i^2)$ for a repeated basis element. Under $W(u,t u)$ these vectors are eigenvectors because translating $v$ by $u$ makes the ratio of coefficients independent of $v$ by the displayed identity. Alternate scalar Weyl conventions just relabel the eigenvectors. The phase probe probabilities are
$$p_{t,a}(\phi)=q^{-1}\big[1+\operatorname{Re}(e^{i\phi}\overline{h_t(1)\chi(a)})\big].$$
The character $\chi(a)$ takes every $p$-th root of unity $q/p$ times. If all probabilities are positive, using $\sin^2 u/(1+\cos u)=1-\cos u$ and summing characters gives $J_t(\phi)=1$. At any zero, exactly $q/p$ outcomes are omitted, each with extended summand $2/q$, so $J_t(\phi)=1-2/p$ in ordinary score convention. For odd $p$ the exceptional set is $\pi+(2\pi/p)\mathbb Z$ for every slope. For $p=2$ the relation $h_t(1)^2=\chi(t)=(-1)^{\mathrm{Tr}(t)}$ gives $\pi\mathbb Z$ for trace-zero slopes and $\pi/2+\pi\mathbb Z$ for trace-one slopes. Both groups have size $q/2$. The probabilities and singularities here are those of the physical probe defined in the same logical computational convention.

For any axis distribution $\mu$ independent of $\phi$, the full Fisher information equals
$$J(\phi)=\sum_{t\in\mathbb F_q}J_t(\phi)\,\mu(\mathcal H(x+t z+S)).$$
The hit events for these slopes are disjoint, since the associated logical lines have nonzero mutual pairing. Uniform axes give the lower bound by Theorem 1 for every term, and the sum of weights gives exactly the three cases stated in Theorem 4. The quantum Fisher information of the pure phase probe is $4(\|\partial_\phi\psi\|^2-|\langle\psi,\partial_\phi\psi\rangle|^2)=1$. A fixed maximally mixed spectator does not change it, as the symmetric logarithmic derivative can be tensored with spectator identity.

To attain all termwise bounds, take $x=(X,\ldots,X)$, $z=(Z,0,\ldots,0)$ and any $r$-dimensional sum-zero subspace on the first $r+1$ sites for $S$. Every $x+t z+S$ consists of full-support vectors. Positivity of the weights identifies equality at a specified phase exactly as stated. When all weights are one, simultaneous extremality requires $x_i+t z_i\notin S_i$ for every $i,t$. If $S_i$ has rank one this means $x_i\notin S_i$ and $z_i\in S_i$, because a nonzero $z_i$ in the quotient by $S_i$ spans it. If $S_i=0$, it means $x_i\ne0$ and $z_i$ is either zero or independent of $x_i$. Rank two is excluded. Choose local symplectic changes taking each $x_i$ to $X$ and $S_i$ or the nonzero line of $z_i$ to the line of $Z$ as needed. The defining pairings then give $\sum_i\alpha_i=1$ and $C$ in the sum-zero hyperplane. Conversely this normal form makes all slope cosets full-support.

Apply an independent uniform phase offset $u$ modulo $2\pi$ at the probe and record it alongside the setting and outcome. Conditional information at phase $\phi+u$ has every slope weight one for almost all $u$. Hence the average information is the generic formula at every operating point. To prove minimax uniqueness for dithered information, take the attaining example above. The union of its disjoint transverse hit sets has $q^{r+1}$ elements and avoids the $Z$ axis at each coordinate. The orbit average is $q^{r+1}/(q+1)^n$, and every translate has that mass for an optimal law. The complement-box averaging and invertibility proof of Section 4 apply with the cardinality $q^{r+1}$. This yields the same unique uniform joint law.

## 7. Scope, related work, and replication

Local uniform Pauli measurements of GHZ phase and shallow-readout bounds motivate the model. The related GHZ study uses a one-word matching estimate for Clifford distinguishers and a particular cat-block construction. Our quantifiers instead range over every field-linear stabilizer rank, arbitrary logical gauge dimension, and arbitrary designated logical direction or plane. The full-support construction recovers the familiar $(2/3)^n$ qubit GHZ phase value at $r=n-1$, while the converse proves that it is exactly the minimum over all such stabilizer encodings. The maximal-visibility theorem, its rigidity and bias dependence, and the correlated minimax design concern different quantities from circuit depth. We do not address the sharp circuit-depth constant for a one-dimensional chain.

Sections 1–7 treat field-linear codes over prime powers. Section 9 considers qubit complexity with one or two designated labels, and records the nearest sources on local grouping, alternative representatives, and counting. Exact finite enumeration supplements the proof. The enumeration uses direct Pauli axis matching for every isotropic space in the indicated small prime dimensions and explicitly checks the first gap, both endpoint classes, and the phase count where listed. All theorems above are stated for field-linear stabilizers; general additive codes, general POVMs, measurement feedback, and gate noise are outside these quantifiers. Lean verification is tracked separately.

## 8. Quantitative rigidity of the minimax design

Let $L=(q+1)^n$ be the number of settings, let $u$ be the uniform design, and put $p_*=q^r/L$. If the design $\mu$ gives visibility at least $p_*-\varepsilon$ on every admissible code and designated logical direction, with $\varepsilon\ge0$, then
$$
 \|\mu-u\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
 \le \min\left\{1,\frac12Lq^{n-r}\big[(2-1/q)^n+q^{-n}\big]\varepsilon\right\}.
$$
To see this, fix a forbidden axis at each site. Average the extremal visibility indicator in the proof of Theorem 3 over the local group stabilizing each forbidden axis. The result is $q^{r-n}$ times the indicator of the box of settings avoiding all forbidden axes: the group is transitive on that box, and the extremal visibility set it averages has size $q^r$ out of $q^n$ settings in the box. Every translated extremal instance has the required lower guarantee. Thus each such box has probability under $\mu$ at least its uniform mass minus $a=q^{n-r}\varepsilon$.

The vector of box-mass differences is $v=(J-I)^{\otimes n}(\mu-u)$, where $J$ is the local all-ones matrix. The entries of $v+a\mathbf{1}$ are nonnegative and sum to $La$, as $v$ has sum zero. The local inverse is $J/q-I$, whose absolute column sum is $2-1/q$ and which acts on constants by $1/q$. Apply the tensor inverse to $(v+a\mathbf{1})-a\mathbf{1}$ and take half its $\ell^1$ norm. This yields the bound and gives a quantitative statement for the same correlated-design scope as Theorem 3. Sharp dependence on the size of the system remains open.

## 9. Decision and counting for one or two designated qubit labels

Consider qubit stabilizers in $\mathbb F_2^{2n}$ with its standard symplectic form. The input consists of an isotropic stabilizer space $S$ given by generators and either one nonzero logical label $g+S$ or two commuting labels $g_1+S,g_2+S$ linearly independent in $S^\perp/S$. A product Pauli setting is a tuple of one-dimensional subspaces $M_i\le\mathbb F_2^2$, one of three axes at each site, with $M=\prod_i M_i$. It sees $g+S$ if $M\cap(g+S)\ne\varnothing$. Let $N_1$ count settings seeing a designated label and $N_2$ count settings seeing both designated labels. Under independently uniform axes, their visibility probabilities are exactly $N_1/3^n$ and $N_2/3^n$.

**Theorem 5.** A single designated label always has a compatible setting. For two designated commuting labels which are independent in the logical quotient, testing whether there exists a compatible setting is NP-complete, and counting compatible ordered pairs of representatives is #P-complete under parsimonious reductions, each with bounded-weight and bounded-incidence stabilizer checks. In addition, $N_2$ is #P-complete under parsimonious reductions, already for stabilizer generators of bounded weight and bounded number incident to each qubit. $N_1$ is #P-complete under polynomial-time metric reductions, using unrestricted generators. On stabilizer generators of bounded weight and bounded incidence, $N_1$ is #P-complete under polynomial-time Turing reductions. The boundedness conditions refer to the stabilizer checks, with no bound imposed on the weight of a designated logical representative.

Each distinct physical setting is counted once. Positivity of $N_2$ belongs to NP by guessing one setting and performing the intersection checks below. The ordered-representative count belongs to #P by guessing two stabilizer coefficient vectors in a basis and checking local linear dependence at every site. Choosing a basis avoids multiplicity from redundant generators. For membership in #P, guess one axis per site, with exactly one accepting encoding per axis, and test each designated coset intersection by binary linear algebra. In particular, one may check whether $g$ lies in the span of $S$ and the independent local axis vectors of $M$. All input promises are also checkable by linear algebra. Exact evaluation of the rational uniform visibility is computationally equivalent to evaluation of the corresponding integer count, since the conversion factor $3^n$ has bit length $O(n)$.

### 9.1. Two labels under a parsimonious reduction

Use an acyclic Boolean circuit with AND, XOR, constants, and a designated output fixed at one. Introduce a constant wire if necessary, so the circuit has at least one wire. AND and XOR with constants suffice to simulate arbitrary Boolean circuits. Replacing high fan-out by binary copy trees enforces bounded uses per wire without changing the count, since every full wire assignment is uniquely determined by the input. Counting satisfying inputs of such circuits is #P-complete under parsimonious reductions: compile the polynomial-time predicate of a #P verifier on its fixed-length witness bits to a Boolean circuit, preserving one satisfying input per accepted witness.

Let $D$ have basis $G,H$ and $E_j,F_j$ for each wire $j$. Superscript $*$ denotes its dual basis. A site labeled $(\alpha,\beta)$ sends $d\in D$ to $(\alpha(d),\beta(d))\in\mathbb F_2^2$. Include every described site twice. The duplicate pair cancels its contribution to the global symplectic form. For every wire include the two pin sites $(G^*,E_j^*)$ and $(H^*,F_j^*)$. They ensure that the map of $D$ into the physical vector space is injective. The abstract stabilizer is the span of all $E_j,F_j$, and logical representatives are $G,H$, all interpreted using that embedding. Thus the resulting space is isotropic and the two logical labels commute and are independent modulo the checks.

A representative $u=G+s_1$ and a representative $v=H+s_2$, with $s_1,s_2$ in the abstract stabilizer, fit at one site precisely when the matrix with column vectors $(\alpha(u),\beta(u))^\mathsf T$, $(\alpha(v),\beta(v))^\mathsf T$ has determinant zero. The constraints are as follows.

- The two pins require $E_j^*(v)=F_j^*(u)=0$. Write $x_j=E_j^*(u)$ and $y_j=F_j^*(v)$.
- A site $(E_j^*+F_j^*,G^*+H^*)$ gives the determinant $x_j+y_j$, forcing the two copies to agree.
- For $j=i\land k$ use $(E_i^*+F_j^*,G^*+F_k^*)$. Its matrix is $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}x_i&y_j\\1&y_k\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, so $y_j=x_i y_k$.
- For a constraint $\sum_i a_i x_i=b$ with a constant number of nonzero coefficients, use $(\sum_i a_i E_i^*+bG^*,H^*)$. Its determinant is $\sum_i a_i x_i+b$. This includes XOR, constants, equality on copy wires and the fixed output.

This gives a bijection between satisfying inputs and locally compatible representative pairs, proving both claimed hardness results for pairs and existence. It also gives a bijection with physical settings. Both pin types have at least one nonzero column, since $G^*(u)=H^*(v)=1$. The copy-equality matrix has second row $(1,1)$, the AND matrix has a one in its lower left corner, and an affine constraint has a one in its lower right corner. Consequently a compatible pair requires a unique common axis at each site, including the duplicates. Conversely, every compatible setting has some representative pair, and the axis at $(G^*,E_j^*)$ is the line generated by $(1,x_j)$, which recovers every wire bit. The recovered bits fix the pair and its setting. Each check acts only on its wire pins, equality or defining site, and a bounded number of fan-out sites. Each site contains at most a constant number of check coordinates. The reduction is parsimonious and preserves bounded check weight and incidence.

### 9.2. One label using a single query

Let $G_{\mathrm{gr}}$ be a simple graph on $m\ge1$ vertices. Take an abstract basis $G_0,E_1,\ldots,E_m$ and stabilizer $\operatorname{span}\{E_i:1\le i\le m\}$. The designated logical label is the coset of $G_0$. Local sites again come in identical pairs. Include a pin $(G_0^*,E_i^*)$ for every $i$. Then the embedding is injective and isotropic. In particular, the representative indexed by $x\in\{0,1\}^m$ uses the nonzero pin vector $(1,x_i)$. Distinct representatives are compatible with disjoint sets of physical settings, since they disagree on some pin axis.

For each graph edge $ij$, take $R=m+1$ pairs of sites labeled $(G_0^*+E_i^*,G_0^*+E_j^*)$. On representative $x$, a site pair gives nine axis choices if $x_i=x_j=1$, and one otherwise. The two sites can choose their axes independently even though the representative values agree on them. Thus
$$
 N_1=\sum_{x\in\{0,1\}^m}9^{R e(G_{\mathrm{gr}}[x])},
$$
where $G_{\mathrm{gr}}[x]$ is the induced subgraph on vertices with $x_i=1$. The residue modulo $9^R$ equals the number of independent sets, since every independent set contributes one and their count is at most $2^m<9^R$. This is one oracle call followed by a polynomial-time exact transformation, a metric reduction. For instance the empty graph gives $2^m$, and a single edge on two vertices gives $3+9^3$. Valiant's problem 7 counts satisfying assignments of a monotone 2-CNF, namely vertex covers of the clause graph, which are complements of independent sets. [Valiant, The Complexity of Enumeration and Reliability Problems](https://www.math.cmu.edu/~af1p/Teaching/MCC17/Papers/enumerate.pdf)

For the reduction type, the following direct argument shows that #P reduces to counting independent sets by one call. Compile a verifier circuit to a 3-CNF with unique auxiliary wires, using clauses enforcing each gate's truth table and the designated output. Form one clique of two vertices per Boolean variable and one clique per clause, with a vertex for each satisfying assignment of that clause's variables. Connect vertices from distinct cliques whenever their local assignments are inconsistent on a common variable. If there are $k$ cliques, independent sets of size $k$ are in bijection with satisfying assignments, since they select one vertex from each clique. Given the resulting $q$-vertex graph, replace each vertex by $q+1$ nonadjacent clones, and each edge by all cross edges between its clone groups. Put $W=2^{q+1}-1$. If $a_j$ is the original number of independent sets of size $j$, the new graph has $\sum_j a_j W^j$ independent sets. Each $a_j<W$ since it is at most $2^q$. Extracting the $k$th base-$W$ digit returns the satisfying count. All steps use polynomial size and one oracle answer. Composing the two metric reductions proves #P-completeness in the stated sense.

### 9.3. One label with bounded-weight, bounded-incidence checks

To keep weights bounded, first replace each repeated graph edge factor from §9.2 by an edge joining a fresh variable copy at either endpoint. Give isolated graph vertices one copy. Give each copy a pin and connect the copies of each original vertex by an equality chain. We implement equality via interpolation. In the following matrices, the two row and column values are $0,1$, and each entry counts axis choices after fixing the endpoint bits.

A duplicated site pair $(G_0^*+E_i^*,G_0^*+E_j^*)$ is the factor $M_0$ below. The same pair together with a duplicated pair $(E_i^*,E_j^*)$ makes the factor $M_1$, since the latter additional pair has nine choices at $(0,0)$ and one elsewhere. Thus
$$
 M_0=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&9\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 M_1=\begin{pmatrix}9&1\\1&9\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Every variable, including internal variables introduced next, is represented by its own check coordinate $E_i$ and has its own duplicated pin; pins give weight one and make the setting sets of different assignments disjoint.

Let $L$ be the total number of equality edges. For $k=1,\ldots,L+1$, replace each equality edge by a path of $k$ factors $M_1$, with new internal variables and pins. Summing over the internal bits gives $M_1^k$, whose diagonal and off-diagonal entries are respectively
$$
 A_k=\frac{10^k+8^k}{2},\qquad B_k=\frac{10^k-8^k}{2}.
$$
The exact count $N_k$ supplied by the resulting instance therefore obeys
$$
 N_k/A_k^L=P(B_k/A_k),\qquad
 P(z)=\sum_x 9^{b(x)}z^{d(x)}.
$$
Here $x$ runs over the endpoint-copy assignments before subdividing equality links, $b(x)$ counts the $M_0$ factors whose two endpoints are one, and $d(x)$ counts equality links whose endpoints disagree. The polynomial has degree at most $L$. The ratios $B_k/A_k$ are strictly increasing in $k$, since $(8/10)^k$ strictly decreases. Exact rational interpolation from these $L+1$ points gives $P(0)$, whose assignments have all copies of each original vertex equal. Hence $P(0)$ is the weighted sum of §9.2, and reduction modulo $9^R$ recovers the independent-set count. When $L=0$, one call gives the constant directly.

The number of queries and total construction size are polynomial in the graph size; paths have polynomial length and both count answers and interpolation rationals have polynomial bit lengths. Each variable occurs in at most one $M_0$ factor and at most two $M_1$ factors, also after subdivisions. Its stabilizer generator therefore has weight at most $2+2+2\cdot4=12$, including its pin. At most two stabilizer generators are incident to any one physical site. The distinguished representative $G_0$ may have high weight. This proves Turing hardness for the bounded-check class.

### 9.4. Literature boundary and verification

The two-label decision and ordered-representative results concern the existence or number of compatible pairs. The physical setting theorem uses the injective pin axes and the absence of unconstrained sites for compatible pairs. In the one-label problem a compatible setting always exists: from any representative, use its unique axis where nonzero and an arbitrary axis elsewhere. The counting result therefore separates positivity from exact evaluation in the same instance model. The finite uniform minimum and maximum values in the original case range over codes and labels; computing the value for a given instance is a different task.

Fixed-list qubit-wise Pauli grouping tests local commutation of given representative words. Stabilizer disjointness also varies representatives but optimizes overlap or congestion among collections of such representatives; Bostanci and Kubica, Theorem 1, prove NP-completeness of fixed-parameter disjointness. Chen et al., Definition 1, use qubit-wise grouping to measure fixed stabilizer checks. The counting objects here are physical settings whose product spaces meet prescribed cosets. [Qubit-wise Pauli grouping](https://doi.org/10.1088/2058-9565/ad9d74) · [Jochym-O'Connor et al.](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevX.8.021047) · [Bostanci–Kubica, Theorem 1](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevResearch.3.043192) · [Chen et al., Definition 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.12551)

The weight-enumerator and two-spin partition-function literature already contains hard exact sums over codewords or spins. Iyer–Poulin's degenerate maximum-likelihood decoding sums error weights within a prescribed coset. Graph subdivision and interpolation in a two-state transfer matrix are standard tools. A direct sum of representative compatibility counts can generally overcount physical settings, because several representatives of a single coset may belong to the same setting. Here the pins guarantee that those setting sets are disjoint, while the constraint matrices realize the required weights with a stabilizer embedding. [Iyer–Poulin, degenerate decoding](https://arxiv.org/abs/1310.3235) · [Vyalyi, binary linear code weight enumerators](https://arxiv.org/abs/cs/0304044) · [Bulatov–Grohe, matrix partition functions](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tcs.2005.09.011) · [Jaeger–Vertigan–Welsh, Tutte evaluations](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0305004100068936)
