Universal convex-order bounds for coalescent tree lengths and exact merger-cost criteria

Collaborative research manuscript · 26 November 2026

Abstract

Let LnΞL_n^\Xi be the total tree length of the nn-sample, time-homogeneous Ξ\Xi-coalescent, normalized so that each pair of ancestral blocks merges at rate one. We prove the sharp comparison LnK≤icxLnΞ≤icxnE,E∼Exp(1), L_n^{\mathrm K}\ \le_{\mathrm{icx}}\ L_n^\Xi\ \le_{\mathrm{icx}}\ nE, \qquad E\sim\operatorname{Exp}(1), for every characteristic probability measure Ξ\Xi on the infinite simplex and every finite n≥2n\ge2. Here ≤icx\le_{\mathrm{icx}} denotes increasing convex order and LnKL_n^{\mathrm K} is Kingman’s total tree length. Thus the comparison holds simultaneously for every stop-loss threshold and every increasing convex loss, including when disjoint groups merge simultaneously. Its mean specialization for Λ\Lambda-coalescents proves the finite-sample extremality statement formulated by Der and Plotkin. The stronger comparison gives sharp bounds for moments of every real order at least one and for mutation counts on a fully linked genealogy. Within the Λ\Lambda subclass, equality of a positive-threshold stop-loss transform identifies the corresponding extremal measure when n≥3n\ge3.

The main additional ingredient is a harmonic occupancy inequality for every paintbox, obtained from a convex-order comparison for random circular differences. An arc-mass variance identity gives a nonnegative-square certificate for that comparison. We also characterize exactly the state costs for which the same two extremal mechanisms bound all finite chains under a pair-merger budget, both with single merging groups and with arbitrary simultaneous partitions. These conditions are finite linear systems in the cost vector, and failure produces an explicit mean counterexample. For costs cb=bαc_b=b^\alpha, the sharp two-sided threshold changes from α=1\alpha=1 for single groups to α=log2/log(4/3)\alpha=\log 2/\log(4/3) for arbitrary simultaneous partitions. A four-sample counterexample explains why the ordinary tree-length theorem requires stronger structure than normalized pair rates and consistency only through a fixed sample size.

1. Scope and relation to earlier work

The Λ\Lambda-coalescent permits any number of ancestral blocks to merge into one, while two disjoint merging groups do not merge simultaneously [P99]. Its finite-sample rates, its consistency as the sample changes, and its relation to population models are established parts of coalescent theory. We use these rates without claiming a new construction of the process.

Der and Plotkin [DP14] studied equilibrium diversity under reproductive skew. Their recursion for expected diversity, pure-measure formulas and asymptotic results led them to conjecture that the Wright-Fisher and star models minimize and maximize diversity for every finite sample size. The explicit finite-sample conjecture appears on PDF pages 14–15 of their author manuscript [DP13]; the numbering differs from the journal version. We address the probability-measure normalization used there. Existing formulas for four-sample diversity are not new results of this paper.

The contribution proved here is a comparison of the entire family of stop-loss transforms for every normalized Ξ\Xi-coalescent, together with exact cost criteria in two larger finite rate-budget classes. The Ξ\Xi construction and its paintbox representation are due to earlier work [S00]; we use them without claiming a new characterization of exchangeable coalescents. The comparison implies the finite-sample mean bounds, but mean bounds alone do not imply the stronger statement. The harmonic occupancy estimate and its random circular representation supply the additional constraint that arbitrary finite simultaneous partitions lack.

Earlier work gives asymptotic sampling formulas and limits for tree lengths [BBL14, K12, DK19]. General expected site-frequency-spectrum recursions and inference questions are treated in [BBE13, SKS16]. These works concern different questions from a uniform finite-sample order comparison. Our argument does not replace their asymptotic or inferential results. The exponential-maximum representation of Kingman’s total length is classical [DDS08]; the comparison with other merger mechanisms, rather than this representation, is the result at issue here.

Generator and positive-semigroup comparison are classical tools [M87]. Phase-type extremality has also been studied under other constraints, including fixed order and mean [OC91] and restrictions on generator column sums [HZV13]. Control of remaining absorption times is a classical route to exponential bounds [FK06]. The present cost criteria specify when these mechanisms apply under a row-wise pair budget; the contribution is not the general semigroup identity or exponential comparison principle. The full text of [OC91] was not available in the literature check, so exclusion of every result in that paper is not claimed.

The circular arc-variance identity used below is an elementary instance of discrepancy–intersection-energy identities associated with Stolarsky invariance; see the fixed-cap comparison in [HBZO19, Theorem 3]. We prove the exact weighted circle form needed here. The additional bridge is the random cyclic representation of the paintbox measure in (7.5), followed by the missing-mass recursion and the coalescent comparison; novelty of the general discrepancy identity is not claimed.

The literature check found no equivalent theorem in the sources and searches described with this manuscript. This is a bounded literature check, not a proof of priority. In particular, the full proof of every result in the broader coalescent and stochastic-comparison literature has not been checked. This manuscript has not undergone external peer review. Its arguments are written mathematical proofs; they have not been checked by a proof assistant.

6.1. A consistent family through four samples

For four labelled blocks, give each of the three perfect matchings event rate one, merging its two pairs simultaneously. From three blocks use binary mergers at rate one per pair, and from two blocks merge at rate one. Restriction to any three labels turns the three matching events into the three possible pair mergers, each at rate one. Pair restrictions also have rate one; invisible mergers do not change that rate. Thus this family is exchangeable and consistent for sample sizes at most four.

Nevertheless, for ordinary length, 𝔼L4=43+2=103<113=𝔼L4K. \mathbb EL_4=\frac43+2=\frac{10}{3} \lt \frac{11}{3}=\mathbb EL_4^{\mathrm K}. \tag{6.5} This does not extend to an infinite Ξ\Xi-coalescent. The representation in [S00] would therefore force every nonbinary paintbox component to vanish, whereas a purely binary component cannot create disjoint simultaneous pairs. Infinite consistency imposes a substantive additional constraint. The next section uses that constraint to restore the ordinary tree-length lower bound.

8. Finite verification and limitations

The mathematical proofs above cover the stated infinite families. A separate reproducible calculation checks exact rate normalization, the finite cost inequalities and their counterexamples, rational first and second moments, and numerical stop-loss transforms from the cost-time matrix exponential. Finite calculations are diagnostics and are not substitutes for the proofs.

The cost calculations use rational arithmetic for 517 normalized Λ\Lambda rows, 1,296 positive integer cost vectors, and 15,598 explicit mean witnesses for failed inequalities. Numerical cost-time checks cover 23,783 state–threshold combinations. The paintbox calculations separately compare three constructions of μp\mu_p for six mass vectors, check the arc-variance identity at 75 thresholds by exact interval integration, and cross-check 190 occupancy distributions using two algorithms. They also test 54 finite paintbox/binary mixtures and 1,881 numerical state–threshold combinations. The scripts, exact counts, seeds and tolerances accompany the manuscript; the numerical tests do not certify arbitrary real parameters or replace the countable-paintbox limit argument.

The following restrictions are material:

  • The coalescent theorem assumes a time-homogeneous characteristic probability measure. A change of physical time or mutation scale changes the numerical bounds.
  • The classes 𝒬N\mathcal Q_N and 𝒫N\mathcal P_N allow arbitrary rows and are not characterizations of infinitely consistent coalescents. Section 6.1 shows why this distinction matters.
  • The necessary and sufficient cost criteria concern every array in their specified finite class and every initial state. Necessity for a single characteristic measure is not proved.
  • The Ξ\Xi theorem concerns continuous-time coalescent limits. It does not assert an exact finite-population Cannings result, or a result for nonconstant demographies or selection.
  • Ordinary stochastic order, general variance minimization, and identifiability of the full reproductive measure are outside the proved claims.
  • The count-distribution application uses one linked genealogy and must not be substituted for the independent-site distribution in the original diversity paper.
