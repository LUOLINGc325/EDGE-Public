Exact causal quantum log-loss at short horizons and a Gram-matrix reduction

Abstract

In sequential prediction of a quantum state under logarithmic loss, the learner must announce a density matrix before the next effect is revealed. We reduce the exact finite-horizon minimax value to a recursion on the Gram matrix of the preceding rank-one effects. At a history of rank rr, the recursion needs only a distribution in ℂr\mathbb C^r with a prescribed second moment and at most r2+1r^2+1 support points. It gives an exact algebraic computation whose size, at a fixed horizon, is independent of the ambient matrix dimension. The three-round value is d3((1+w)/3)3d^3((1+w)/3)^3, where w>d−1/2w>d^{-1/2} solves w3−3w/d−2/d2=0w^3-3w/d-2/d^2=0. We give optimal forecasts and an attaining sequence of measurements. For d≥3d\ge3 this value is strictly below the best static tensor bound, even when that tensor need only dominate likelihoods on product effects. The values agree for d=2d=2. At fixed horizon the first correction as dd increases is given by a dimension-independent adaptive covariance problem on complex correlation matrices. Its coefficient is 333\sqrt{3} at three rounds, compared with 66 for the best static tensor bound.

Guide for readers

On each round the learner announces a density matrix, which is a positive semidefinite matrix of trace one. In an experiment, an effect is one positive operator in a collection summing to the identity. The Born rule gives the probability of its outcome as the trace of the effect times the state. For example, the rank-one projector onto a unit vector has probability 1/d1/d in the uniform state I/dI/d. In the regret ratio, each effect can be rescaled because its scale multiplies the learner and hindsight likelihoods equally. A proper forecast uses a single density matrix for all the effects that could be supplied next. This ensures that its outcome probabilities sum to one for each possible measurement.

The quantity Cd,TC_{d,T} is a ratio of likelihoods. Its logarithm is the excess cumulative loss over the best single state chosen after seeing the entire history. Sections 2 and 3 turn the requirement to cover every continuation of a history into a minimization over forecast matrices and an equivalent maximization over measurements. The Gram matrix lists the inner products of the earlier directions. It suffices to track those inner products and the projection of the next direction into their span.

When the number of rounds TT is fixed, the resulting computation uses a fixed number of real variables and treats the ambient dimension dd as an input. Section 4 gives a closed formula at three rounds, and Section 5 determines the first correction as dd increases with TT held fixed. These estimates leave open the joint growth rate when the number of rounds also increases. Section 6 contrasts causal forecasts with a single tensor that must bound the likelihood on every product of measurement outcomes.

7. Relation to earlier work

Tseng, Chen, Xiao and Li study proper state forecasts and give large-horizon regret guarantees via VB-FTRL. Li and Tseng’s Theorem 1.1 excludes dimension exponents below two in guarantees of the form O(dγlogT)O(d^\gamma\log T) at sufficiently large horizons. Their Remark 3.4 leaves the exact minimax rate open. Our algorithm treats the finite-horizon value and does not provide their style of efficient large-horizon guarantee. The dimension-free moment transition and fixed-horizon coefficient refer to the original order of play with the full density-state comparator. ALT paper · Li–Tseng preprint

Liu, Attias and Roy’s contextual Shtarkov sum likewise maximizes an expert likelihood along each path of a context tree. Their forecaster chooses a probability vector after receiving a context. Our forecast is a density matrix announced before the effect. The local dual normalization is operator completeness, and proper-state constraint reduction uses quantum unitary invariance. The use of a sum of maximal path likelihoods and finite-dimensional cone duality is antecedent methodology. NeurIPS paper

The maximum of summed likelihoods also appears in quantum maximal leakage. Farokhi’s Theorems 1–2 give the formula and a rank-one support bound for an unrestricted measurement on a finite quantum ensemble. Xiao, Zhao, Zhu and Farokhi apply this objective to tensor powers of a finite ensemble and identify the collective optimization with equal-prior state discrimination. The tree in (6) applies that likelihood objective to one-copy measurements with classical outcome conditioning and with the full density-state family as comparator. Equations (7)–(8) exploit the restricted measurement structure and recover proper forecasts. Section 6 compares this causal class with complete product measurements that need not form a causal tree. Krattenthaler and Slater analyze universal quantum coding through relative-entropy redundancy, and Hayashi treats universal approximation of multi-copy states in general fixed dimension. The objective in (1) instead involves a worst path of effects and logarithmic likelihood ratios after measurement. Bennett et al. give distinguishability gaps between local and separable operations for specified product-state ensembles. The separation here concerns the maximized likelihood of a common unknown state on three fresh copies. The lower bound already holds for Hermitian product certificates without global positivity. The low-rank tomography theorem of Nayak and Zhou applies to trace-norm estimation at a target accuracy under sample-block restrictions. It provides a different comparison with sequential observations and does not determine the pathwise likelihood value.
