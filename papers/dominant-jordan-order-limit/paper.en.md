abstract: |
  We distinguish the maximal Jordan order of a qubit first-passage kernel from the maximal order at its spectral radius. Positivity rules out order four in dimension two. A completely positive qubit map with a peripheral order-three block must be generated by a common triangular shear and at most one extra rank-one Kraus operator. Such a Kraus space is incompatible with a time-homogeneous monitored Lindblad first-passage kernel, even with arbitrary hidden jump channels. The maximal peripheral order is therefore two. A single counted jump attains it at any prescribed positive Laplace parameter, with a nonzero full-trace first-passage coefficient. The prior subdominant order-three construction makes the unrestricted order bound sharp.
---

## 1. Model and main statements

All maps act on complex \(2\times2\) matrices. A positive map sends positive semidefinite matrices to positive semidefinite matrices. Its restriction to Hermitian matrices is a real linear map of dimension four. We use the complexified spectrum and the usual Jordan block sizes. A spectral value is peripheral if its modulus equals the spectral radius.

Consider a finite-dimensional time-homogeneous monitored Lindblad process on a qubit. Write \(H=H^\dagger\) for its Hamiltonian, \(L_j\) for the counted jump operators, and \(U_\ell\) for the uncounted jump operators. A count is a jump of any counted type. Put
\[
\begin{split}
K&=-iH-\tfrac12\left(\sum_j L_j^\dagger L_j+
                               \sum_\ell U_\ell^\dagger U_\ell\right),\\
\mathcal L_0(X)&=KX+XK^\dagger+\sum_\ell U_\ell XU_\ell^\dagger,\qquad
\mathcal J(X)=\sum_j L_jXL_j^\dagger .
\end{split}
\tag{1}
\]
The one-count Laplace kernel at \(s>0\) is
\[
\mathcal R_s=\int_0^\infty e^{-st}\mathcal J e^{t\mathcal L_0}\,dt.
\tag{2}
\]
The no-count evolution is completely positive. For a positive input its trace is nonincreasing, because the trace derivative is minus the trace of the counted jump map. This gives convergence in (2). The ordinary Laplace transform of the \(n\)-th count time from initial \(\rho\), with zero contribution if no such count occurs, is \(\operatorname{Tr}\mathcal R_s^n(\rho)\) by iteration of the one-count kernel [1,2].

**Theorem 1 (positive and completely positive qubit maps).** A positive qubit map has Jordan blocks of size at most three. If it is nilpotent, the index is at most two. If a completely positive qubit map \(T\) has a size-three block at a nonzero peripheral value, that value is \(\lambda=\rho(T)>0\). In some orthonormal basis, with \(E=|0\rangle\langle1|\), the map has exactly the form
\[
T(X)=(\sqrt{\lambda} I+aE)X(\sqrt{\lambda} I+aE)^\dagger
                +\beta EXE^\dagger,
\quad a\ne0,\quad\beta\ge0.
\tag{3}
\]
Conversely every map (3) has this block.

**Theorem 2 (monitored kernels).** For every \(s>0\), the peripheral blocks of \(\mathcal R_s\) in (2) have size at most two. A process with one counted jump and no uncounted jumps achieves a size-two block at the positive spectral radius, at any prescribed \(s>0\). Its ordinary first-passage transform contains a nonzero \(n\lambda^{n-1}\) term.

The pinned qubit construction [1] has an ordinary-trace-visible size-three block strictly inside the spectral circle. Together with that construction, the sharp size bound anywhere in the spectrum is three. Theorem 2 determines the sharper bound governing the leading exponential terms.

## 2. The positive-map obstruction to size four

A real matrix of dimension four cannot have a block of size at least three at a nonreal eigenvalue, because the conjugate block has the same size. Suppose a nonzero real peripheral value with such a block is negative. There is only one block of that size. Normalize \(T^n\) by \(\rho(T)^n n^{p-1}\), where \(p\ge3\) is its size. The Jordan formula gives opposite nonzero limits along even and odd powers. Both limits must be positive maps. A map and its negative are both positive only when the map is zero, because the positive cone is pointed and spans the Hermitian space. Thus such a block can only occur at a positive peripheral value \(\lambda\).

For \(\lambda>0\) and a peripheral block of size \(p\ge3\), the normalized powers instead converge to a nonzero positive rank-one map \(D\). Indeed the unique large block supplies its highest nilpotent power of rank one, and every other block vanishes under this normalization. This highest nilpotent power gives \(D^2=0\), and \(TD=DT=\lambda D\). The positive cone is self-dual under the trace pairing. Since \(D\) is positive of rank one, it can therefore be written
\[
D(X)=U\operatorname{Tr}(VX),\qquad
U,V\ge0,\qquad U,V\ne0.
\tag{4}
\]
For example, \(D(I)\) is nonzero and positive, and the scalar functional in this range must be nonnegative on positive inputs. The identity \(D^2=0\) implies \(\operatorname{Tr}(UV)=0\). Two nonzero positive matrices with this property have orthogonal supports. In dimension two both are rank one. Thus we can choose an orthonormal basis with \(U\) proportional to \(P_0=|0\rangle\langle0|\) and \(V\) proportional to \(P_1=|1\rangle\langle1|\). The intertwining identities read
\[
T(P_0)=\lambda P_0,\qquad T^*(P_1)=\lambda P_1
\tag{5}
\]
in the trace duality.

If \(T\) were nilpotent with index \(p\ge3\), then \(D=T^{p-1}\) would be rank one and positive, with \(D^2=0\) and \(TD=DT=0\). The same construction gives (5) with \(\lambda=0\). For every positive \(X\), \(T(X)\) then has zero expectation against \(P_1\) and is supported on \(P_0\). Since \(T(P_0)=0\), this would make \(T^2=0\), a contradiction. The map \(X\mapsto EXE^\dagger\) for \(E=|0\rangle\langle1|\) attains index two.

It remains to exclude size four at positive \(\lambda\). In this case it is automatically peripheral and (5) holds. Write a Hermitian input as \(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}u&z\\\bar z&v\end{smallmatrix}\right)\), with \(z\) represented as a real two-vector and \(u,v\) real. Formula (5) gives the block form
\[
u'=\lambda u+\alpha\mathbin{\cdot}z+\beta v,\qquad
z'=Az+cv,\qquad
v'=\lambda v
\tag{6}
\]
for a real \(2\times2\) matrix \(A\). Test positivity on \(u=\tau^2|z_0|^2,\ z=\tau z_0,\ v=1\). Dividing the output determinant inequality \(u'v'\ge|z'|^2\) by \(\tau^2\) and sending \(\tau\to\infty\) shows \(|Az_0|\le\lambda|z_0|\) for all \(z_0\). Since all four eigenvalues equal \(\lambda\), \(A\) has trace \(2\lambda\). Its operator norm is at most \(\lambda\), so each real diagonal entry equals \(\lambda\) and the off-diagonal entries are zero. Consequently \(A=\lambda I\). The strictly upper block-triangular part of (6) then has cube zero. It cannot have a block of size four, proving the first claims of Theorem 1.

## 3. Completely positive normal form at order three

Suppose the peripheral block has size three and \(T\) is completely positive. As above it occurs at \(\lambda>0\), and we use the basis of (5). In any Kraus representation \(T(X)=\sum_j A_j X A_j^\dagger\), the identity \(T(P_0)=\lambda P_0\) forces each \(A_j\) to send \(|0\rangle\) into its span, by positivity of the summands. Write
\[
A_j=\begin{pmatrix}c_j&b_j\\0&d_j\end{pmatrix}.
\]
By (5), both diagonal coefficient vectors have squared norm \(\lambda\). The eigenvalue list of \(T\) is \(\lambda,\lambda,\zeta,\bar\zeta\), where \(\zeta=\sum_j c_j\bar d_j\) is the scalar multiplication on the coherence block in (6). For \(\lambda\) to have a size-three block, this list must have \(\zeta=\lambda\). In particular \(\sum_j|c_j-d_j|^2=0\). Every Kraus operator is therefore in
\[
\mathcal S=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb C}\{I,E\},
\qquad E=|0\rangle\langle1|.
\tag{7}
\]
Unitary mixing of the Kraus operators preserves the map. Rotate the diagonal coefficient vector into the first coordinate with value \(\sqrt\lambda\). The remaining operators are multiples of \(E\), so their combined squared coefficient is a single \(\beta\ge0\). This is (3), for some \(a\). Let \(N=T-\lambda I\), where \(I\) here denotes the identity map. Multiplication on the left by \(E\) and on the right by \(E^\dagger\) commute and square to zero. Expanding (3) gives
\[
N^2(X)=2\lambda |a|^2 EXE^\dagger,\qquad N^3=0.
\tag{8}
\]
Thus order three requires \(a\ne0\), and every such normal form attains it. Its Choi support, viewed as a subspace of vectorized matrices, lies in \(\mathcal S\).

## 4. Why monitored kernels cannot have the normal form

Use the expansion of \(e^{t\mathcal L_0}\) over the uncounted jumps. At fixed \(t\), the term with \(\ell\) uncounted jumps integrates over the nonnegative durations \(v_0,\ldots,v_\ell\) between events, of total duration \(t\). Its Kraus operators after a counted jump are
\[
L_j e^{Kv_\ell} U_{i_\ell} e^{Kv_{\ell-1}}
 \cdots U_{i_1} e^{Kv_0}.
\tag{9}
\]
The expansion follows from the usual variation-of-constants formula for the sum of \(X\mapsto KX+XK^\dagger\) and the bounded uncounted-jump map, by iteration. At every finite time the series converges in finite-dimensional norm. Each term in (2) then makes a positive contribution to the Choi matrix. After integrating in \(t\), the durations can be integrated directly on the nonnegative orthant with weight \(e^{-s\sum_\nu v_\nu}>0\). For any vector perpendicular to the Choi support, its squared inner product with the vectorization of (9) integrates to zero. Continuity forces that inner product to vanish for every strictly positive duration vector and hence also at its boundary. Consequently all operators (9) lie in the Choi support, including zero-duration limits.

Suppose the kernel has a peripheral block of size three. Its radius is positive by the nilpotent part of Theorem 1. In the basis of (3), every (9) lies in \(\mathcal S\). In particular all \(L_j\) lie there. Some \(L_j\) must be invertible: if every counted jump were a scalar multiple of \(E\), the range of \(\mathcal R_s\) would be contained in the one-dimensional span of \(P_0\), which cannot support a size-three block. The algebra \(\mathcal S\) is closed under inversion of its invertible elements since \(E^2=0\).

Fix an invertible counted \(L_j\). Differentiating \(L_j e^{Kt}\) at \(t=0\) gives \(L_jK\in\mathcal S\), so \(K\in\mathcal S\). A path with one uncounted jump gives \(L_jU_i\in\mathcal S\) at zero durations, so every \(U_i\in\mathcal S\). All jumps now have the form \(cI+dE\). For such a jump, the lower diagonal entry of its squared norm operator \((cI+dE)^\dagger(cI+dE)\) exceeds the upper entry by \(|d|^2\). On the other hand, \(K\) has equal diagonal entries because \(K\in\mathcal S\). By the Hermitian part of (1),
\[
K+K^\dagger=-\sum_j L_j^\dagger L_j-\sum_i U_i^\dagger U_i .
\]
Its diagonal difference therefore forces every shear coefficient \(d\) to be zero. All jumps are scalar. The Hermitian part of \(K\) is then scalar. Since a strict upper triangular shear in \(K\in\mathcal S\) has a nonzero off-diagonal Hermitian part unless its coefficient vanishes, \(K\) is scalar, and so is \(H\). Both \(\mathcal J\) and \(\mathcal L_0\) act as scalar multiples of the identity map. The same holds for \(\mathcal R_s\), contradicting order three. This proves the universal peripheral bound, including in the presence of uncounted jumps.

## 5. A sharp one-jump example

Take \(s=1\), no uncounted jumps, and
\[
L=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&\sqrt2\end{pmatrix},\qquad
H=\begin{pmatrix}0&-i/2\\i/2&0\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{10}
\]
Then \(K=-iH-L^\dagger L/2\) has diagonal entries \(-1/2,-3/2\) and upper-right entry \(-1\), with lower-left zero. Solving its triangular exponential gives
\[
L e^{Kt}=\begin{pmatrix}
x&-x+2y\\0&\sqrt2 y
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad x=e^{-t/2},\qquad y=e^{-3t/2}.
\tag{11}
\]
Using (2), in real coordinates \((u,\operatorname{Re}z,\operatorname{Im}z,v)\) the matrix of the kernel is
\[
\begin{pmatrix}
\lambda & 1/3 & 0 & 1/6\\
0 & \delta & 0 & \sqrt2/6\\
0 & 0 & \delta & 0\\
0 & 0 & 0 & \lambda
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad \lambda=\tfrac12,\quad \delta=\tfrac{\sqrt2}{3}<\lambda .
\tag{12}
\]
For example the entries are obtained by integrating \(e^{-t}x^2,\ e^{-t}xy,\ e^{-t}y^2\), with respective values \(1/2,1/3,1/4\). The two algebraic eigenvalues at \(\lambda\) have a nonzero effective off-diagonal coefficient
\[
b_*=\tfrac16+\frac{(1/3)(\sqrt2/6)}{\lambda-\delta}>0 .
\tag{13}
\]
Equivalently, start at \(P_1\) and iterate the triangular matrix. The \(v\) component equals \(\lambda^n\); the real coherence equals \((\sqrt2/6)(\lambda^n-\delta^n)/(\lambda-\delta)\). Inserting this into the \(u\) recursion gives the ordinary transform
\[
\operatorname{Tr}(\mathcal R_1^n(P_1))
=\lambda^n+b_* n\lambda^{n-1}
-\frac{(1/3)(\sqrt2/6)}{(\lambda-\delta)^2}
                      \bigl(\lambda^n-\delta^n\bigr),
\qquad n\ge1.
\tag{14}
\]
The nonzero polynomial coefficient proves a visible size-two block at the positive peripheral value. At a prescribed \(s=s_*>0\), replace \(H\) by \(s_*H\) and \(L\) by \(\sqrt{s_*}L\). Changing variables \(t\mapsto s_*t\) in (2) gives the same kernel at that Laplace parameter.

## 6. Relation to the fixed source

The pinned result [1] constructs a subdominant size-three spectral block in a monitored qubit and shows that the complete final trace retains its quadratic term. The proof here gives a sharp global upper limit, a normal form for peripheral order three in abstract completely positive maps, and a stricter microscopic limit for time-homogeneous Lindblad first-passage kernels. The last obstruction uses the common Kraus support across every counted and uncounted path and the tied diagonal damping in the Lindblad generator. This analysis treats fixed conditioned dynamics between counts, as in (1). A general completely positive iteration need not come from such a kernel.
