Full-support unbounded service times with a quantitative feedback capacity gap

Research manuscript, 26 November 2026

Abstract

We construct a discrete-time first-in-first-out timing queue whose service law has full support on the positive integers and an exponential tail, but whose full-feedback capacity strictly exceeds its weak-feedback and no-feedback capacities. Let S=1+2G+BS=1+2G+B, where Pr(G=k)=14(34)k\Pr(G=k)=\frac14(\frac34)^k and BB is an independent Bernoulli variable with parameter 1/161/16. Writing H0=log(256/27)+h(1/16)H_0=\log(256/27)+h(1/16), we prove

CF=log(1+e−H0),C≤CWF≤CF−140000nats per slot. C^F=\log(1+e^{-H_0}),\qquad C\le C^{WF}\le C^F-\frac1{40000} \quad\text{nats per slot}. \tag{1}

All three capacities in (1) are optimized over their admissible output rates. A probability-generating-function construction attains the full-feedback upper bound. For the converse, every weak-feedback residual output obeys a linear inequality involving only its first three probabilities, whereas the corresponding maximum-entropy geometric output violates it. Relative entropy converts this separation into a quantitative loss, and strict concavity controls output rates away from the optimum. The same positive gap holds on an explicitly specified open set of service-law parameters. We also characterize exactly the residual waiting distributions for the lattice family S=1+dGqS=1+dG_q. The entropy separation supplies a counterexample to Theorem 3, equation (7), of the inspected author version arXiv:2309.14145v2. The argument concerns that stated unbounded-support assertion, not the paper’s bounded-support results. The exact weak-feedback capacity and a classification of general service laws remain open.

1. Model, notation, and established capacity interface

Time, arrival instants, and service durations are integer valued. The queue has one server, follows first-in-first-out order, and is initially empty. Packet service times are independent and identically distributed with law SS on ℕ+={1,2,…}\mathbb N_+=\{1,2,\ldots\}. Put m=𝔼S<∞m=\mathbb ES\lt \infty, μ=1/m\mu=1/m, and assume H(S)<∞H(S)\lt \infty. All logarithms are natural. A rate is measured in nats per unit time slot, rather than per packet.

Full feedback reveals departure times causally. Weak feedback reveals the times at which packets enter service. No feedback reveals neither. We use the expected-duration timing-code convention of [1,2]: a code sends nn packets, its final departure has expected time at most TnT_n, and an asymptotically achievable communication rate is the limiting message log-cardinality divided by TnT_n, with error probability tending to zero. At fixed output rate λ\lambda, the expected final departure is bounded by n/λn/\lambda, and the asymptotic message rate is λlogMn/n\lambda\log M_n/n. Write C(λ),CWF(λ),CF(λ)C(\lambda),C^{WF}(\lambda),C^F(\lambda) for these rate-constrained capacities and C,CWF,CFC,C^{WF},C^F for their suprema over 0<λ<μ0\lt \lambda\lt \mu.

The following established interface is used with precisely these conventions. It is the full-feedback entropy characterization and weak-feedback converse of [1,2], also restated in [3, Propositions 2 and 3]. It is not a new coding theorem of this manuscript.

9. Relation to the inspected literature and limitations

The timing-channel model and capacity interface (3) come from [1,2]. Theorem 3, equation (7), in the inspected arXiv version of [3] asserts equality of the residual and unrestricted entropy suprema for every unbounded service law, at every admissible rate, including its weak-feedback case τ=0\tau=0. Equations (22) and (29), together with full-feedback attainment in Theorem 9, contradict that assertion within its discrete-time model. The contradiction uses finite means, independent service copies, the original upper mean-cost constraint, and full positive-integer support. It does not depend on a failure of an optimizer to exist.

There is also a separately checkable issue in the discrete proof in [3, Section IV-F]: lower triangularity with diagonal a>0a>0 does not force the smallest singular value to equal aa. For b>0b>0,

σmin(a0ba)=b2+4a2−b2<a. \sigma_{\min}\begin{pmatrix}a&0\\b&a\end{pmatrix} =\frac{\sqrt{b^2+4a^2}-b}{2}\lt a. \tag{47}

To verify (47), diagonalize the positive matrix M𝖳MM^\mathsf TM; its smaller eigenvalue is the square of the expression displayed. The strict inequality is equivalent to b2+4a2<b+2a\sqrt{b^2+4a^2}\lt b+2a. This algebraic observation identifies a faulty implication but, on its own, would not disprove a theorem. The probability and entropy certificates above provide the independent counterexample.

The bounded-support results of [3] are outside the counterexample. The general conjecture concerning nonmemoryless service laws is not settled here. We do not determine CWFC^{WF} or CC exactly, optimize the gap constant, treat continuous service times, or characterize every parameter outside (35). The sharp statement (42) concerns the specified lattice family and its stated geometric-output domain, not general service distributions.
