Exact phase realization and strong stability for matrix-valued distributed delays

Research manuscript, 26 November 2026. This manuscript has not undergone external peer review. Formal verification is not included.

Abstract

We study finite-dimensional linear functional equations whose delay coefficients are arbitrary finite matrix-valued measures. We prove the generalized Hale–Silkowski criterion for robustness under Borel changes of the delay variable, both in the full well-posed class and in the subclass with spectrum-determined growth. The missing critical case is resolved by constructing, in every uniform neighborhood of the identity, a delay map whose pushed-forward equation has spectral abscissa and solution growth bound both equal to zero. Two ingredients make this construction possible. First, the largest matrix spectral radius over measurable disk-valued multipliers is attained by a unit-modulus multiplier, including for measures with both atoms and a nonatomic part. Second, the spectral radius in the matrix convolution-measure algebra is bounded by this measurable phase radius. The latter estimate controls all algebra characters and yields a solution-growth bound without assuming that characteristic roots determine growth. It also gives the sharp common bound ω ≤ log r for every Borel pushforward of a kernel with phase radius 0<r<10\mathrel{<}r\mathrel{<}1 on the normalized delay interval. The results establish Conjectures 3.18 and 3.19 in the accessible October 2026 monograph of Mazanti and Conjecture 4.6 of Chitour, Gonçalves Netto and Mazanti. The claims concern their Borel perturbation model; no claim is made for time-varying delay maps or for a common transient prefactor.

1. Problem, prior results, and contribution

Consider the real equation

x(t)=∫[−1,0]μ(dθ)x(t+θ),t≥0, x(t)=\int_{[-1,0]}\mu(d\theta)x(t+\theta),\qquad t\geq0, \tag{1.1}

where d≥1d\geq1 is finite and μ\mu is a d×dd\times d matrix of finite real Borel measures. A perturbation replaces θ\theta by a Borel map ϕ:[−1,0]→[−1,0]\phi:[-1,0]\to[-1,0]. Equivalently, it replaces μ\mu by its pushforward ϕ*μ\phi_*\mu.

For finitely many point delays, the Hale–Silkowski criterion characterizes strong exponential stability by a strict phase-radius inequality. Chitour, Gonçalves Netto and Mazanti [CGM] extend the perturbation model to distributed matrix measures. Their Conjecture 4.6 asks for the same equivalence within the spectrum-determined-growth class. Their Theorem 4.9 proves the version with a common negative growth rate; their Lemma 4.16 already excludes phase radius greater than one. The unclosed case is equality to one: a sequence of almost unstable perturbations does not by itself furnish one nonstable perturbation.

Mazanti’s later monograph [M26, Conjectures 3.18 and 3.19, pp. 67–68] separately states the full well-posed and spectrum-determined-growth versions. Section 7.8, pp. 216–217, retains both as open problems and identifies the missing direct connection between the phase radius and solution growth. We address that connection using a convolution-measure algebra, then realize the critical phase exactly.

The contribution is the complete implication at the critical boundary and the resulting full well-posed-class criterion. The convexity/purification tools, Banach-algebra character theory, spectral-radius formula, and renewal representation used in the proof are classical; we do not claim them as inventions. The supercritical obstruction is imported with explicit attribution from [CGM]. The new argument combines these tools to construct a single admissible critical perturbation and prove its growth bound even when its measure is singular continuous.

The finite-delay stability and robust coefficient-uncertainty results of Song, Michiels, Zhou and Duan [SMZD] are a technically comparable control-theory benchmark. Our result treats a different missing issue: arbitrary finite distributed matrix measures and Borel delay perturbations. It does not improve their LMI algorithms or their structured coefficient-uncertainty analysis. Braun, Auriol and Brivadis [BAB] study exponential stability across state spaces for point-delay plus L1L^1 kernels; their renewal methods are relevant background, while their stated kernel class excludes singular-continuous measures.

2. Definitions and main results

All scalar-measure algebras below are complex, even when a delay equation has real coefficients. Matrix multiplication retains its usual order. Measures are integrated entrywise. Put

∥K∥row=max1≤i≤d∑j=1d∥Kij∥TV, \|K\|_{\mathrm{row}}=\max_{1\leq i\leq d}\sum_{j=1}^d\|K_{ij}\|_{\mathrm{TV}},

and use the coordinate maximum norm for vectors. This matrix-measure norm is submultiplicative under convolution. Let ν=∑i,j|μij|\nu=\sum_{i,j}|\mu_{ij}| be a common finite dominating measure. Statements about measurable multipliers may initially be made ν\nu-almost everywhere; a Borel representative, modified on a null set, is always chosen when defining a delay map.

Definition 2.1 (well-posed and spectral-growth classes). Write μ∈𝒲\mu\in\mathcal W when I−μ({0})I-\mu(\{0\}) is invertible. An admissible history is h∈C([−1,0],ℝd)h\in C([-1,0],\mathbb R^d) satisfying

h(0)=∫[−1,0]μ(dθ)h(θ). h(0)=\int_{[-1,0]}\mu(d\theta)h(\theta).

For its continuous solution let xt(θ)=x(t+θ)x_t(\theta)=x(t+\theta). Define the growth bound

ωμ=inf{a∈ℝ:∃Ca<∞,∥xt∥∞≤Caeat∥h∥∞for every admissible h and every t≥0}. \omega_\mu=\inf\{a\in\mathbb R:\exists C_a\mathrel{<}\infty,\ \|x_t\|_\infty\leq C_a e^{at}\|h\|_\infty\ \text{for every admissible }h\text{ and every }t\geq0\}. \tag{2.1}

The infimum may be −∞-\infty. The characteristic determinant and spectral abscissa are

Δμ(z)=det(I−∫ezθμ(dθ)),Sμ=sup{Rez:Δμ(z)=0}, \Delta_\mu(z)=\det\left(I-\int e^{z\theta}\mu(d\theta)\right),\qquad S_\mu=\sup\{\operatorname{Re}z:\Delta_\mu(z)=0\}, \tag{2.2}

where the supremum of an empty set is −∞-\infty. Set 𝒢={μ∈𝒲:Sμ=ωμ}\mathcal G=\{\mu\in\mathcal W:S_\mu=\omega_\mu\}. This is the class denoted 𝒢\mathcal G in [CGM] and SDG in [M26]; 𝒲\mathcal W is WP in [M26]. Exponential stability means ωμ<0\omega_\mu\mathrel{<}0.

Definition 2.2 (strong stability). For 𝒦=𝒲\mathcal K=\mathcal W or 𝒢\mathcal G and μ∈𝒦\mu\in\mathcal K, local 𝒦\mathcal K-strong stability means that some ε>0\varepsilon>0 has the following property: every Borel ϕ:[−1,0]→[−1,0]\phi:[-1,0]\to[-1,0] with ∥ϕ−id∥∞<ε\|\phi-\mathrm{id}\|_\infty\mathrel{<}\varepsilon and ϕ*μ∈𝒦\phi_*\mu\in\mathcal K gives an exponentially stable equation. Global 𝒦\mathcal K-strong stability tests all Borel ϕ\phi with ϕ*μ∈𝒦\phi_*\mu\in\mathcal K. The uniform-rate versions require one α>0\alpha>0 with ωϕ*μ≤−α\omega_{\phi_*\mu}\leq-\alpha over the corresponding family. They do not require one common constant CC in (2.1).

Definition 2.3 (measurable phase radius). For a finite matrix measure on a compact interval, put

R(μ)=supu Borel,|u|=1ρ(∫udμ), R(\mu)=\sup_{u\text{ Borel},\ |u|=1}\rho\left(\int u\,d\mu\right), \tag{2.3}

where ρ\rho is the ordinary finite-matrix spectral radius. This is ρHS\rho_{\mathrm{HS}} in [CGM, M26], since every Borel unit-modulus function has a Borel argument. Theorem 3.4 below shows that the supremum is a maximum and equals the maximum over |u|≤1|u|\leq1.

Theorem 2.4 (exact critical witness). Let μ\mu be any finite real matrix measure on [−1,0][-1,0] with R(μ)=1R(\mu)=1. For every ε>0\varepsilon>0, there exist a Borel map ϕ:[−1,0]→[−1,0]\phi:[-1,0]\to[-1,0], a number δ>0\delta>0, a frequency b>0b>0, and v∈ℂd\{0}v\in\mathbb C^d\setminus\{0\} such that

∥ϕ−id∥∞<ε,ϕ([−1,0])⊂[−1+δ,−δ],(∫eibθd(ϕ*μ)(θ))v=v. \|\phi-\mathrm{id}\|_\infty\mathrel{<}\varepsilon,\qquad \phi([-1,0])\subset[-1+\delta,-\delta],\qquad \left(\int e^{ib\theta}\,d(\phi_*\mu)(\theta)\right)v=v. \tag{2.4}

Moreover ϕ*μ∈𝒢\phi_*\mu\in\mathcal G and

Sϕ*μ=ωϕ*μ=0. S_{\phi_*\mu}=\omega_{\phi_*\mu}=0. \tag{2.5}

No well-posedness assumption on the original μ\mu is needed for this witness theorem. The witness is well posed because its support stays away from zero.

Theorem 2.5 (complete strong-stability criteria). Let μ∈𝒲\mu\in\mathcal W. The following are equivalent:

  1. R(μ)<1R(\mu)\mathrel{<}1.
  2. The equation is globally 𝒲\mathcal W-strongly stable.
  3. The equation is locally 𝒲\mathcal W-strongly stable.
  4. The equation is globally 𝒲\mathcal W-strongly stable with a uniform rate.
  5. The equation is locally 𝒲\mathcal W-strongly stable with a uniform rate.

If μ∈𝒢\mu\in\mathcal G, the same five-way equivalence holds with 𝒢\mathcal G in place of 𝒲\mathcal W. Thus the conclusions apply with the exact perturbation qualifications of [CGM, Conjecture 4.6] and [M26, Conjectures 3.18 and 3.19].

Theorem 2.6 (strict-radius growth bound). If r=R(μ)<1r=R(\mu)\mathrel{<}1, every Borel pushforward ϕ*μ\phi_*\mu is well posed and exponentially stable, without an additional spectral-growth hypothesis. If 0<r<10\mathrel{<}r\mathrel{<}1, then

ωϕ*μ≤logr. \omega_{\phi_*\mu}\leq\log r. \tag{2.6}

If r=0r=0, the growth bound is −∞-\infty. For an interval [−H,0][-H,0] with H>0H>0, the bound is (logr)/H(\log r)/H. The bound is sharp as a common bound depending only on rr and HH.

The proof order is phase attainment, the convolution spectral bound, the renewal estimate, and finally exact realization. The strict and supercritical sides are distinguished from the new equality case throughout.

3. Attainment by a pure measurable phase

Lemma 3.1 (compact disk moment set). Let μ\mu be a finite complex matrix measure on a compact interval. The set

𝒟μ={∫ηdμ:η Borel,|η|≤1} \mathcal D_\mu=\left\{\int\eta\,d\mu:\eta\text{ Borel},\ |\eta|\leq1\right\}

is compact. In particular, ρ\rho has a maximum on 𝒟μ\mathcal D_\mu.

Proof. Write dμij=fijdνd\mu_{ij}=f_{ij}\,d\nu for ν=∑i,j|μij|\nu=\sum_{i,j}|\mu_{ij}|. The closed unit ball of L∞(ν)L^\infty(\nu) is compact in its weak-star topology. Each coordinate η↦∫ηfijdν\eta\mapsto\int\eta f_{ij}\,d\nu is weak-star continuous because fij∈L1(ν)f_{ij}\in L^1(\nu). The image of the ball in the finite-dimensional matrix space is therefore compact. Borel representatives do not change this image. Continuity of the finite-matrix spectral radius proves the assertion. The zero-measure case gives the singleton {0}\{0\}. ▫\square

Lemma 3.2 (nonatomic phase purification). Let ν\nu be finite and nonatomic and F∈L1(ν;ℂm)F\in L^1(\nu;\mathbb C^m). For every measurable η\eta with |η|≤1|\eta|\leq1, there is a measurable uu with |u|=1|u|=1 such that

∫uFdν=∫ηFdν. \int uF\,d\nu=\int\eta F\,d\nu. \tag{3.1}

Proof. Put r=|η|r=|\eta| and z=η/rz=\eta/r where r>0r>0, and z=1z=1 where r=0r=0. Set

u+=z(r+i1−r2),u−=z(r−i1−r2). u_+=z\bigl(r+i\sqrt{1-r^2}\bigr),\qquad u_-=z\bigl(r-i\sqrt{1-r^2}\bigr).

Both have modulus one and (u++u−)/2=η(u_++u_-)/2=\eta. The finite-dimensional real vector measure

V(E)=∫E(u+−u−)Fdν V(E)=\int_E (u_+-u_-)F\,d\nu

is nonatomic. By the compact convex range theorem for nonatomic vector measures, some measurable EE satisfies V(E)=V(X)/2V(E)=V(X)/2. Define u=u+u=u_+ on EE and u=u−u=u_- off EE. Then (3.1) follows by subtraction.

For completeness, the half-range fact needed here follows from the usual extreme-point proof of the vector-measure theorem. The weak-star compact nonempty convex set of functions 0≤q≤10\leq q\leq1 with ∫qGdν=(∫Gdν)/2\int qG\,d\nu=(\int G\,d\nu)/2, for G∈L1(ν;ℝk)G\in L^1(\nu;\mathbb R^k), has an extreme point. If it lies strictly between zero and one on a positive-measure set, some smaller set has a uniform margin from both endpoints. Split that set into k+1k+1 disjoint sets of positive measure. A nontrivial linear dependence among their GG-integrals produces a nonzero bounded perturbation preserving all kk moments; a sufficiently small multiple can be both added and subtracted while retaining 0≤q≤10\leq q\leq1. This contradicts extremality. Thus an extreme point is an indicator and gives the required set. The standard tools behind this argument, and purification more generally, are credited to the vector-measure literature [DWW, Balder]. ▫\square

Lemma 3.3 (finite matrix maximum principle). If A(z)A(z) is a finite-matrix holomorphic function on a neighborhood of the closed unit disk, then

ρ(A(z0))≤max|z|=1ρ(A(z)),|z0|≤1. \rho(A(z_0))\leq\max_{|z|=1}\rho(A(z)),\qquad |z_0|\leq1. \tag{3.2}

Proof. Let the boundary maximum be qq and fix r>qr>q. The resolvents (λI−A(z))−1(\lambda I-A(z))^{-1} are uniformly bounded on the compact set |λ|=r|\lambda|=r, |z|=1|z|=1. The matrix Cauchy formula gives a constant CrC_r with ∥A(z)n∥≤Crrn+1\|A(z)^n\|\leq C_r r^{n+1} on |z|=1|z|=1, for every integer n≥0n\geq0. For |z0|<1|z_0|\mathrel{<}1, apply the scalar-entry Cauchy formula to the holomorphic matrix A(z)nA(z)^n to obtain

∥A(z0)n∥≤Crrn+11−|z0|. \|A(z_0)^n\|\leq\frac{C_r r^{n+1}}{1-|z_0|}.

The finite-matrix spectral-radius formula, followed by r↓qr\downarrow q, proves (3.2). Boundary points require no argument. This is a maximum principle, not a convexity assertion about spectral radius. ▫\square

Theorem 3.4 (pure-phase maximum). For every finite matrix measure μ\mu on a compact interval,

max|η|≤1ρ(∫ηdμ)=max|u|=1ρ(∫udμ)=R(μ). \max_{|\eta|\leq1}\rho\left(\int\eta\,d\mu\right) =\max_{|u|=1}\rho\left(\int u\,d\mu\right)=R(\mu). \tag{3.3}

Proof. Choose a disk-valued maximizer η0\eta_0 by Lemma 3.1, and denote its value by rr. Decompose the dominating measure into its nonatomic part and its at most countably many point atoms tjt_j. Put Aj=μ({tj})A_j=\mu(\{t_j\}). The series ∑j∥Aj∥\sum_j\|A_j\| converges. Lemma 3.2, applied to the vector of all matrix-entry densities on the nonatomic part, replaces η0\eta_0 there by a unit phase without changing that part of the matrix integral. Call its fixed integral CC.

Keep all but the first nn atomic coordinates equal to their original values. Apply Lemma 3.3 successively to these first nn disk coordinates of

C+∑jzjAj. C+\sum_j z_j A_j.

Each coordinate can be moved to the unit circle without decreasing the spectral radius. No value greater than rr is possible by global maximality, so after these changes the value remains exactly rr.

Choose a coordinatewise convergent subsequence of the resulting countable sequences. For every fixed jj, its limiting coordinate has modulus one, since all sufficiently late terms place that coordinate on the circle. Absolute summability of AjA_j makes the matrix sums converge in norm: first fix a finite head, then bound the common tail by 2∑j>N∥Aj∥2\sum_{j>N}\|A_j\|. Spectral-radius continuity preserves the value rr. Combining these limiting atomic phases with the purified nonatomic phase gives the required Borel unit phase. The finite-atom and atom-free cases are the corresponding simpler cases. ▫\square

Proof. For c>0c>0, the product of a unit phase and w/cw/c is disk-valued, so use (3.3); c=0c=0 is immediate. For a pushforward, pull back each phase by ϕ\phi. ▫\square

4. A spectral bound in the convolution-measure algebra

Let 𝒜=ℳ([0,∞))\mathcal A=\mathcal M([0,\infty)) be the complex finite regular Borel measures on the additive half-line, with total-variation norm, convolution, and identity δ0\delta_0. This is a commutative unital complex Banach algebra. A finite matrix measure KK is an element of Md(𝒜)M_d(\mathcal A), equipped, for example, with the row-sum norm defined in Section 2.

Theorem 4.1 (convolution spectral domination). If KK is a finite matrix measure of compact support on [0,∞)[0,\infty), then

rMd(𝒜)(K)≤R(K). r_{M_d(\mathcal A)}(K)\leq R(K). \tag{4.1}

Proof. Fix any character χ\chi of 𝒜\mathcal A. It is a norm-one complex linear functional with χ(δ0)=1\chi(\delta_0)=1. Set ν=∑i,j|Kij|\nu=\sum_{i,j}|K_{ij}| and Kij=fijνK_{ij}=f_{ij}\nu. The map

J:L1(ν)→𝒜,J(h)=hν, J:L^1(\nu)\longrightarrow\mathcal A,\qquad J(h)=h\nu,

is a linear isometry. Thus χ∘J\chi\circ J is a bounded functional of norm at most one on L1(ν)L^1(\nu). By L1L^1–L∞L^\infty duality there is a single η∈L∞(ν)\eta\in L^\infty(\nu), |η|≤1|\eta|\leq1, for which

χ(hν)=∫hηdν(h∈L1(ν)). \chi(h\nu)=\int h\eta\,d\nu\qquad(h\in L^1(\nu)).

In particular, for every matrix entry at once,

χ(K)=∫ηdK,ρ(χ(K))≤R(K). \chi(K)=\int\eta\,dK, \qquad \rho(\chi(K))\leq R(K). \tag{4.2}

The same η\eta represents all entries; independent entrywise multipliers are not being introduced.

For every λ∈ℂ\lambda\in\mathbb C, the matrix λI−K\lambda I-K is invertible over the commutative algebra 𝒜\mathcal A if and only if its determinant is invertible in 𝒜\mathcal A. This follows from determinant multiplicativity and the adjugate formula. The character criterion for invertibility in a commutative unital Banach algebra then gives

σMd(𝒜)(K)=⋃χσℂ(χ(K)). \sigma_{M_d(\mathcal A)}(K)=\bigcup_{\chi}\sigma_{\mathbb C}(\chi(K)). \tag{4.3}

Equations (4.2)–(4.3) prove (4.1). The zero-measure case is immediate. ▫\square

The proof uses all characters of the measure algebra. It does not identify them with Laplace-transform evaluations, and therefore does not require a spectral-naturalness assumption or discard possible Wiener–Pitt phenomena. The Banach-algebra facts in this proof are standard [Rudin]; their use here supplies the required link between the phase criterion and a norm-convergent renewal resolvent.

5. From a convolution inverse to solution growth

Reflect the delay variable and write K=(−id)*μK=(-\mathrm{id})_*\mu, so KK is supported in [0,1][0,1]. Reflection is a pushforward; it is not multiplication of the measure by a minus sign. Equation (1.1) becomes

x(t)=∫[0,1]K(ds)x(t−s). x(t)=\int_{[0,1]}K(ds)x(t-s). \tag{5.1}

Lemma 5.1 (continuous well-posedness). If I−K({0})I-K(\{0\}) is invertible, every continuous compatible history has a unique continuous global solution of (5.1), bounded on every finite time interval.

Proof. Separate the zero atom and set B=(I−K({0}))−1B=(I-K(\{0\}))^{-1}. The total variation of KK on (0,a](0,a] tends to zero as a↓0a\downarrow0. Choose a step a>0a>0 for which the row-norm contraction factor of BK|(0,a]B K|_{(0,a]} is less than one. On the first interval [0,a][0,a], extend candidate continuous functions by the fixed history. The map y(t)↦B∫(0,1]K(ds)y(t−s)y(t)\mapsto B\int_{(0,1]}K(ds)y(t-s) preserves the history value at zero by compatibility. It maps continuous glued functions to continuous functions, by dominated convergence, and differences between candidates involve only delays s≤t≤as\leq t\leq a. It is therefore a contraction. The same argument on successive intervals of length aa, using the previously constructed trajectory as history, gives existence and uniqueness on every finite interval. The pieces agree at their endpoints by the equation. Their union is the continuous global solution. ▫\square

Lemma 5.2 (weighted renewal estimate). Define Ka(ds)=e−asK(ds)K_a(ds)=e^{-as}K(ds) for a∈ℝa\in\mathbb R. If KK is well posed and

Proof. Let x+(t)=x(t)x_+(t)=x(t) for t≥0t\geq0 and zero for t<0t\mathrel{<}0. For t≥0t\geq0 put

The strict lower endpoint matters: the term at s=ts=t belongs only to K*x+K*x_+, with value K({t})x(0)K(\{t\})x(0). For all t≥0t\geq0 we have the exact identity

Here fhf_h vanishes for t≥1t\geq1. It need not be continuous when KK has atoms. With ya(t)=e−atx+(t)y_a(t)=e^{-at}x_+(t) and ga(t)=e−atfh(t)g_a(t)=e^{-at}f_h(t), (5.4) becomes

7. Completion of the two conjectures

We record the already-established supercritical fact in the exact form used.

Proposition 7.1 (known supercritical obstruction [CGM, Lemma 4.16]). If R(μ)>1R(\mu)>1, then every uniform neighborhood of the identity contains a Borel delay map ϕ\phi whose pushforward is a finite point-delay measure in 𝒢\mathcal G and has a characteristic root in the open right half-plane.

Justification and attribution. Lemma 4.16 of [CGM] constructs, for every ε,η>0\varepsilon,\eta>0 when R(μ)≥1R(\mu)\geq1, a finite-range ϕ\phi within ε\varepsilon of the identity whose characteristic determinant has a root with real part at least logR(μ)−η\log R(\mu)-\eta. When R(μ)>1R(\mu)>1, take η<logR(μ)\eta\mathrel{<}\log R(\mu). The proof approximates a disk multiplier by a finite simple function on small-diameter Borel cells and assigns distinct rationally independent delays to those cells. Finite-dimensional phase maximum and the classical finite-delay Hale–Silkowski theorem supply the unstable finite-delay equation. This is prior work. We use neither this approximation nor a limiting argument to claim the new equality case. [M26, Lemma 3.30 and Corollary 3.31] provide the corresponding later formulation.

Proof of Theorem 2.5. Theorem 2.6 shows that R(μ)<1R(\mu)\mathrel{<}1 implies global 𝒲\mathcal W-strong stability with a uniform negative rate; choose, for example, any 0<β<−logR(μ)0\mathrel{<}\beta\mathrel{<}-\log R(\mu) if the radius is positive, and any β>0\beta>0 if it is zero. This property implies each of the weaker global or local properties in the statement.

Conversely, suppose local 𝒲\mathcal W-strong stability holds. A radius greater than one contradicts Proposition 7.1, since the constructed finite-delay witness belongs to 𝒢⊂𝒲\mathcal G\subset\mathcal W. Radius equal to one contradicts Theorem 2.4. Thus the radius is strictly less than one. This proves the five-way equivalence in 𝒲\mathcal W.

Now suppose the original measure lies in 𝒢\mathcal G. The strict-radius direction still follows from Theorem 2.6, even after restricting to pushforwards in 𝒢\mathcal G. Both the critical witness in Theorem 2.4 and the supercritical witness in Proposition 7.1 belong to 𝒢\mathcal G. They therefore also disprove local 𝒢\mathcal G-strong stability whenever the radius is at least one. This proves the second five-way equivalence. ▫\square

The theorem does not say that all singular-continuous kernels have spectrum-determined growth. It proves enough growth control to certify the specifically constructed critical witness, and gives strict-radius stability directly without invoking that property.

8.1. A stable absolutely continuous kernel on the critical robustness boundary

Let J=(0−110)J=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix} and U(a)=exp(aJ)U(a)=\exp(aJ). On positive delays define

Proof. The Euclidean operator norm of U(s2)U(s^2) is one and the scalar weight integrates to one, so every disk-multiplied matrix integral has spectral radius at most one. 9. Scope and remaining questions

The coefficient dimension is finite. The proof uses a common finite dominating measure and a finite-dimensional moment space for purification. We do not extend the conclusion to operator-valued measures on an infinite-dimensional state space.

The perturbation ϕ\phi is Borel and fixed in time, exactly as in [CGM, M26]. The exact lift may be discontinuous and noninjective. Stability restricted to continuous, monotone, differentiable, or bounded-slew delay maps is a different problem and is not characterized here. Time-varying delays and nonlinear equations are also outside the result.

The common rate in Theorem 2.6 is a bound on the infimum in (2.1). Constants for exponents strictly above that infimum may depend on the kernel, the perturbation, and the chosen exponent. A common transient prefactor is not asserted. The existence proof for a maximizing phase uses compactness and nonatomic purification; it is not an efficient algorithm for computing R(μ)R(\mu) from a general measure representation.

All core statements have written proofs. The results have not undergone external peer review or proof-assistant verification.
